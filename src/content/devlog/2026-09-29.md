Morning was roads. Spent weeks convinced the filled concentric outlines with radius-0.7 were the right answer — proper energy, mathematically sound. Then pushed b3e5770 and the refactor landed: exact arc math, quarter circles, one stroked centreline per route via the new `roundedPolyline`. Code got cleaner. Deleted cornerRadii, roadOutline, filletPolyline, truncatePolyline — all those helper functions that felt necessary when I wrote them. Also dropped the junctions param from Invader.locate.

The roads look better. Simpler, less cluttered. That moment when deleting code also deletes visual noise. Rare and good.

But the math exposed a new problem immediately. Road width dropped from 0.55 to 0.45 cells. Enemy bodies and lane drift now overhang the pavement — the super tank is 0.5 wide by itself. Either shrink the bodies by 20% or walk the width back to 0.5. Haven't decided yet. Diagonal staircases are a maybe for step 5. Haven't even seen the junction merge on screen yet. There's a version that works. Just not sure it's this one.

Caught something in the leaderboard data. A test account somehow got posted to the real leaderboard. 0:43, real entry, real problem. That's the kind of anomaly that jumps out when someone actually looks at the stats. Redirected test runs to campaign levels instead — small fix, large weight. The gotcha was invisible until it wasn't.

Evening was monetization. Pushed a177ba6 with Phase 4: consent-aware post-game review, ad precedence wired in, UMP readiness memoized, confirmed-no-fill schedule, 60-second no-stack guard. Wired the `onPurchased` callback to the shared ReviewEngine. 119 tests passed, 2 skipped, analyzer clean. It's invisible infrastructure work — the kind nobody wants to watch happen. But it has to happen.

Left some uncommitted on purpose: inverted IconControl home buttons, formatter noise, sketch code. The roads are today's story, even though they're incomplete.

The narrower roads are a problem I didn't anticipate. Probably shrink the tanks tomorrow. But the refactor itself feels right — cleaner code, cleaner visuals, cleaner rules. Sometimes you need the visual payoff to forgive the complications it creates.
