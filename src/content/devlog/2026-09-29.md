Morning was roads. Spent weeks convinced the filled concentric outlines with radius-0.7 were the right answer — proper energy, mathematically sound. Then pushed the new approach: exact arc math, radius-0.5 quarter circles, one stroked centreline per route via roundedPolyline. Code cleaner than expected. Deleted cornerRadii, roadOutline, filletPolyline, truncatePolyline, and the junctions parameter entirely. That moment when deleting code also deletes visual noise. Rare and good.

But the width collapsed from 0.55 to 0.45 cells. Invader.locate's exact math bought us tighter visuals and cost us road space. Now enemy bodies and lane drift overhang the pavement. The super tank alone is 0.5 wide. Either shrink the bodies by 20% or revert to 0.5-width roads. Haven't decided yet. There's a version that works. Just not sure it's this one.

Caught something in the leaderboard while reviewing the data. An idle daily survival run somehow got posted to the real leaderboard. 0:43, genuine entry, real problem. The kind of anomaly that jumps out when someone actually looks at the stats. Redirected test runs to campaign levels instead of daily survival. Small fix, large weight — one line, prevents a category of test noise.

Evening was monetization. Phase 4 wired down: consent-aware post-game review, ad precedence logic, UMP readiness memoized while impossible states continue retrying, confirmed-no-fill schedule, 60-second no-stack guard. Connected Monetization's onPurchased callback to the shared ReviewEngine. Validation passed 119 tests with 2 skipped and a clean analyzer. The kind of invisible infrastructure work that nobody wants to watch happen but has to exist.

Left some uncommitted on purpose: inverted IconControl home buttons, formatter churn, sketch code. The roads are today's story.

The narrower roads are a problem I didn't anticipate. Probably shrink the tanks tomorrow. But the refactor itself feels right — cleaner code, cleaner visuals, tighter rules. Sometimes you need the visual payoff to justify the complications it creates.
