Cleanup in the morning. Postcard queue was rebuilding maps twice on campaign tab switches — once from the isolate callback, once from paint if the result wasn't ready. Fixed it. Opacity fades in place now. The campaign district got faster and it feels smoother.

Then the real thing landed: star-earned tower progression. Needle and Bloom free. Frost at 25 stars, Ember at 50, Lance at 100. The Field Guide got a second tab — TURRETS — with live arenas. Each tower in its own little battle, zoomed to a 2.6-cell window, locked cards showing how many stars away. Built it proper: TurretKind carrying unlock costs and ordering, ProgressStore.arsenal tracking what you've earned, Session knowing which towers to run with.

The Lance redesign was the moment. Used to be this generic pointy thing. Now it's dark glass, a rounded tile beneath. When it focuses — pulls power — the glass goes white-hot. Nine lilac motes accelerate in from 1.5 cells. Shipped a fragment shader for it (lens_drain.frag), a geometric drain effect that washes the board in desaturation, heat shimmer cutting through. User asked to push it harder. Then asked me to dial every knob back. First instinct was right. Lesson learned: one change at a time.

Tower toast after a win. Stars land on the end card, and a dark panel drops from the top: "FROST UNLOCKED" with a small haptic thud, or the next tower dimmed with the progress bar filling. It's got its own queue, survives the Continue button, dismisses on tap. The juice is gentle. It lands.

This is the stuff — visuals clicking into place, animation feels right, the screen does exactly what I pictured. Showed it to everyone around.

Then the numbers came in. Ran the greedy bot on all 100 campaign levels. It builds nothing but needles. Thirty-six needles. Forty needles. Wins every single level. Loses maybe four lives across the whole campaign. The unlock system is meaningless.

The problem isn't balance knobs. It's that needles are so efficient they're the only choice. Frost needs a niche. Lance needs a reason. Bloom can't be a filler tower. But that's not a tuning problem — that's a system problem. Greedy optimization finds the dominant strategy and stays there. Real play is different. A human trying all the towers will discover weaknesses. A bot that only measures "win the level" will never care.

Campaign UI was muddy. Three tiny cards with blurry circle lenses, a yellow Play tag on each, and most of the screen empty. User hated it. Threw together two directions offline — map cards vs a journey line — and user picked the journey. A winding path down the screen, regions as stations alternating left and right, each a postcard lens onto a real level, rimmed by 25 ticks showing clears and stars. The road fills as you progress. Committed 611fe72.

Switched fonts while I was at it. Bundled TeX Gyre Heros (GUST license) — Helvetica won't ship on Android, and Mini Motorways looks too soft for this. Wordmark got Chakra Petch Bold (commits 7847864, 08f0083). Harder, more geometric. Campaign cards, home buttons, all of it. First instinct was techy/sci-fi. User kept it.

Region colors iterated live (commit b37243e): coral got replaced by poppy, periwinkle by sky blue. The lens miniature now paints a 390x844 phone slice, leaning toward the water so the coast shows. Green Cliffs got mountains. Fixed a bug where Apricot Bay inlet drew as a straight bar — the preview wasn't passing shore paths to the terrain painter. Computed Landscape shore in board coords.

Home page got a proper overhaul. Dropped the old demo art, built a city grid in grey and white — two wide streets, one diagonal cutting across. Static streets, but I hung invaders on a ticker walking three routes at game speed. Mad scientist mode: Invaders walk, camera pans over empty city, cards drop in staggered from above with easeOutBack. Commit ad73418. The whole thing has motion.

Then came the invaders themselves. They were bland swimmer outlines. Spent the evening redrawing the whole roster — started with soft single-eyed cells, then went deeper. Motes became tadpoles with a kick-and-glide swim pattern (commit 5ba0eb0). Swarms are water fleas now, hopping and drifting. Tanks are manta rays. Colonies burst into rosets of six tiny darts instead of five motes. User played with directions for a while. Rejected: krill, dividing cells, spirillum. Settled on the seafaring theme — everything feels organic now, purposeful. First TestFlight build with the new roster went out as 1.1.0+14 (commit 87487b7).

Built the earned-tower system properly — ProgressStore.arsenal, TurretKind carrying unlock costs and ordering. All four towers now require stars you actually earned by playing. Seed tool estimates: Frost around level 14, Ember level 26, Lance at level 51. Sweetspot — you unlock them while you're still learning what they do.

Field Guide got its turrets tab running. Each tower on a real board zoomed to 2.6 cells, matched prey circling, live unlocks counting up. Locked cards show how many stars away. User loved how the Lens (renamed from Lance for clarity) pulls the board into white-hot focus. Watched the glass drain effect iterate — first push too hard, then walked every knob back. The subtle version was right. Shipped six more builds through the evening, each one carrying another piece.

Campaign cards restyled with hand-picked pastel colors per region — lavender for this one, coral for that (commit b37243e). Played with the backdrop, brightened the lenses, made the whole thing less muddy. Approved. Region colors locked. Then moved on to the level selector — postcard previews of each region with a winding road connecting 25 stops. Each stop a tiny locked/unlocked level marker snapped to a seeded sine curve. Softwood gentle, Apricot Bay sweeping, Moss Valley switchbacks. Committed 152a3e1.

By late evening the whole campaign experience had transformed. Still broken at the meta level — needles still win — but the journey through it, the presentation, the invaders, the towers you unlock... that part sings.

Balance pass lives in a note. The greedy bot beats everything with needle spam. That's a different kind of fix, and it's waiting for a smarter simulation. Real players will discover the specialists. The bot won't.

For now the gap sits between the beautiful things I made today and the system that breaks it. The code works. The meta doesn't. But the UI shipped. The invaders shipped. The towers shipped. And tomorrow, probably, the balance.
