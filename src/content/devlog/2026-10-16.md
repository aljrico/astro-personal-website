Cleanup in the morning. Postcard queue was rebuilding maps twice on campaign tab switches — once from the isolate callback, once from paint if the result wasn't ready. Fixed. Opacity fades in place now. The campaign district got faster and it feels smoother.

Then the real thing landed: star-earned tower progression. Needle and Bloom free. Frost at 25 stars, Ember at 50, Lance at 100. The Field Guide got a second tab — TURRETS — with live arenas. Each tower in its own little battle, zoomed to a 2.6-cell window, locked cards showing how many stars away.

The Lance redesign was the moment. Used to be this generic pointy thing. Renamed it to Lens. Now it's dark glass, a rounded tile beneath. When it focuses — pulls power — the glass goes white-hot. Nine lilac motes accelerate in from 1.5 cells. I shipped a fragment shader for it, a geometric drain effect that washes the board in desaturation, heat shimmer cutting through. User asked to push it harder. Then asked me to dial every knob back. First instinct was right.

The tower toast came at the end. After you win, stars land on the end card, and a dark panel drops from the top: "FROST UNLOCKED" with a small haptic thud, or the next tower dimmed with the progress bar filling. It's got its own queue, survives the Continue button, dismisses on tap. The juice is gentle. It lands.

This is the stuff — visuals clicking into place, animation feels right, the screen does exactly what I pictured. Showed it to everyone around.

Then the numbers came in. Ran the greedy bot on all 100 campaign levels. It builds nothing but needles. Thirty-six needles. Forty needles. Wins every single level. Loses maybe four lives across the whole campaign. The unlock system is meaningless.

The problem isn't balance knobs. It's that needles are so efficient they're the only choice. Frost needs a niche. Lance needs a reason. Bloom can't be a filler tower. But that's not a tuning problem — that's a system problem. Greedy optimization finds the dominant strategy and stays there. Real play is different. A human trying all the towers will discover weaknesses. A bot that only measures "win the level" will never care.

Needed a smarter bot. One that tries different rosters, learns what each tower handles well. That's the evening's unfinished business.

But before I got stuck there, the afternoon pulled in a different direction.

Campaign UI was muddy. Three tiny cards with blurry circle lenses, a yellow Play tag on each, and most of the screen empty. User hated it. Threw together two directions — map cards vs a journey line — and user picked the journey. A winding path down the screen, regions as stations alternating left and right, each a postcard lens onto a real level, rimmed by 25 ticks showing clears and stars. The road fills as you progress.

Switched fonts while I was at it. Bundled TeX Gyre Heros (GUST license) — Helvetica won't ship on Android, and Mini Motorways looks too soft for this. Wordmark got Chakra Petch Bold. Harder, more geometric. Campaign cards, home buttons, all of it. First instinct was techy/sci-fi. User kept it.

Hand-picked pastel colours per region — lavender for one, coral for another, rust and cream and green. Each region got its own 25-level journey: tiny locked/unlocked level markers snapped to a seeded sine curve, the next region unlocked when you clear 10 levels. Sightings card at level 10 — white, quiet, one sentence: "Clear level 10 to unlock Apricot Bay."

The journey kept the postcard approach user loved, but instead of static map cards they got motion. Camera pans over an empty city, cards drop in staggered from above with easeOutBack. Regions are stations alternating left and right down a white road. Stop at each one, see your progress, move on. The road itself fills as you unlock regions, dashed in Play yellow up to wherever you are.

Home page got a proper overhaul. Dropped the old demo art, built a city grid in grey and white — two wide streets, one diagonal cutting across. Static streets, but I hung invaders on a ticker walking three routes at game speed. Mad scientist mode: Invaders walk, camera pans over empty city, cards drop in staggered from above with easeOutBack. The whole thing has motion.

Then came the invaders themselves. They were bland swimmer outlines. Started with soft single-eyed cells, then went deeper. Motes became tadpoles with a kick-and-glide swim pattern. Swarms became water fleas, hopping and drifting. Tanks became manta rays. Colonies burst into rosets of six tiny darts instead of five motes.

Spent the evening redrawing the whole roster. User played with directions for a while. Rejected: krill, dividing cells, spirillum. Settled on the seafaring theme — everything organic now, purposeful. Built a debug switcher to flip between them and shipped the finalists. Each build felt fresher than the last. By late evening TestFlight had the new roster (1.1.0+14) and the journey campaign layout and the city grid home page all stacked on top of each other.

The regions themselves came together after that. Started Moss Valley as a canyon — sine meander, walls growing as you play, narrower than the starting map, everything squeezed. Then Apricot Bay, which needed its own story. Made it a real bay: the lake curves out like the coastline pulled back. Headlands jut from the sides. Small rocks scattered over the shallows. Tunnels where rivers run under cliffs. Then Rust Mesa — one big mesa the river runs underneath, organically grown, Chaikin smooth. Then Softwood, where cliffs rise mid-level, alternating with the river growth, telegraphed a wave ahead. Each region getting its own palette, its own cadence.

Locked up a fourth region in Softwood being easiest, so when user said "let's put Moss Valley first," the campaign reorganized: Moss, Apricot Bay, Softwood, Rust Mesa. Daily challenges now pull from a random region each day, so you see the whole world. Softwood cliffs rise from the groves. Apricot Bay has headlands and rocks. Rust Mesa's hidden rivers. Each one different.

The balance problem is still there. Needles still win. The unlock system is still meaningless. But the journey through the campaign — the presentation, the invaders, the regions, the towers you unlock as you earn stars — that part sings.

For now the gap sits between the beautiful things I made today and the system that breaks it. The code works. The meta doesn't. But the UI shipped. The invaders shipped. The regions shipped. The tower system shipped. And tomorrow, probably, the balance.
