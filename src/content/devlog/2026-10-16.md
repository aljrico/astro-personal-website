Cleanup in the morning. Postcard queue was rebuilding maps twice on campaign tab switches — once from the isolate callback, once from paint if the result wasn't ready. Fixed by dropping the sync fallback entirely. Now we wait on the isolate quietly, bare ground until the scene arrives. Opacity fades in place. The campaign district got faster and it feels smoother. Lenses themselves got sharper — camera pans over the zoomed board showing the actual coast or canyon, not some blurry circle of chaos.

Then the real thing landed: star-earned tower progression. Arsenal system wired in properly. ProgressStore.arsenal tracks TurretKind unlocks; each kind carries its own unlock cost and ordering. Needle and Bloom free on first play. Frost at 25 stars, Ember at 50, Lance at 100. The seed tool estimates: Frost around level 14, Ember level 26, Lance at level 51. Sweetspot — you unlock them while you're still learning what they do. The Field Guide got a second tab — TURRETS — with live arenas. Each tower runs on a real board, zoomed to a 2.6-cell window, with matched prey circling. Locked cards show how many stars away.

The Lance redesign was the moment. Used to be this generic pointy thing. Now it's dark glass, a rounded tile beneath. Renamed it to Lens for clarity. When it focuses — pulls power — the glass goes white-hot. Nine lilac motes accelerate in from 1.5 cells. Shipped a fragment shader for it, a geometric drain effect that washes the board in desaturation, heat shimmer cutting through. User asked to push it harder. Then asked me to dial every knob back. First instinct was right. Tuning lesson: make one change at a time, or you won't know what actually mattered. Motes constant, drain subtle.

The tower toast came at the end of the sequence. After you win, stars land on the end card, and a dark panel drops from the top with a gentle haptic thud: "FROST UNLOCKED" with a progress bar if it's the next tower. Or just dimmed with stars counting up. Toast queue survives the Continue button, dismisses on tap or after a few seconds. The juice is gentle. It lands.

This is the stuff — visuals clicking into place, animation feels right, the screen does exactly what I pictured. Showed it to everyone I could find.

Then the numbers came in. Ran the greedy bot on all 100 campaign levels. It builds nothing but needles. Thirty-six needles. Forty needles. Wins every single level. Loses maybe four lives across the whole campaign. The unlock system is meaningless.

The problem isn't balance knobs. It's that needles are so efficient they're the only choice. Frost needs a niche. Ember needs a reason. Bloom can't be a filler tower. But that's not a tuning problem — that's a system problem. Greedy optimization finds the dominant strategy and stays there. Real play is different. A human trying all the towers will discover weaknesses. A bot that only measures "win the level" will never care.

The afternoon pulled in a different direction before I got stuck on the bot.

Campaign UI was muddy. Three tiny cards with blurry circle lenses, a yellow Play tag on each, and most of the screen empty. User hated it. Threw together two directions offline — map cards vs a journey line — and user picked the journey. A winding path down the screen, regions as stations alternating left and right, each a postcard lens onto a real level, rimmed by 25 ticks showing clears and stars. The road fills as you progress.

Bundled TeX Gyre Heros (GUST license) for the typography — Helvetica won't ship on Android, and MM looks too soft for this. Wordmark got Chakra Petch Bold. Harder, more geometric. Campaign cards, home buttons, all of it. First instinct was techy/sci-fi. User kept it. Hand-picked pastel colors per region while I was at it — lavender for Moss Valley, coral for Apricot Bay, sky blue for Pacific Haze. Each region's postcard shows the actual coast or canyon, not generic level 1. The 25-tick ring beneath each postcard stays in that region's dark cliff color.

Grabbed the lens miniatures for the cards themselves — LandscapePainter renders a 390x844 phone slice of each region's picked level, with the river under the board and the spawn/base not clipped at the rim. The whole system got more integrated.

Then came the home page redesign. Dropped the old demo art. Built a city grid in grey and white — two wide streets, one diagonal cutting across. Static streets, but I hung invaders on a ticker walking three routes at game speed. The whole thing has motion. "Mini TD." wordmark in TeX Gyre Heros, full stop in the Play yellow. Cards drop in staggered from above with easeOutBack while a camera pans across the empty city. The invaders walk while the page builds.

That pulled me toward the invaders themselves. They were bland swimmer outlines. Spent the evening redrawing the whole roster — started with soft single-eyed cells, then went deeper. Motes became tadpoles with a kick-and-glide swim pattern. Swarms are water fleas now, hopping and drifting. Tanks are manta rays. Colonies burst into roseates of six tiny darts instead of five motes. Each kind has its own quirk seeded from the level's RNG — subtle reach, subtle timing, personality without noise. Tried dozens of directions: krill, dividing cells, spirillum, amoebas with pseudopods, corkscrew spirilla. Rejected all of them. The seafaring theme felt purposeful.

Shipped TestFlight 1.1.0+14 with the new roster as the first upload that actually moved the build number. The invaders look alive now.

Built the infrastructure properly for the earned towers. ProgressStore.arsenal, TurretKind carrying unlock costs. All four towers now require stars you actually earned by playing. Each tower in the Turrets tab runs on a real board, zoomed tight, with matched prey circling nearby. Locked cards show how many stars away. The Lens—that glass pulling white-hot while draining the board—got more iterations. Used to push it harder, then walked every knob back. The subtle version was right.

Shipped six more builds through the evening, each one carrying another piece. Campaign cards got hand-picked pastel colors per region — lavender, coral, periwinkle, dusty blue. Played with the backdrop, brightened the lenses, made the whole thing less muddy. The region level selector moved to a winding road down the screen, 25 stops per region on a seeded sine curve. Softwood gentle, Apricot Bay long sweeps, Moss Valley switchbacks. Each stop a tiny level marker, locked ones greyed and blurred inside the frozen snapshot so it renders cheap. Maps build one per frame off-thread, priority given to the next unbeaten level.

By late evening the whole campaign experience had transformed. The journey down the page felt purposeful. The invaders in the home city grid had personality. The Turret Guide showed what each tower could do. The tower toast landed smoothly. Needles still dominated every strategy the bot tried, but the presentation, the unlock progression, the way the screen felt — that part sings.

The balance problem sits in a note. The greedy bot beats everything with needle spam. That's a system-level fix, not a tuning pass. Real players will discover the specialists. The bot won't. But the UI shipped. The invaders shipped. The towers shipped. And tomorrow, maybe, the balance.

For now there's this gap: the beautiful things I made today and the system that breaks them. The code works. The meta doesn't. The visual story is told. The mechanical story is still being written.
