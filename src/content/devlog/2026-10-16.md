Cleanup in the morning. Postcard queue was rebuilding maps twice on campaign tab switches — once from the isolate callback, once from paint if the result wasn't ready. Fixed. Opacity fades in place now. The campaign district got faster and it feels smoother.

Then the tower system landed: star-earned progression. Needle and Bloom free. Frost at 25 stars, Ember at 50, Lance at 100. The Field Guide got a second tab — TURRETS — with live arenas. Each tower in its own little battle, zoomed to a 2.6-cell window, locked cards showing how many stars away. The Lance redesign was the moment. Used to be this generic pointy thing. Now it's dark glass, a rounded tile beneath. When it focuses — pulls power — the glass goes white-hot. Nine lilac motes accelerate in from 1.5 cells. Shipped a fragment shader for it, a geometric drain effect that washes the board in desaturation, heat shimmer cutting through. User asked to push it harder. Then asked me to dial every knob back. First instinct was right.

The tower toast came at the end. After you win, stars land on the end card, and a dark panel drops from the top: "FROST UNLOCKED" with a small haptic thud, or the next tower dimmed with the progress bar filling. It's got its own queue, survives the Continue button, dismisses on tap. The juice is gentle. It lands.

This is the stuff — visuals clicking into place, animation feels right, the screen does exactly what I pictured. Showed it to everyone around.

Then came the campaign redesign, and it cascaded into the whole afternoon.

Campaign UI was muddy. Three tiny cards with blurry circle lenses, a yellow Play tag on each, and most of the screen empty. User hated it. So I threw together two directions offline — map cards vs a journey line — and user picked the journey. A winding path down the screen, regions as stations alternating left and right, each a postcard lens onto a real level, rimmed by 25 ticks showing clears and stars. The road fills as you progress.

Switched fonts while I was at it. Bundled TeX Gyre Heros (GUST license) — Helvetica won't ship on Android, and Mini Motorways looks too soft for this. Wordmark got Chakra Petch Bold. Harder, more geometric. Campaign cards, home buttons, all of it. First instinct was techy/sci-fi. User kept it.

Home page got a proper overhaul. Dropped the old demo art, built a city grid in grey and white — two wide streets, one diagonal cutting across. Static streets, but I hung invaders on a ticker walking three routes at game speed. Mad scientist mode: Invaders walk, camera pans over empty city, cards drop in staggered from above with easeOutBack. The whole thing has motion.

That pulled me toward the invaders themselves. They were bland swimmer outlines. Spent the evening redrawing the whole roster — started with soft single-eyed cells, then went deeper. Motes became tadpoles with a kick-and-glide swim pattern. Swarms are water fleas now, hopping and drifting. Tanks are manta rays. Colonies burst into rosets of six tiny darts instead of five motes.

User played with directions for a while. Rejected: krill, dividing cells, spirillum. Settled on the seafaring theme — everything feels organic now, purposeful. Built a debug switcher to flip between them and shipped the finalists. First TestFlight build with the new roster went out as 1.1.0+14.

Then the infrastructure sprint. Built the earned-tower system properly — ProgressStore.arsenal, TurretKind carrying unlock costs and ordering. All four towers now require stars you actually earned by playing. Seed tool estimates: Frost around level 14, Ember level 26, Lance at level 51. Sweetspot — you unlock them while you're still learning what they do.

Field Guide got its turrets tab running. Each tower on a real board zoomed to 2.6 cells, matched prey circling, live unlocks counting up. Locked cards show how many stars away. User loved how the Lens (renamed from Lance for clarity) pulls the board into white-hot focus. Watched the glass drain effect iterate — first push too hard, then walked every knob back. The subtle version was right.

Then regions became real.

The journey campaign layout meant I had to actually build multiple regions, each with its own character and difficulty curve. Started with just the structure — Moss Valley first (it's the easiest by definition), then Apricot Bay, Softwood, Rust Mesa. Each region gets its own postcard crop — a hand-picked slice of one of its later levels, locked in full colour behind a white veil. Twenty-five stops per region, snapped to a winding road generated from seeded sine curves. Softwood gentle, Apricot Bay sweeping, Moss Valley switchbacks. Each stop either a cleared level (region colour), a 3-star clear (gold), or locked.

The region-level-10 card reads as a promise now, not a done deal. "Clear level 10 to unlock Apricot Bay" — level number bold, region name bold in that region's dark cliff colour. Callout ring fades to 40% so it doesn't scream.

Apricot Bay got its own palette and canyon shape. Ground colour shifted to a warm sand tone, and I carved a bay into the lake — smooth sine headlands, variable width, organic feel. Added small rocky outcrops scattered across the land, plus 1–2 blocking headlands that jut from the sides. Rivers can tunnel under the headlands now, giving the region its own playstyle. Softwood's cliffs are static, but Bay's rocks give variety.

Moss Valley is a proper canyon now. Two wall cliffs, sine meander, width breathing between 3.9 and 6.5 cells. Built a debug switcher to watch the erosion process — started with boxy bulges, then inset terraces, finally half-cell erosion plus Chaikin smoothing for flowing contours. User: "love it. let's keep it." Palette went dark green — Mini Motorways references, darker cliffs per terrace level.

Then Rust Mesa. Full-width rock band over the first river so it tunnels underneath. Organically grown mesa, smooth curves, placed so the river hides 3-8 cells. The difficulty lives in that hidden stretch — can't see the incoming critters, can't place towers on the tunnel. No twist needed, the geography is enough. Palette went rust: sandy ground, terracotta cliffs.

Shipped six builds through the evening, each carrying pieces. Campaign cards restyled with hand-picked pastel colours per region — lavender for Softwood, coral for Apricot Bay. Played with the backdrop, brightened the lenses, made the whole thing less muddy. Approved. Region colours locked. Then winding-road level selectors, then region-10 milestone cards, then the journey layout itself. It transformed from a three-card stub into a path down the screen.

But then the numbers came in. Ran the greedy bot on all 100 campaign levels. It builds nothing but needles. Thirty-six needles. Forty needles. Wins every single level. Loses maybe four lives across the whole campaign. The unlock system is meaningless.

The problem isn't balance knobs. It's that needles are so efficient they're the only choice. Frost needs a niche. Ember needs a reason. Bloom can't be a filler tower. But that's not a tuning problem — that's a system problem. Greedy optimization finds the dominant strategy and stays there. Real play is different. A human trying all the towers will discover weaknesses. A bot that only measures "win the level" will never care.

Needed a smarter bot. One that tries different rosters, learns what each tower handles well. That's the evening's unfinished business.

For now the gap sits between the beautiful things I made today and the system that breaks it. The code works. The meta doesn't. The UI shipped. The invaders shipped. The towers shipped. The regions shipped. The journey shipped.

And tomorrow, probably, the balance.
