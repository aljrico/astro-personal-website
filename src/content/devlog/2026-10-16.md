Cleanup in the morning. Postcard queue was rebuilding maps twice on campaign tab switches — once from the isolate callback, once from paint if the result wasn't ready. Fixed. Opacity fades in place now. The campaign district got faster and it feels smoother. Bundled TeX Gyre Heros (GUST license) — Helvetica won't ship on Android. Wordmark switched to Chakra Petch Bold. Harder, more geometric. First instinct was techy/sci-fi. User kept it.

Then the real thing landed: star-earned tower progression. Needle and Bloom free. Frost at 25 stars, Ember at 50, Lance at 100. The Field Guide got a second tab — TURRETS — with live arenas. Each tower in its own little battle, zoomed to a 2.6-cell window, locked cards showing how many stars away. Each tower runs on a real board, matched prey circling, live unlocks counting up.

The Lance redesign was the moment. Used to be this generic pointy thing. Now it's dark glass, a rounded tile beneath. When it focuses — pulls power — the glass goes white-hot. Nine lilac motes accelerate in from 1.5 cells. I shipped a fragment shader for it, a geometric drain effect that washes the board in desaturation, heat shimmer cutting through. User asked to push it harder. Then asked me to dial every knob back. First instinct was right.

The tower toast came at the end. After you win, stars land on the end card, and a dark panel drops from the top: "FROST UNLOCKED" with a small haptic thud, or the next tower dimmed with the progress bar filling. It's got its own queue, survives the Continue button, dismisses on tap. The juice is gentle. It lands.

This is the stuff — visuals clicking into place, animation feels right, the screen does exactly what I pictured. Showed it to everyone I could find.

But before I got stuck there, the afternoon pulled in a different direction.

Campaign UI was muddy. Three tiny cards with blurry circle lenses, a yellow Play tag on each, and most of the screen empty. User hated it. Threw together two directions offline — map cards vs a journey line — and user picked the journey. A winding path down the screen, regions as stations alternating left and right, each a postcard lens onto a real level, rimmed by 25 ticks showing clears and stars. The road fills as you progress. Hand-picked pastel colors per region. Apricot Bay gets coral, Softwood gets lavender. The lenses now actually show the terrain — you see the coast for Apricot Bay, mountains for the others. Real postcard previews of what you're unlocking.

Home page got a proper overhaul. Dropped the old demo art, built a city grid in grey and white — two wide streets, one diagonal cutting across. Static streets, but I hung invaders on a ticker walking three routes at game speed. Mad scientist mode: Invaders walk, camera pans over empty city, cards drop in staggered from above with easeOutBack. The whole thing has motion.

That pulled me toward the invaders themselves. They were bland swimmer outlines. Spent the evening redrawing the whole roster — started with soft single-eyed cells, then went deeper. Motes became tadpoles with a kick-and-glide swim pattern. Swarms are water fleas now, hopping and drifting. Tanks are manta rays. Colonies burst into rosets of six tiny darts instead of five motes. User asked to try directions for a while. Rejected: krill, dividing cells, spirillum. Settled on the seafaring theme — everything feels organic now, purposeful. Built a debug switcher to flip between them and shipped the finalists in TestFlight.

Then came the infrastructure sprint. Built the earned-tower system properly — each tower carries its unlock cost and ordering. Seed tool estimates: Frost around level 14, Ember level 26, Lance at level 51. Sweetspot — you unlock them while you're still learning what they do.

Field Guide got its turrets tab running. Each tower on a real board zoomed to a 2.6-cell window, matched prey circling, live unlocks counting up. Locked cards show how many stars away.

Shipped multiple builds through the evening, each one carrying another piece. Campaign cards restyled with colors per region. The level selector got rebuilt as a winding road that snakes down the screen with 25 stops per region — seeded sine curves that feel different for each region. Softwood gentle, Apricot Bay sweeping, Moss Valley switchbacks. Stopped reading maps as generic campaign levels and started reading them per region instead. Moss Valley, Apricot Bay, Softwood, Rust Mesa — four regions, 25 levels each, all on a unified board now. Each region has its own palette, its own terrain rules. Moss has a canyon. Apricot Bay has a curved shoreline. Rust Mesa has a mesa that tunnels under the river. Every region needed its own seed picker to tune the difficulty curve right. Open all levels for now — unlock one at a time later.

By late evening the whole campaign experience had transformed. Still broken at the meta level — needles still win — but the journey through it, the presentation, the invaders, the towers you unlock... that part sings.

Then the numbers came in. Ran the greedy bot on all 100 campaign levels. It builds nothing but needles. Thirty-six needles. Forty needles. Wins every single level. Loses maybe four lives across the whole campaign. The unlock system is meaningless.

The problem isn't balance knobs. It's that needles are so efficient they're the only choice. Frost needs a niche. Lance needs a reason. Bloom can't be a filler tower. But that's not a tuning problem — that's a system problem. Greedy optimization finds the dominant strategy and stays there. Real play is different. A human trying all the towers will discover weaknesses. A bot that only measures "win the level" will never care.

Needed a smarter bot. One that tries different rosters, learns what each tower handles well. That's the evening's unfinished business.

But the gap sits between the beautiful things I made today and the system that breaks it. The code works. The meta doesn't. But the UI shipped. The invaders shipped. The towers shipped. The regions shipped. And tomorrow, probably, the balance.
