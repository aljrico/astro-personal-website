Cleanup in the morning. Postcard queue was rebuilding maps twice on campaign tab switches — once from the isolate callback, once from paint if the result wasn't ready. Fixed. Opacity fades in place now. The campaign district got faster and it feels smoother.

Then the real thing landed: star-earned tower progression. Needle and Bloom free. Frost at 25 stars, Ember at 50, Lance at 100. The Field Guide got a second tab — TURRETS — with live arenas. Each tower in its own little battle, zoomed to a 2.6-cell window, locked cards showing how many stars away.

The Lance redesign was the moment. Renamed it Lens. Used to be this generic pointy thing. Now it's dark glass, a rounded tile beneath. When it focuses — pulls power — the glass goes white-hot. Nine lilac motes accelerate in from 1.5 cells. I shipped a fragment shader for it, a geometric drain effect that washes the board in desaturation, heat shimmer cutting through. User asked to push it harder. Then asked me to dial every knob back. First instinct was right.

The tower toast came at the end. After you win, stars land on the end card, and a dark panel drops from the top: "FROST UNLOCKED" with a small haptic thud, or the next tower dimmed with the progress bar filling. It's got its own queue, survives the Continue button, dismisses on tap. The juice is gentle. It lands.

This is the stuff — visuals clicking into place, animation feels right, the screen does exactly what I pictured. Showed it to everyone around.

Then the numbers came in. Ran the greedy bot on all 100 campaign levels. It builds nothing but needles. Thirty-six needles. Forty needles. Wins every single level. Loses maybe four lives across the whole campaign. The unlock system is meaningless.

The problem isn't balance knobs. It's that needles are so efficient they're the only choice. Frost needs a niche. Lens needs a reason. Bloom can't be a filler tower. But that's not a tuning problem — that's a system problem. Greedy optimization finds the dominant strategy and stays there. Real play is different. A human trying all the towers will discover weaknesses. A bot that only measures "win the level" will never care.

Needed a smarter bot. One that tries different rosters, learns what each tower handles well. That's the evening's unfinished business.

But before I got stuck there, the afternoon pulled in a different direction.

Campaign UI was muddy. Three tiny cards with blurry circle lenses, a yellow Play tag on each, and most of the screen empty. User hated it. Threw together two directions offline — map cards vs a journey line — and user picked the journey. A winding path down the screen, regions as stations alternating left and right, each a postcard lens onto a real level, rimmed by 25 ticks showing clears and stars. The road fills as you progress. I bundled TeX Gyre Heros (GUST license) — Helvetica won't ship on Android, and Mini Motorways looks too soft for this. Wordmark got Chakra Petch Bold, shipped it everywhere. Harder, more geometric. Campaign cards, home buttons, all of it. First instinct was techy/sci-fi. User kept it.

Hand-picked pastel per region: lavender, coral, poppy, periwinkle. Each region a station with its own palette. Softwood gentle, Apricot Bay long sweeps, Moss Valley switchbacks. Then beyond that — Rust Mesa with a mesa the river runs under. Each level a stop on the winding road, locked ones greyed and blurred, 3-starred ones gold. The level selector became this: 25 stops, miniature maps, the next region's unlock tied to 10 clears. User loved how the whole thing came together as one journey instead of a grid of samey squares.

But first, I needed the regions to *be*. Four regions, each with its own maps, growth patterns, difficulty curves. Moss Valley's canyon walls breathing width, Apricot Bay's headlands jutting from the sides, Rust Mesa's mesa creating slots where rivers burrow under. Softwood stayed the base, no tricks. Each region seeded and baked separately, bots tested for a monotonic lives-lost curve (easy to hard). The picker rewrote the campaign order: Moss first as the true beginning, then Bay, Softwood, Rust. Reordered seeds across all 100 levels. Dailies now pull from a random region each day — same region for everyone, different tomorrow.

The invader redesign happened in parallel. They were bland swimmer outlines. Spent the evening redrawing the whole roster — started with soft single-eyed cells, then went deeper. Motes became tadpoles with a kick-and-glide swim pattern. Swarms are water fleas now, hopping and drifting. Tanks are manta rays. Colonies burst into rosets of six tiny darts instead of five motes. Tried spirilla, amoebas, krill. User rejected them all. Settled on the seafaring theme — everything feels organic now, purposeful. First TestFlight build with the new roster went out as 1.1.0+14.

Home page got a proper overhaul. Dropped the old demo art, built a city grid in grey and white — two wide streets, one diagonal cutting across. Static streets, but I hung invaders on a ticker walking three routes at game speed. Invaders walk, camera pans over empty city, cards drop in staggered from above with easeOutBack. The whole thing has motion. Then the Field Guide became its own district on the home map — a second city block where you can see each creature you've encountered and the towers you can unlock.

Then came the infrastructure sprint. Built the earned-tower system properly — ProgressStore.arsenal, TurretKind carrying unlock costs and ordering. All four towers now require stars you actually earned by playing. Seed tool estimates: Frost around level 14, Ember level 26, Lens at level 51. Sweetspot — you unlock them while you're still learning what they do.

Shipped six more builds through the evening, each one carrying another piece. Campaign cards restyled with the hand-picked palette. Region lenses popped with better contrast. Then moved on to the level selector — postcard previews of each region with a winding road connecting 25 stops, each stop a tiny locked/unlocked level marker snapped to a seeded sine curve. Per-region meanders via different rhythms: Softwood gentle, Apricot Bay sweeping, Moss Valley switchbacks. Locked ones blurred and greyed until unlocked. The region-10 marker became its own card: "Clear level 10 to unlock Apricot Bay," with the level number bold and the region name in that region's cliff colour.

By late evening the whole campaign experience had transformed. Still broken at the meta level — needles still win — but the journey through it, the presentation, the invaders, the towers you unlock... that part sings.

But the technical debt is real. The bot beats everything with needle spam. That's a different kind of fix, and it's waiting for a smarter simulation. Real players will discover the specialists. The bot won't. One part of me knows I should reorder the towers' strengths — make Frost handle swarms better, make Bloom shine somewhere. Another part of me knows the real problem is that the bot is greedy, and I need to either fix the bot or accept that campaign balance is for humans, not algorithms.

For now the gap sits between the beautiful things I made today and the system that breaks it. The code works. The meta doesn't. But the UI shipped. The invaders shipped. The towers shipped. The regions shipped with their own identities. And tomorrow, probably, the balance.
