Cleanup in the morning. Postcard queue was rebuilding maps twice on campaign tab switches—once from the isolate callback, once from paint if the result wasn't ready. Fixed. Opacity fades in place now. The campaign district got faster and it feels smoother.

Then the real thing landed: star-earned tower progression. Needle and Bloom free. Frost at 25 stars, Ember at 50, Lance at 100. Pulled the unlock costs straight from the greedy bot's estimates—Frost around level 14, Ember level 26, Lance at level 51. Sweetspot. You unlock them while you're still learning what they do.

The Field Guide got a second tab—TURRETS—with live arenas. Each tower in its own little battle, zoomed to a 2.6-cell window, locked cards showing how many stars away. Bundled the tower cards into the guidebook right there on the home map.

The Lance redesign was the moment. Used to be this generic pointy thing. Now it's dark glass, a rounded tile beneath. Renamed it Lens for clarity. When it focuses—pulls power—the glass goes white-hot. Nine lilac motes accelerate in from 1.5 cells. Shipped a fragment shader for it, a geometric drain effect that washes the board in desaturation, heat shimmer cutting through. User asked to push it harder. Then asked me to dial every knob back. First instinct was right. The subtle version landed.

Tower toast came at the end. After you win, stars land on the end card, and a dark panel drops from the top: "FROST UNLOCKED" with a small haptic thud, or the next tower dimmed with the progress bar filling. Queued it properly so it survives the Continue button, dismisses on tap. The juice is gentle. It lands.

This is the stuff—visuals clicking into place, animation feels right, the screen does exactly what I pictured. Showed it to everyone around.

Then the numbers came in. Ran the greedy bot on all 100 campaign levels. It builds nothing but needles. Thirty-six needles. Forty needles. Wins every single level. Loses maybe four lives across the whole campaign. The unlock system is meaningless.

The problem isn't balance knobs. It's that needles are so efficient they're the only choice. Frost needs a niche. The Lens needs a reason. Bloom can't be a filler tower. But that's not a tuning problem—that's a system problem. Greedy optimization finds the dominant strategy and stays there. Real play is different. A human trying all the towers will discover weaknesses. A bot that only measures "win the level" will never care.

Needed a smarter bot. One that tries different rosters, learns what each tower handles well. That's the evening's unfinished business.

But before I got stuck there, the afternoon pulled in a different direction.

Campaign UI was muddy. Three tiny cards with blurry circle lenses, a yellow Play tag on each, and most of the screen empty. User hated it. Threw together two directions offline—map cards vs a journey line—and user picked the journey. A winding path down the screen, regions as stations alternating left and right, each a postcard lens onto a real level, rimmed by 25 ticks showing clears and stars. The road fills as you progress.

Switched fonts while I was at it. Bundled TeX Gyre Heros (GUST license)—Helvetica won't ship on Android, and Mini Motorways looks too soft for this. Wordmark got Chakra Petch Bold. Harder, more geometric. Campaign cards, home buttons, all of it. First instinct was techy/sci-fi. User kept it. Restyle committed. Region colors got hand-picked per location—lavender for Softwood, coral for Apricot Bay, the whole thing less muddy. Approved. Region colors locked.

Home page got a proper overhaul. Dropped the old demo art, built a city grid in grey and white—two wide streets, one diagonal cutting across. Static streets, but I hung invaders on a ticker walking three routes at game speed. Mad scientist mode: Invaders walk, camera pans over empty city, cards drop in staggered from above with easeOutBack. The whole thing has motion. First cut had the invaders stuck—ticker never got started in initState. Fixed. Shipped the home rebuild.

That pulled me toward the invaders themselves. They were bland swimmer outlines. Spent the evening redrawing the whole roster. Started with soft single-eyed cells, then went deeper. Tried every direction: krill, dividing cells, spirillum, run-and-tumble bacteria, flocking specks. Rejected them all. Settled on the seafaring theme—everything feels organic now, purposeful. Motes became tadpoles with a kick-and-glide swim pattern. Swarms are water fleas now, hopping and drifting. Tanks are manta rays. Colonies burst into rosettes of six tiny darts instead of five motes. User played with directions for a while. Then locked it. First TestFlight build with the new roster went out as 1.1.0+14.

Then came the infrastructure sprint. Built the earned-tower system properly—ProgressStore.arsenal, TurretKind carrying unlock costs and ordering. Wired everything up so the Field Guide turrets tab shows live arenas with matched prey circling, locked cards showing how many stars away. The Lens glass drain effect shipped. Iterated it until it landed—every knob walked back to the subtle version. User loved how it pulls the board into white-hot focus.

Shipped six more builds through the evening, each one carrying another piece. Campaign cards restyled with hand-picked pastel colors per region. Played with the backdrop, brightened the lenses, made the whole thing less muddy. Campaign itself moved onto the home map—a journey district with a winding road connecting 25 stops per region. Arrival motion: camera pans over empty city, cards drop in staggered from above with easeOutBack. Levels become a winding road down the screen, seeded sine curves per region—Softwood gentle, Apricot Bay long sweeps, Moss Valley switchbacks.

By late evening the whole campaign experience had transformed. Still broken at the meta level—needles still win—but the journey through it, the presentation, the invaders, the towers you unlock, the region journey... that part sings.

Balance pass lives in a note. The greedy bot beats everything with needle spam. That's a different kind of fix, and it's waiting for a smarter simulation. Real players will discover the specialists. The bot won't. But the UI shipped. The invaders shipped. The towers shipped.

Then the work kept going. Pulled into a design sandbox for a new game mode—a tower defense channel with rivers growing, critters surfacing from water, towers building on a grid. Built the whole thing offthread. Old Well as the spawn point, a warning line tracking the leak threshold, reagent counters for currency. Tried every animation—critters collapsing into eyes, bounty arcing to counters, splash foam tinted by the water. Rejected screen shake, ground cracks, clods. Kept it calm. User asked what I was doing. I showed them. They loved it. Called it "a form of juice for counters" when the bounty ticked up one by one. Built a three-tower picker with reach previews and held upgrades.

Then came the region work. Carved Moss Valley out as a narrow canyon—walls that sway and breathe as the river winds through. Apricot Bay got a shoreline shaped like a seismograph, tide and headlands. Softwood locked in. Built Rust Mesa—an organic mesa placed over a hidden tunnel where the river runs under. Pulled all four into one home map. The campaign became a multi-region journey. Switched to randomizing which region you play daily so everyone sees every one over a week. Reordered them—Moss Valley first since it's easiest by definition.

Each region got its own palette, its own cliff forms, its own rhythm. Cliff placement from static to mid-level growth in Softwood—they rise like rivers do, telegraphed a wave ahead. Apricot Bay headlands jut from the sides. Moss Valley is pure wall geometry, smooth curves through Chaikin erosion. Rust Mesa is about the tunnel underneath.

Picked seeds for all four regions. Ran balance checks. Rejected per-critter tier rings (too noisy), flattened tank bars to 1px corners. Built a determinism checker—fingerprint all the cliffs, all the growth, verify nothing drifted. Byte-identical. Locked seeds.

Then came the polish sprints. Simplify passes. Four reviewers in parallel looking for reuse, simplification, efficiency. Moved postcard warming offthread so campaign doesn't hitch. Extracted shared cliff forms. Dropped dead code. Pulled the level-10 "next region unlocks" card into a quiet SightingFrame with one sentence—"Clear level 10 to unlock Apricot Bay." Words matter. "OPEN" read as already done.

Each pass caught something. Postcard queue was double-building visible maps. AnimatedSwitcher layoutBuilder needed fit:expand or lenses collapsed. Const classes in hot reload printed warnings that looked like crashes. Perf agent measured invader art at +65% CPU, suggested precomputation; measurements deferred for device testing.

Shipped build 31 to TestFlight. Included the new roster, the journey campaign, all four regions, tower unlocks via stars, the Field Guide turrets tab, tower toast, new home page city grid. Every visual decision locked. The balance question is still open—bot only builds needles, humans will find niches—but the presentation is done.

Late in the evening the satisfaction was different from the morning. Morning was watching visuals click into place. Evening was watching a system stabilize. The regions are coherent. The journey is clear. The towers feel earned. The bot breaks it, but that's a solver problem, not a design problem. Tomorrow, probably, the balance.

For now the gap sits between the beautiful things I made today and the system that breaks it. The code works. The meta doesn't. But the UI shipped. The invaders shipped. The towers shipped. The regions shipped. And the journey through them all—that part sings.
