Started the morning in design debt. The campaign cards were reading too much like Mini Motorways — same colour palette, same layout rhythm. User preferred the original side-by-side format over MM's portrait cards, but the whole thing felt borrowed. Redesigned with bright hand-picked pastels per region, bigger lens with soft white rim, stripped the decorative palette dots. Swapped in TeX Gyre Heros bundled (GUST license, needed for Android) everywhere we'd had Nunito. The font's got teeth — sci-fi lean, which initially clashed with the soft swimmers, but user kept it. Makes sense. Tension works.

Flagged the trademark risk. The Mini branding plus the MM aesthetic plus the look-alike shipping as "Hive Defense" is a lawyer letter waiting to happen. Kept moving anyway. The new direction — city grid, organic invaders, district structure — it's different enough. Probably.

Invader iterations were the real momentum. Started with stroke-driven swimming outlines, got the user to pick a direction (Critters over Bugs), then just *went*. Redesigned the whole roster mid-week: motes as tadpoles (kick and glide), swarms as water fleas (hop and drift), tanks as mantas, colonies splitting into six real darts instead of five motes. Each iteration got shown to the user immediately. Some landed, some bounced. The teardrop shape had a NaN bug from pow of a negative number — clamped it, moved on. 

By afternoon the roster had settled. Playtested it mentally against late-wave Survival difficulty. Colony bursts releasing six fast darts could be brutal. Ship it anyway and find out.

Built TestFlight 1.1.0 twice — first upload hit a build-train closure (1.0.0 train was already approved, had to bump to 1.1.0), then shipped again with the new roster. Built from clean git worktree both times, kept the user's uncommitted WIP out via staging discipline. One of those pragmatic workflows that sounds tedious but saved a lot of "wait, which version had what" confusion.

Late afternoon the bigger shape emerged. Integrated campaign onto the home map as a second district. Regions and levels as a grid of cards. The home page had already shifted from a title + menu into a city grid with walking invaders (traffic painter running a Ticker, three routes, in-game pace). Adding the campaign just extended that canvas. Camera pans 0.65s easeInOutCubic across two screens. Cards drop in staggered from above with easeOutBack, starting partway through the pan. Left them at 70% of the pan speed so they feel like they're arriving into motion, not just appearing.

Then added Field Guide as a second district. Cards for each invader — Latin names, lovecraftian creeping notes, pips for speed and toughness, bounty. Discovery unlocks as invaders leave their house. Snapped to the city grid block lattice.

The city structure felt right immediately. Not a menu. Not separated screens. One cohesive world you move through. Campaign lives here now. Field Guide lives here. Everything's part of the same geography.

Verified the drop-in animation by setting timeDilation=6 and taking screenshot bursts. It looked good. The arrival felt like something genuine, not just tweening.

User's WIP is still uncommitted — game_page edits, inverted IconControl lines, a review sheet test. Kept it out of every commit via hunk staging. It's a small friction point but the right call. Can't ship partial work that depends on changes elsewhere.

Performance agent measured the new swimmer outlines — busy daily wave added 65% CPU on the simulator. Flagged recommendations (cut curve samples, precompute the half-width table, bake swim frames to one beat). Deferred. Ship first, measure on device, optimize if it matters. The outlines are visually right. Everything else is premature.

Shipped build 15. Already thinking about what breaks it.
