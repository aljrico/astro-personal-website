Today's output: one icon. One commit. And I'd defend every minute of it.

The Cases button on the home screen had this book icon that never sat right with me. It said "library" when it should have said "your stuff, organized." So I went hunting. Checked the icon packs I've got stashed on iCloud first — nothing. Not a single folder icon in the whole collection. The closest candidates were an envelope, a clipboard, and a scroll. A scroll. For case files. No.

So we made one. Pasted in a PNG of some folder icons I liked, had the machine trace them into duotone SVGs, then strip out their navy-and-grey and retint them into the app's palette — red chocolate and mango, the combo that makes everything feel like it belongs. Then the fun part: three variants side by side, single folder, pair, stack, hot reload flipping between them while I just... looked. No spreadsheet. No framework. Just squinting at a screen going "that one."

The stack won. Obviously the stack. A stack of folders says *accumulation*, says *you've been working*, says more than one case lives here. The single folder was too lonely. The pair was indecisive.

This is the stuff that puts me in flow and I've stopped apologizing for it. It's not architecture. It's not algorithms. It's making the screen look like the picture in my head, and today the loop was tight — trace, tint, reload, compare, pick. Gut decisions at gut speed. The agentic thing earns its keep here: I never touched an SVG path by hand, just kept saying "warmer" until it was right.

The quiet voice showed up on schedule, of course. *You spent a session on an icon.* Yeah. I did. But the home screen is the handshake. It's the thing you see forty times a day, and every time it looked slightly wrong, it cost something small. Now it doesn't.

One commit, pushed to the 1.13.0 release branch. The book icon is gone, the folder stack lives in its place, and I won't miss the old one for a second. Small day, clean win. Tomorrow can have the hard problems.
