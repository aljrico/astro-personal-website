Today's output, first half: one icon. Total.

The Cases button on the home screen has been bugging me for weeks. It had this book icon that never quite said "cases." It said "library," maybe "diary." Close enough that nobody would complain, wrong enough that I noticed it every single time I opened the app. That kind of wrong is worse than broken. Broken you fix. Slightly-off you just live with, quietly annoyed, until one day you snap.

Today I snapped.

First stop: the icon packs I already own, the ones sitting in iCloud that I keep telling myself justify their price. Surely someone drew a folder. No. Envelopes, clipboards, scrolls — a whole stationery shop, no folders. Who makes an icon pack without a folder? Rejected the lot.

So I made my own. Pasted in a folder icon I liked and let the machine do the tracing — I pointed, it drew, duotone SVG out the other end. Then it retinted the thing from its boring navy and grey into the app's palette: the red chocolate and the mango shade, colors I named months ago and still stand behind. This is the agentic thing working exactly the way I want it to. I didn't touch a path point. I just kept saying "warmer" and "no, the other layer" and watched it happen. Then the fun part: three variants — single folder, pair, stack — swapping them live with hot reload like trying on jackets. Change a line, watch the screen, feel it. This is the part of the work I'd do for free. The stack won immediately. A stack of folders says *cases* — plural, accumulating, work waiting inside. The single folder just said "folder." The pair said nothing at all.

Committed, pushed to the release branch for 1.13.0. One icon, one commit. I thought that was the day.

It wasn't.

Because the folder stack apparently woke something up, and by the second session I was building a whole Performance section into the stats sheet. Folder-tab panel — tabs, actual folder tabs, the icon metaphor escaping its button and colonizing the UI — one tab per difficulty, and inside each one a density curve of your completion times per environment, pulled straight out of the XP ledger. Not bar charts. Proper Gaussian KDE curves, smooth and organic, the kind of thing that makes a puzzle app's stats sheet look like it went to university.

The physicist got to come out and play, and for once I didn't resent him. The naive KDE clipped ugly at zero — completion times can't be negative but the kernel doesn't know that — so: reflection at the zero boundary, let the tail breathe a couple of bandwidths past the 95th percentile, make sure the area actually integrates to one. Twenty minutes of the kind of math I used to do for a living, in service of a curve that just needs to *look right* on a cream-colored tile — 3px stroke, a whisper of fill. Honestly the best use of a physics degree I've found yet.

Then the colors fought back. Each environment needed its own curve color, and the first picks — an orange, a watermelon — looked cheap on the cream background. I tried hand-picking pastels next. Also bad. Two rounds of guessing before I did the artisanal thing I should have done first: sampled the blue straight from the islands' water sprite, the pink from the grove's blossom trees. Now each environment carries its own representative color in one registry — one source of truth instead of scattered hex codes — and each curve is literally wearing its environment's own paint. That's the detail nobody will ever consciously notice and I will notice every single time, which after this morning is apparently the entire theme of the day.

And because not everyone has enough solves yet, the section needed a locked state that doesn't feel like a wall. Progress bars — X of N solves per environment, all wired to one threshold constant. Six samples, currently. I keep nudging that number up and down like it's a thermostat. Too low and the curves are noise. Too high and nobody ever sees them. Statistics says more, impatience says less. Impatience is winning.

Small trap along the way: the empty state says you need a few more solves to "unlock the plot," and the machine translating the copy decided "plot" meant *storyline* in every non-English locale. La trama. Die Handlung. Eight languages promising narrative where there is only a graph. Caught it, fixed it, filed it under "trust but grep." The orchestration itself worked beautifully though — the big model on the heavy build, a smaller one on copy and progress bars across nine locales, me stepping in directly only to nudge single values. Conducting, not typing. The devlog keeps recording this shift and I keep not being sure how I feel about it, except fast. Fast is how I feel about it.

Second commit, same release branch. What started as "fix one icon" ends as a release with a new icon *and* a stats section that draws actual probability densities.

The morning ledger said: hours of effort, one small asset. The evening ledger says: the icon was never the point. It was the warm-up. Some days the polish is the appetizer and the main course shows up uninvited.

Venga. 1.13.0 is getting heavy. Time to ship it.
