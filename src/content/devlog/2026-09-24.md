Morning was roads. The old approach looked wrong — concentric outlines, variable radii, inside corners pinching. Pinching is poison for geometry that's supposed to feel clean. So we swapped to exact arc math. Fixed radius-0.5 quarter circles, one centreline per route.

Deleted five methods. Simplified invader location. Narrowed the roads from 0.55 cells to 0.45.

Now the roads are too narrow. Enemy bodies overhang, especially the super tank at 0.5 wide. Either shrink bodies 20%, or widen roads back to 0.5, or leave it for step 5. Haven't seen the junction merges on screen yet, so the full picture is still missing. That's the problem with precision work — you optimize one thing and break the balance of three others.

Evening was Phase 4. That one locked in.

Ads logic knows when to quit. When to stop stacking, when to respect the user's choice, when it's just not filling. Memoized the success paths. Added the 60-second guard that kills the attempt if nothing's working. Wired the purchase event back into the review engine. That was the last wire. Everything talks to everything now.

119 tests passed. Analyzer clean. The code is boring — guard clauses, scheduling logic, memoization. That's exactly right. Clever wakes you at 2am with a bug. This is the thing you don't think about again.

There's philosophy underneath. Every state machine resolves the conflict between the user celebrating their win and the business wanting monetization. Today I just turned that decision into code.

One gotcha: left the simulator running on a stray daily run. It lost and posted to the real leaderboard. Reminder that your test environment will eat your prod if you're careless. Test on campaign levels.

Left changes uncommitted on purpose. Home buttons, formatter noise, other bits. Tomorrow.
