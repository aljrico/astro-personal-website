Campaign page got the treatment today. The panels were muddy — desaturated greys, weak lens, pointless palette dots fighting green blobs. Stripped it back. Picked bright pastels per region (the orange and lavender went nowhere; landed on a warm #FFDD94 for Poppy Bay, sky blue #92d2ea for Blue Hour). New PageFrame backdrop, bigger lens with soft white rim. The cards stayed side-by-side — portrait layout didn't sell.

The font thing got complicated. Helvetica can't ship on Android, so TeX Gyre Heros everywhere Nunito was. It's got the spacing and weight of Motorways, which... yeah. Made sense at the time.

Then the home page happened.

Was supposed to be a card grid. Instead rebuilt it as a city grid — Mini Motorways aesthetic, white streets on ground grey, invaders walking traffic routes like little pedestrians. Added a wordmark (TeX Gyre Heros, full stop in warm gold). The invaders needed new shapes to match the softer vibe, so crawled through a parade of rejected forms: amoebas with pseudopods, tapered spirilla with corkscrew surges, bead chains, run-and-tumble rods. Landed on the swimmers — single-eyed droplet forms with travelling-wave outlines, different per-kind (tadpoles kick-and-glide, water fleas hop, mantas glide, colony members cluster then split).

The whole aesthetic pulled in the MM direction. Soft. Organic. Playful.

Shipped the TestFlight build (1.1.0+14). First upload rejected because the 1.0.0 train was already closed. Android bundling worked clean. No issues.

Late in the day, someone (the perf measurement system) flagged the new swimmer art: +65% CPU draw time. Dropped recommendations — precompute the curves, cut outline samples, bake per-kind half-widths. Didn't implement yet. Kept the art for now.

Here's the thing: the home page looks *good*. The invaders walk the city like they own it. The colors are right. The pace of decisions, the zero-friction pushes to main, the fast iteration — it all felt smooth.

But there's a trade-off living underneath. The "Mini" name plus the MM aesthetic is a trademark landmine. Dinosaur Polo Club territory. The app ships as "Hive Defense" for a reason, and today I pushed it toward "Mini TD" with that exact visual language.

Is it worth it? The design is honest. It feels right. And it probably can't ship with that branding.

The user's uncommitted work sat in the background all day, excluded from every commit. Good staging hygiene. But there's a weight to unfinished business — the inverted lines, the widget refactor, the test stubs. They're waiting.

Tomorrow probably means resolving the naming risk or backing the visual direction away from Motorways. Or both. Or shipping it anyway and accepting the risk.

For now: the swimmers look great. The home page walks. The campaign lens shows the coast. That's the day.
