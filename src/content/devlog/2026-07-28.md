Today's output, measured honestly: two colors. Three commits, two hex values. And I'd defend every minute of it.

The casebook folders were dissolving into the sky. Cream cards on a peach backdrop, close enough in tone that the whole home screen read as one warm smear. The kind of thing nobody would file a bug about, but once you see it you can't unsee it. So I gave the folders their own paper palette instead of touching the shared cream ramp, because that ramp feeds the difficulty pills and the premium gradient and I've been burned before by "small" color changes that ripple into places I forgot existed.

Then the good part. Hot reload on the simulator, one value at a time. Tried a shade that was basically the sky wearing a disguise. Too close. Tried darker. Way too dark, suddenly the folders looked dirty. Landed on the same subtle step the cream ramp uses, lived with it for a bit, then nudged it darker one more time because the first version was too polite. This is the loop I actually love. Change a number, watch the screen, feel whether it's right. No spreadsheet can tell you when paper looks like paper. Your eyes just know.

And I mean *my* eyes, specifically. At some point the agent could have been taking screenshots and judging the shades itself, and I shut that down fast. During visual iteration the loop is: it changes the value, I look at the simulator, I say warmer or darker or done. The machine types, I taste. That division of labor felt exactly right today.

The agent offered to call the color "manila." Tempting. Flavor names are fun until six months from now when I'm grepping for a color and can't remember whether it's manila or parchment or biscuit. Kept the boring names. Boring names are a gift to future me, who is, historically, an idiot.

Once the paper ramp existed, it started pulling other things toward it. The countdown bar on the home screen was still sitting on the old cream shade, and next to the new folders it looked like it hadn't gotten the memo. Switched its track over to the folder-paper ramp. Third commit, one line, and suddenly the whole screen agrees with itself. That's the thing about introducing a new color: you're not adding one value, you're adding a small gravitational field.

Also settled something about how I work now. I've been strict about the orchestrator setup, the big model plans, the worker models type. But making a subagent ceremony out of changing one hex value is like hiring a contractor to hang a picture frame. So: trivial one-value tweaks get edited directly, real work gets delegated. Obvious in hindsight. Most workflow rules are.

All three commits pushed by end of day. Small day. Paper-colored folders that finally sit *on* the sky instead of *in* it, and a countdown bar that matches them. Nobody will ever notice, which is exactly the point. The best polish is invisible. It just makes the screen feel slightly more like a place.
