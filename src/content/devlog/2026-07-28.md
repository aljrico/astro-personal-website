Today's output, measured honestly: two colors. Three commits, two hex values. And I'd defend every minute of it.

The casebook folders were dissolving into the sky. Cream cards on a peach backdrop, close enough in tone that the whole home screen read as one warm smear. The kind of thing nobody would file a bug about, but once you see it you can't unsee it. So I gave the folders their own paper palette instead of touching the shared cream ramp, because that ramp feeds the difficulty pills and the premium gradient and I've been burned before by "small" color changes that ripple into places I forgot existed. Two new names in the palette, folder paper and its shade, living next door to the cream they refused to be.

Then the good part. Hot reload on the simulator, one value at a time. Tried `#eeca90` first, which was basically the sky wearing a disguise. Too close. Tried darker, `#e2ad6b`, and suddenly the folders looked dirty. Landed on `#fee8c6`, the same subtle RGB step the cream ramp uses for its own shade. This is the loop I actually love. Change a number, watch the screen, feel whether it's right. No screenshots, no agent describing pixels back to me. My eyes, the simulator, a number that keeps changing. No spreadsheet can tell you when paper looks like paper. Your eyes just know.

The agent offered to call the color "manila." Tempting. Flavor names are fun until six months from now when I'm grepping for a color and can't remember whether it's manila or parchment or biscuit. Kept the boring names. Boring names are a gift to future me, who is, historically, an idiot.

Late afternoon I came back to it. Lived with `#fee8c6` for a couple of hours and it was too polite. The step was there but it whispered. Nudged it darker one more notch, `#f9e0b8`, and this time it held. That's the thing about color work: you can't judge it in the moment you make it. You have to leave, come back, and let it ambush you.

And then the small ripple I'd half expected. The countdown bar under the folders was still riding the old cream shade, so once the folders got their paper ramp, the bar looked like it belonged to a different screen. One-line fix in the countdown widget, third commit. This is why I didn't touch the shared ramp in the first place: the ripples came anyway, but at least they came to me instead of surprising me in some pill three screens away.

Also settled something about how I work now. I've been strict about the orchestrator setup, the big model plans, the worker models type. But making a subagent ceremony out of changing one hex value is like hiring a contractor to hang a picture frame. So: trivial one-value tweaks get edited directly, real work gets delegated. Obvious in hindsight. Most workflow rules are.

All three commits pushed to the 1.13.0 release branch. Small day. Paper-colored folders that finally sit *on* the sky instead of *in* it, and a countdown bar that agrees with them. Nobody will ever notice, which is exactly the point. The best polish is invisible. It just makes the screen feel slightly more like a place.
