Spent the session on something nobody will ever consciously notice. The placement ripple.

When you drop a suspect onto the board, the row and column flash. It used to fire all at once, and the origin cell — the one you actually placed on — didn't flash at all. Which is insane when you say it out loud. The star of the show, excluded from its own party. Now the landed cell flashes first and the ripple cascades outward, 25ms per cell, like dominoes falling away from the impact. I didn't type a line of it. Described the domino feel, let the agent build it, watched it run.

Then the interesting part. The timing was *correct* and it read *wrong*. Ripple firing exactly at touchdown looked late, because the ink effect has a slow attack — by the time it's visually loud, the piece has been sitting there for ages. So the cascade now starts 90ms *before* the piece lands. It's a lie. The animation cheats. And it reads perfectly. The haptic and the reveal still lock to the true landing because those are instant, they don't need the head start.

This is the stuff. Not architecture, not clever algorithms. Ninety milliseconds of dishonesty that makes a piece of glass feel like it has weight. I kept placing suspects just to watch the dominoes go. Nobody will ever say "nice cascade timing" in a review. Doesn't matter. I'll know.

Almost shipped a landmine, though. Days ago the flight duration got cut from 280ms to 180ms, and the tests still had 280ms-derived pump timings hardcoded in them. Green the whole time. Testing nothing. Caught it right before commit, made the duration public so the tests derive their timing from the real constant instead of a copy-pasted number. The kind of bug that never breaks anything until the day it breaks everything, and you find it by accident or not at all. Found it by accident. Taking the win.

Committing was its own little dance — another session has live changes in the same tree, so it's temp-index surgery to take only my files and leave theirs breathing. Two of me working the same repo at once. The mad scientist would call it leverage. The evening version notes that I've built a workflow where I have to be careful not to step on my own toes, plural.

Small day. Good day. The board feels better than it did this morning, and I'm the only person on earth who knows why.
