Spent the day on ripples. The domino cascade — you place a piece and the whole row and column flash outward from where it landed, 25ms per cell. The origin cell wasn't flashing before. It was literally excluded from its own party. It is now. Small thing. Completely changes how the landing feels. The board *reacts* to you instead of politely acknowledging you.

This is the work I actually love. Not the architecture, not the state management. This. Making the screen do the thing your gut says it should do. I kept placing pieces just to watch the cascade run. That's how you know it's right — when you can't stop triggering it.

The interesting part was the timing. The cascade was firing exactly at touchdown, which is technically correct and perceptually wrong. The ink ripple has a slow attack, so by the time your eye registers it, the piece has been sitting there for a beat and everything reads late. Fix: start the cascade 90ms *before* the piece lands. There's now a constant in the codebase literally called the ripple lead, whose entire job is to lie on schedule. The animation fibs about when things happen so that it feels like the truth. Haptics and the occupant reveal still lock to the real landing, because your thumb is harder to fool than your eye.

I find this genuinely funny. Correct timing feels wrong. Wrong timing feels correct. Physics brain wants to object. Physics brain has been overruled.

Also caught something that could've bitten later: flight duration got cut from 280ms to 180ms a while back, and the flight tests were still pumping frames derived from the old number. Hardcoded. Silently broken. Green, but green by accident. Made the duration constant public so the tests derive their pump timings from the real value now. Five minutes of work, and honestly the most satisfying five minutes of the day — catching the bug *before* the commit instead of three weeks after, mid-confusion, wondering why the tests lie to me. Past me usually leaves those landmines. Today's me defused one. Progress, maybe.

The AI built most of the cascade while I sat there directing timing values like some kind of animation conductor. Faster, slower, earlier, no — earlier. This is the workflow now. My contribution was taste and 90 milliseconds. I think that might be the whole job, actually. The taste and the milliseconds.

Wrapped up by late afternoon: full test suite green, analyzer clean, everything honest for once. Then the commit itself, which was its own little operation. Another session has live uncommitted work in the same repo — gradle stuff, dependency changes, not mine to touch — so I committed through a temp index, picking my files by hand and stepping around theirs. Surgery next to someone else's open surgery. It worked. Don't love it. Two versions of me operating on the same patient and neither one anesthetized.

Good day. Small, but good. The kind where nothing shipped to anyone and it still felt like the game got better.
