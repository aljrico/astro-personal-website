Animation timing day. The best kind of day.

The domino cascade had been bugging me. A piece lands, the row and column ripple outward — except the origin cell wasn't flashing, so the ripple looked like it started from nowhere. Fixed that. Now the origin flashes too and the rest cascade outward by distance, 25ms per cell. Sonnet built it while I directed. This is the arrangement now. I say "the ripple should start where the piece lands, obviously," and thirty seconds later it does. No keystrokes. Just decisions.

Then the interesting part. The cascade was firing exactly at touchdown, which is technically correct and visually wrong. The ink ripple has a slow attack — by the time your eye registers it, the piece landed ages ago. It read late. So now the cascade *leads* the touchdown by 90ms. It starts before the piece hits.

I love this stuff. The truth of the physics and the truth of the eye are different truths, and the eye wins. The haptic and the occupant reveal still lock to the real landing, because your thumb isn't fooled as easily as your eye. Lying to one sense and not the other, in the same frame. This is the craft nobody sees. When it's right, it's invisible. That's the whole point and also the whole tragedy.

Almost shipped a landmine, though. Earlier the flight duration got cut from 280ms to 180ms, and the tests were still pumping frames derived from the old 280. They passed. Green checkmarks, checking nothing. Caught it right before commit — made the duration public so the tests derive their timings from it instead of hardcoding numbers that rot. I say code quality is overrated and I mostly mean it, but tests that lie are worse than no tests. At least no tests are honest about it.

Committed via a temp index because another session had its own uncommitted mess in the shared repo, and I tiptoed around it like a burglar in my own house. Two versions of me working the same checkout, trying not to step on each other. There's a metaphor in there. Not going to dig for it.

Everything green. Small day, good day. The kind where nothing dramatic happens except the game feels 90ms better, and 90ms is everything.
