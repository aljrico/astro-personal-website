Spent the afternoon on the ripple.

When you place a suspect, the piece flies from the tray to the cell, and now the whole row and column ripple outward from the landing point. Domino cascade, 25ms per cell, each cell's delay derived from its distance to the origin. The origin cell flashes too — it was excluded before, which looked subtly wrong in a way I couldn't name until I fixed it and suddenly could.

This is my flow state and I know it. Not architecture, not algorithms. A tile landing on a board and the board *reacting*. I directed, the agent typed, and the loop was tight enough that it felt like painting. Trigger the animation, squint, adjust, again. I must have placed the same suspect forty times.

The interesting bug wasn't a bug. The ripple fired exactly at touchdown, frame-perfect, and it still read late. Because the ink ripple has a slow attack — by the time your eye registers it, the piece has already been sitting there for a beat. So now the cascade starts 90ms *before* the piece lands. The haptic and the occupant reveal stay locked to the true landing, but the ripple lies a little, and the lie is what feels true. There's even a named constant for the lie now, `_rippleLead`, sitting in the flight code like it was always meant to be there. You can't reason your way to that number. The physicist brain wants the animation to fire when the event happens. The eye doesn't care what the physicist thinks.

Also caught an actual bug before committing. We'd cut the flight duration from 280ms to 180ms a while back, and the test was still pumping frames derived from the old 280. Silently broken — passing, but testing a flight that no longer exists. Made the duration public so the tests derive their timings from the real value. Five minutes of unglamorous work that will save some future version of me an hour of confusion he'll never know he avoided.

Ran the whole suite before shipping. Everything green, analyzer clean. Then committed through a temporary index because another session of me was live in the same tree, mid-surgery on the build config and dependencies — I had to thread my commit around their changes without touching them. Two of me working the same repo at once. There's a joke in there somewhere about parallel timelines and paths not taken, but it's evening and I'm not going to reach for it.

Ninety milliseconds of anticipation. That's the day's output. Nobody will ever consciously notice it.

That's the whole point.
