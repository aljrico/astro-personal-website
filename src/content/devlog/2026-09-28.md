Got the roads right this morning. Quarter circles, proper junctions, one clean stroked line per route. The old approach — filled concentric outlines with those adaptive radii — looked worse the more I stared at it. Radii shrank on short legs, inside corners pinched. It was doing too much.

This felt like the right call. Stripped out five functions that were doing variations of the same thing and weren't buying anything anymore. Invader's code is lighter now. The roads look solid. Shipped it.

Then reality came knocking: the new geometry is narrower, and now the enemy bodies and lane drift overhang. Super tank is half a cell wide. The roads are .45 cells. The math works on paper but looks wrong on screen.

Could shrink the bodies twenty percent. Or go back to the old width. Or accept the overhang as a stylistic thing. Haven't decided yet. That's tomorrow's problem.

---

The Verdoku work landed hard. Phase 4 — consent-aware post-game ads, proper precedence, UMP memoization to handle the readiness state while impossible states keep retrying. Wired the monetization layer into the review engine. Ran the full test suite. One hundred nineteen tests passing, two skipped, analyzer clean.

That's the kind of session where the tests are telling you something is actually right. Not just aesthetically right. Functionally right. The kind that makes you trust the code.

---

Small embarrassment: an idle daily survival run in the simulator lost and somehow posted itself to the real leaderboard. NeonWhale9018, forty-three seconds. Nobody's going to mistake that for a real run, but it's the kind of mistake that makes you feel stupid for about two minutes. Testing on campaign levels only from here on.

Carrying uncommitted changes on some tool work and the formatter didn't care enough to push. Those can wait.

Two projects, two different temperatures. One's asking questions I can't answer yet. The other's answering them cleanly. Both feel right, in their own way.
