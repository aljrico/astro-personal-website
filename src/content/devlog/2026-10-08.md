Campaign cards needed the visual treatment. Muddy desaturated panels, weak colour story, pointless palette dots—nothing that drew the eye. New approach: bright hand-picked pastels per region. Iterated through Pacific Haze blue, rejected it, tried lavender (too cool against the warm palette), coral (too close to apricot), landed on poppy. Then the user picked a golden #FFDD94 that shouldn't have worked but did. Added a soft white lens rim, bigger than before. Bundled TeX Gyre Heros for the display titles since Android won't bundle Google Fonts. These aren't earth-moving changes individually but stacked together they're the difference between a page that whispers and one that breathes.

Rebuilt the home page as a city grid. Walking invaders on patrol routes at game speed, two streets crossing in the middle, the whole thing ticking along. First cut had a ticker that never started—technically correct, completely useless. Moment it fired the page came alive. That's the thing about these mechanical resets: the design doesn't need to change, it just needs to move.

Creature design was the work of the day. Riffed through bells and rods and run-and-tumble cells and bead chains—hours of nothing landing. Then hit on stroke-driven swimmers: tadpole that kicks and glides, water flea that hops, manta, a rosette colony. Each one draws as a travelling wave plus surge with one centred eye. Something about the simplicity made it work. Shipped two TestFlight builds off it. Momentum is everything.

Verdoku got the exploit fix mid-afternoon. Case timer now banks exact stopwatch time and runs while the app's inactive—stops only when hidden. Found the attack vectors: app switcher freezing the timer, sub-second resumes gaming the old logic, XP farming on Android, device pairs copying solutions. Wrote the server-side migration: hardened submit, per-tier floors, server-derived score, reject future dates. All tested. All done. Then the database hit a wall—migration history drifted between prod runs by microseconds but that's enough to block the push. The work is solid. It'll ship when the infrastructure stops fighting itself. This teaches patience.

Late afternoon I opened a worktree sandbox and started sketching a completely different game. Tower defense on a canal. Black sea at the top, mint waterway flowing down, creatures trying to drain out the bottom, towers to intercept them. No plan for this. Wasn't scheduled. Just started building and four hours disappeared.

The tower UI became the thing. Picker boxes morph into an upgrade screen, selection peeks next-rank reach in dashed lines, selling towers counts down their cost with digit swaps and pops them away with a square thud. Death juice stacks—burst and shards in tower colour, eye arcing to a counter. Every moment got tuned down to the timing. Critters surface near shore with eyes fading in. Kill payouts tick up slowly. Leak animations collapse into red. The whole thing reads immediately.

Can't remember the last time I hit this state. Where every decision feels obvious and the screen becomes exactly what you pictured. No second-guessing. No clock-watching. Just clean intentions and momentum and the feeling that you're not writing code—you're watching something come together.

Still in the sandbox. Not pushed, not merged, not real yet. No idea if this is the future or beautiful waste. Doesn't matter tonight. Tonight it's just flow.
