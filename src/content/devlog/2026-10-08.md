Campaign cards needed the visual treatment. Muddy desaturated panels, weak color story, pointless palette dots—nothing that drew the eye. Started with Pacific Haze blue (rejected, too cool against the warm palette), tried lavender (clashed), coral (too close to apricot), landed on poppy. Then the user picked a golden #FFDD94 that shouldn't have worked but did. The whole thing locked into place once I added a soft white lens rim bigger than before, and shifted the lens content itself: a 390x844 phone slice showing the river and terrain instead of empty ground. Clipping was killing it until I realized the preview wasn't computing shore and sea paths properly—fixed by shifting board coords correctly.

Font decision: Mini Motorways uses Helvetica, can't bundle on Android. Pulled TeX Gyre Heros (GUST license) for display titles and body everywhere Nunito was. ARCO stays for the logo. Only got 400 and 700 weights so w800 renders bold. Bundled it locally so it doesn't depend on the network. Tried MM-style portrait cards (colour top/white bottom) but the user kept the original side-by-side layout with the new colors.

Rebuilt the home page as a city grid—two wide white streets crossing in the middle, invaders walking patrol routes at game speed. First cut had a ticker that never started—technically correct, completely useless. Moment it fired the page came alive. That's the thing about these mechanical resets: the design doesn't need to change, it just needs to move. The wordmark went to Chakra Petch Bold (OFL from Google Fonts), more techy than the soft organic swimmers but the user kept it. Added a "Play" tag to unlocked campaign cards—flat #F8C66A, white bold text, sits bottom-right next to the clear counter. Contrast is weak (1.7:1 instead of WCAG 4.5:1) but the text is large and bold enough. The tag is semantic only; the whole card still opens the level grid.

Creature design was hours of nothing. Riffed through bells and rods and run-and-tumble cells and bead chains and amoebas and spirilla and corkscrew swarms. Then hit on stroke-driven swimmers: tadpole that kicks and glides, water flea that hops, manta, a rosette colony of six darts. Each one draws as a travelling wave plus surge with one centred eye. Something about the simplicity made it work. Each invader gets per-kind quirks via seeded randomness so they're not all identical. The roster settled fast: mote as tadpole, swarm as water flea, tank as manta, colony as six real darts (not five motes—they burst out as full threats with shared health pools in sixths). 

Shipped TestFlight 1.1.0 (14) but it got rejected—the 1.0.0 train had already closed. Bumped to 1.1.0 (15) and uploaded. Built from a clean worktree to keep uncommitted work out. The performance agent flagged the new swimmer art at +65% CPU draw time on simulator, recommended cutting outline samples from 24 down to 10-12 by curving through midpoints. That's a defer—measure on device first.

Verdoku got the exploit fix. Case timer now banks exact stopwatch time and runs while the app's inactive—stops only when hidden. Found the attack vectors: app switcher freezing the timer, sub-second resumes gaming the old logic, XP farming on Android, device pairs copying solutions. Wrote the server-side migration: hardened submit, per-tier floors, server-derived score, reject future dates. All tested. Then the database hit a wall—migration history drifted by microseconds and that's enough to block the push. The work is solid. It'll ship when the infrastructure stops fighting itself.

Afternoon came and I opened a worktree sandbox and started sketching a completely different game. Tower defense on a canal. Black sea at the top, mint waterway flowing down, creatures trying to drain out the bottom, towers to intercept them. No plan for this. Wasn't scheduled. Just started building and four hours disappeared.

The design evolved fast. Canal on a home-grey grid, wakes per critter kind, one black Old Well spawning pool, scale bar showing wave progress. The objective was the first puzzle: houses filling with goo didn't read, tried comb branches, settled on the last stretch darkening 1/10 per leak with a liquid front (40px wavelength, 1.5s period). Critters invisible in the black sea, eyes fade in near shore, water swells before each surfaces. Four towers: three established ones plus a new Ember at 400 cost.

The tower UI became the whole afternoon. Drag-to-build with a ghost that floats above the thumb and snaps to grid—watermelon red when invalid. Reach preview shows as a hairline ring in tower color with canal water lit white inside it. Tap to select a tower and the four picker boxes morph into three: name plus rank pips, UPGRADE slot with next-rank icon and deltas, and SELL with refund. Holding UPGRADE peeks next rank's reach in dashed lines. No floating stats card—board stays clear.

Every moment got tuned down to the timing. Critters surface with eyes fading in. Kill payouts tick up one-by-one (reaching full count in 0.5s) in the reagent counter. Leak animations collapse into a red radial glow that gathers onto an eye, eye arcs to the counter. Death juice stacks—burst and shards in tower color, eye arcing with white flash ring. Sold towers pop away with a square thud ring. Leak path runs red end to end: body collapses red, eye flies to homes counter in red. Kill payouts stay charcoal eye and yellow burst. A warning line—dashed watermelon red—sits one row short of the lake so there's wiggle room. Critters past it blink red at 4Hz but stay targetable.

Reagent counter ticks in place now (killed the jolt), homes counter keeps the spring on life loss—different motions for different signals. Digit swaps happen at Duration.zero so they feel instantaneous. Late in the session I realized the jolt was separate from the digit animation—when the user said "it jumps," they meant two different things. Fixed both. The homes counter keeps its spring; the reagent just counts up quietly. That distinction matters.

The palette shifted mid-session: ground went grass #CBE2B5, canal and lake cream #F8F7EF, grid light #D6E9C4. The wakes flipped to full charcoal ink via saveLayer, pencil feel. The reach light-up went from white overlay to tower color tint since white on cream does nothing. User said "astonishingly good" when the promo palette landed.

Can't remember the last time I hit this state. Where every decision feels obvious and the screen becomes exactly what you pictured. No second-guessing. No clock-watching. Just clean intentions and momentum and the feeling that you're not writing code—you're watching something come together.

Still in the sandbox. Not pushed, not merged, not real yet. No idea if this is the future or beautiful waste. Doesn't matter tonight. Tonight it's just flow.
