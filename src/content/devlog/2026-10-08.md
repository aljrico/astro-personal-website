The morning was color work. Four iterations on the campaign page backdrop — blue, then lavender (rejected, too cool), then coral (too close to apricot), then poppy. The user picked a warm golden yellow (#FFDD94) and it landed. The whole page snapped into place: bright hand-picked pastels per region, bigger lens with soft white rim, the muddy desaturated panels finally gone. This is the work that doesn't feel like work — picking a number and watching the screen turn beautiful.

Font updates followed. Bundled TeX Gyre Heros everywhere (GUST license, no Android bundling issues). Helvetica was never happening on Android anyway. The wordmark, the page titles, all of it tightening up. The font feels softer, less utilitarian. The campaign cards matched the shift.

Then the home page. Rebuilt it as a city grid — Mini Motorways style, but ours. Streets, grid, walking invaders looping three routes in real time. The invader animation was busted in the first cut (ticker never started), fixed in maybe ten minutes. It's satisfying when the bug is something that obvious. The invaders walk. The page breathes. There's something living in it now.

Pushed three times before lunch. Play tag on the cards. Better lens geometry. Better colors. Each commit was small and intentional.

The invader roster shifted again. Soft swimmers instead of sharp geometric shapes — tadpole mote with a kick-and-glide gait, water flea swarm that hops and drifts, manta tank, rosette of six darts in the colony. Rejected: amoebas (too lumpy), spirilla (weird corkscrew felt forced), krill, dividing cells. The protozoan family settled. The user kept it.

Shipped TestFlight 1.1.0 (14). Version numbering was already drifting — earlier bumps weren't all committed — so rebuilt from clean worktree to keep the WIP out. The build went up, added to the groups, submitted for beta review. The usual process, the usual slight inefficiency (pushing 1.0.0 that then got rejected, bumping to 1.1.0). It's fine. The new roster is in people's hands now.

Afternoon: Field Guide district on the home map. Cards with Latin names and lovecraftian notes, speed and toughness pips, bounty per kind. The discovery mechanic wired in — seenInvaders from the progress store. The invaders walk routes, and when they leave their house, you've *seen* them. The cards unlock.

Then the campaign moved onto the map. Regions became block cards with lenses and clears and stars. Levels became a 5x5 grid on half-block lattice. The next unbeaten level glows in Play yellow. The camera pans over the empty city first, then the cards drop in staggered with easeOutBack — all this motion, all this arrival. It feels like coming home.

But the real work started in the evening.

The canal mode. Been exploring it in a worktree sandbox for hours now. In-game map redesign. The concept shifted from elaborate (canal town, specimens, survey sheet, lights out atlas) to minimal. A canal on the home grey grid, wakes per critter kind, an Old Well pool where they spawn. Black sea across the top, mint canal fading up from it. A scale bar that's actually the wave progress. A key-style tower picker.

The objective is elegant: last stretch darkens one-tenth per leak. A liquid front with a 40px wavelength, 1.5s period. Critters go invisible in the sea, eyes fade in near the shore. They surface and move toward the houses. You shoot them before they reach the canal.

Iterated eight times on details tonight. Leak animation collapsed into the eye flying to the homes counter. Bounty impact in Play yellow with a jolt and a flash. The reagent counter counts up one by one. Towers mutable so frost slow and kill-shard colors follow your builds. Tower picker redesigned: drag-to-build with a ghost that snaps to grid, reach preview as a hairline ring in tower color with the water lit white inside. Tap to arm or tap twice. Selecting a tower morphs the four picker boxes into three: name and rank pips, upgrade slot, sell button.

The red warning line. Dashed watermelon red across the canal one row short of the lake — critters past it blink red at 4Hz, still targetable. The whole leak path goes red: body collapses red, eye flies to the counter red. Kill payouts stay charcoal and yellow.

Cut the swell lines, the crashing waves, the pulsing mouth rings. The night finale. Too much. User was right. Minimal reads louder.

None of this is pushed. It's all sitting in the sandbox worktree, eight commits deep, not on main. But the energy is there. This is the thing. The city grid was good — it's shipped, it's out there. But this canal mode is *alive*. Every iteration lands. Every decision tightens it. The geometry, the colors, the motion, the juice on the impacts — it's all singing.

That's the night. The devlog closes on one sentence: the canvas exists, and it's waiting for tomorrow.
