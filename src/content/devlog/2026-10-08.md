Started the morning restyling campaign cards. Needle turret barrel was orange — shouldn't be. Pulled it back to ink black. The whole campaign page was muddy: desaturated panels, a green blobby backdrop fighting the cards, pointless palette dots. Looked locked when it wasn't.

Fixed it by picking bright hand-picked pastels per region. New PageFrame backdrop. Bigger lens with soft white rim. The dots vanished. Cards breathed again.

Font was next. Mini Motorways uses Helvetica. Can't bundle that on Android. So: TeX Gyre Heros everywhere Nunito lived — page titles, buttons, all of it. ARCO stays for the logo because the logo needs weight. Heros only ships at 400 and 700 so anything heavier fakes bold, but it works.

Shipped the first build.

Then pushed home page as a Mini Motorways city grid with walking invaders. Real streets, invaders walking real routes at in-game pace. A Ticker, drawn on every frame, invaders colliding if they pile up. First cut had a `late final` ticker never accessed so nothing moved. Noticed immediately — that kind of bug screams in your face when you press play and watch the city sit static.

Fixed it. Invaders walked.

There's a trademark problem here. "Mini" plus this look is someone else's filing. App ships as Hive Defense currently. That's a decision for later but the flag is up.

Mid-afternoon, started shipping invader redesigns. Polygon critters were sharp. Soft ones read better — swimmers with traveling waves, one centered eye. Motes became tadpoles. Swarms became water fleas. Tanks became mantas. Colonies became six darts instead of five motes.

By evening, landed on the final roster. Tested it. Shipped build 15.

---

But the real day happened in the sandbox.

Spun a worktree for a completely different game. Same critters, different world. A canal on a grid. Critters emerge from a black sea at the top, flow down blue water, reach a white lake at the bottom. You build towers on the grid. Water flows between them. Last stretch of canal has liquid rising — lose homes when a critter reaches the lake.

Spent the whole night polishing it. Dragging towers from a picker, ghosts snap to grid, invalid zones glow red. Reach preview shows a ring in tower color with white coverage inside. Selecting a tower morphs the picker into three buttons: name and rank, upgrade with deltas, sell with refund.

The juice landed hard. Critters shot by towers burst into eight spinning chunks and a colored shard spray. Leak impacts flash red. Reagent counter ticks up one by one — twenty per second — digits rolling as they catch up. Tower boxes dim and light against the total. Last row of canal has a dashed warning line. Critters past it blink red at 4Hz but still die if you shoot them before the lake.

It's minimal and dense. Dark and wet. Every animation has weight. The grid stays clear. No floating text, no UI clutter — everything folds into the HUD on top.

Didn't push any of it. It's all on the sandbox worktree, uncommitted. The main branch has its own momentum — builds shipping, campaign refined, invaders landed. The canal is something else entirely. Two games, one codebase, one person stretching across both.

This is the moment before the fork. Before I have to pick which one becomes the thing and which one hibernates forever in a forgotten branch.

Tonight, both exist in parallel. Both are alive. Tomorrow one of them probably isn't.
