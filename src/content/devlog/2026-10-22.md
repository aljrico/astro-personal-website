Started convinced the balance was close. Wasn't.

Pushed the perf patch (clamp helper) and round-2 tweaks. Ember at 5/8/13. Wave-mix genes for the evolved bot. The hybrid-seeded run was pulling 17+ waves where the old one barely hit 14. That felt like vindication.

Then I ran the full ladder and the edifice cracked.

The evolved bot was good at exactly one thing: Bloom + Lance in a specific ratio. The neutral bot went straight Ember and hit 20 waves. Even the hybrid start plateaued—CMA-ES stuck in a local optimum. Two days of compute and the answer was: I'd trained a bot that was excellent at one strategy and couldn't break out of it.

So I built a search tool. Beam search from scratch—what's the cheapest defense that survives wave 11? No evolution, no machine learning. Just: what does the search pick?

Needle + Ember.

Not Bloom. Not Frost.

I'd spent 18 hours tuning Bloom's reload and range. The search said it didn't matter.

There's a voice that says: you're not good at this. Someone smarter tries search first.

There's another that says: you didn't know that until you tried the other thing.

Both are true.

Around 4:25pm the campaign UI needed fixing. The three muddy cards with blurry lenses read as broken—60% of the screen empty, weak lenses, pointless palette dots. User hated it enough that the whole thing got rebuilt.

Pushed into a complete restyle. Bright hand-picked pastels per region: Softwood #FFDD94, Apricot Bay #92d2ea, Moss Valley #cfe8d5, Rust Mesa #ECD9B0. Each card got a bigger lens rimmed in region colour with a soft white border on a light backdrop. Added a yellow Play tag (#F8C66A) bottom-right on unlocked cards—flat design, white bold text—and dropped the pointless three dots. Matches the Mini Motorways language.

Then swapped the fonts entirely. Nunito wouldn't bundle on Android, Helvetica the same. Picked TeX Gyre Heros (GUST licensed, already bundled in the tree) for everything UI. Heros reads cleaner at display sizes, all caps all the time. The wordmark stays ARCO. Once I committed to Heros everywhere—titles, cards, buttons—the whole grid locked into place. Added Chakra Petch Bold (Google Fonts OFL) for the home wordmark and square buttons, wanted something with a touch more personality there.

White on #F8C66A is technically 1.7:1 contrast, below WCAG spec. Kept it anyway because Mini Motorways does the same and the text is large and bold.

Then I looked at the home page and forgot about balance for a while.

Home was a logo and three muddy cards. Killed the ARCO logo, drew a TeX Gyre Heros wordmark ("Mini TD." with a full stop in Play yellow) and painted a city grid—two wide white streets, a diagonal street crossing an avenue—with a ground colour and faint grid. Put a Ticker underneath that walks invaders down patrol routes at game pace. The invaders were still the old four shapes, all angles, unloved. By late afternoon I'd redesigned the whole roster.

Six sketches per critter. Kept the ones that felt alive. Settled on soft single-eyed swimmers: mote as a teardrop kick-and-glide, swarm as a long water flea with a hop-and-drift, tanks as a squat manta with swimming tail lobes, colony starting as five pulsing motes. Stroke-driven outlines, one centred eye. Tested bells, spirilla, flocking specks, bead chains, krill, runs of dividing cells. Burned them all.

But iterations kept pulling. Motes became tadpoles, swarms became water fleas, tanks became mantas, colony became six real darts instead of five motes—each a full dart with full health, so the waveWeight math worked in sixths. The creatures started to feel alive.

Shipped build 14 to TestFlight with the new invader designs. The perf agent measured 65% CPU / 16% GPU overhead on a busy wave compared to the old shapes. Recommended cutting outline samples or precomputing swim frames. Parked it—the designs read better than they perform.

Built a Field Guide while momentum carried. Two tabs: invaders with Latin names and Lovecraftian notes (a joke, but it landed), and towers. Each tower card runs on a real board zoomed so you see it work. New tower selection: drag-to-build with a floating ghost, reach preview in tower colour, all the juice. Red bursts for leaks, yellow for bounties, counters that tick up one-by-one. Renamed Lance to Lens—the charging effect via fragment shader drains light around the focal point while 9 lilac motes accelerate in from 1.5 cells. That visual landed immediately.

Then I noticed the greedy bot only builds Needles. All 30+ of them. Every level. Every region. The unlocking system was perfect except it was meaningless.

Back to balance. But this time I didn't spiral. The search tool had cracked the first 11 waves. Now I needed regional difficulty curves. Each terrain is different. Moss Valley canyon walls narrow the options. Apricot Bay's coast splits the river. Rust Mesa's tunnel trades space for vulnerability. The waves had to respect that.

Rewrote waves as floor formulas of difficulty x and wave w. x = 0.25 per region + 0.01 per level minus 1. Gating: wave 1 motes and swarms only, shells on even waves, darts on odd waves past 3, colonies rare. The lever was mote HP growth: 4·x·(w−1). A fractional multiplier quietly gave darts extra hits. Fixing that retuned everything.

Rounds 3, 4, 5 happened in a blur. Dial the knobs. Ember gets overtuned (bots hit 30 waves, sims take 3 minutes each). Bloom stays dead. By hour 20 I was tired and the search was right. Starting cash to 250. Ember 7/11/16. Bloom buffed on range (user's call—range identity over DPS parity). Bloom went from 3% of builds to 12%. Also discovered: 20% of dailies are unwinnable at 150 cash. Every bot dies by wave 4. That's not balance. That's broken.

The campaign structure came together region by region. Moss Valley opened the campaign (easiest by definition), canyon walls that breathe in and out as you descended. Apricot Bay with its coast: hand-picked postcard crops showing water that shaped itself per level via headlands jutting from a side, small rocks strewn across the land. Softwood with gentle meanders—cool blues and greys. Rust Mesa last: a full-width rock band with rivers that could tunnel underneath, giving the region its own difficulty without needing a handicap.

Each region got its own palette and character. The campaign card lenses painted live, locked regions veiled behind white, cleared ones bright. Built a journey layout—regions as stations down a white road, alternating left and right, each ringed by 25 level stops (region shade = cleared, gold = 3 stars). Road dashed in Play yellow, filling as clears opened the next region, so the next region unlocked at 10 clears. Winding paths inside each region too—no straight grids. Per-region character baked in at the generator level.

Added a milestone card at level 10 for each region. A quiet sighting frame with one sentence: "Clear level 10 to unlock Apricot Bay." Once the clears landed, the toast dropped and the card showed "LEVEL 10 UNLOCKED X."

But the greedy bot still only built Needles. So I pushed deeper on the regional mechanics themselves. Softwood's cliffs now rise mid-level, alternating with river growth—a dashed footprint a wave ahead, build-blocked, then a 1.2-second slide up. Apricot Bay got headlands (blocky cliffs jutting 3–4 columns from a side) and small nameless rocks strewn over the land. Moss Valley's canyon walls stayed carved, just shaped better. Rust Mesa kept its mesa, but now the generator placed it over the first river so it tunneled underneath, giving players a spatial puzzle without new enemy types.

Around 6:45pm I added the wordmark in Chakra Petch Bold—a typeface with a little more personality. SquaredButton labels switched to it too. Body text and titles stay TeX Gyre Heros. The contrast felt right immediately.

Built a ladder tool—parallel sims on 8 cores, moved map gen to an Isolate so boards build in 20ms. Then faster: grids as quarter-cell with ray-cast fallback, Bloom SpatialHash with dense Int32List buckets. 1.8x faster on AOT.

Built builds 14, 15, 16, all the way to 32. Campaign postcards, region lenses, invader redesigns, tower showcase in the Field Guide. Each build shipped to TestFlight, each one a little more real than the last. By the end of it I was shipping builds faster than I could count them.

Tower unlocking: stars from campaign levels unlock new towers. Needle and Bloom free, Frost at 25 stars, Ember at 50, Lance at 100. After a win's stars land on the end card, a dark toast drops from the top: "FROST UNLOCKED" or the next tower dimmed with "29 / 50 ★" and a bar filling. That juice matters. The user seeing that counter climb is everything.

There's a thing you learn: you can't solve balance before humans touch it. You ship. They find breaks you didn't simulate. Some things you only know once someone plays it for real.

I want to ship and see what breaks. But first the journey has to feel alive. And today—today it does.
