Started convinced the balance was close. Wasn't.

Pushed the perf patch (clamp helper) and round-2 tweaks. Ember at 5/8/13. Wave-mix genes for the evolved bot. The hybrid-seeded run was pulling 17+ waves where the old one barely hit 14. That felt like vindication.

Then I ran the full ladder and the edifice cracked.

The evolved bot was good at exactly one thing: Bloom + Lance in a specific ratio. The neutral bot went straight Ember and hit 20 waves. Even the hybrid start plateaued—CMA-ES stuck in a local optimum. Two days of compute and the answer was: I'd trained a bot that was excellent at one strategy and couldn't break out of it.

So I built a search tool. Beam search from scratch—what's the cheapest defense that survives wave 11? No evolution, no machine learning. Just: what does the search pick?

Needle + Ember.

Not Bloom. Not Frost.

I'd spent 18 hours tuning Bloom's reload and range. The search said it didn't matter.

There's a voice that says: you're not good at this. Someone smarter tries search first.

There's another that says: you didn't know that until you tried the other thing.

Both are true.

The search cracked the first 11 waves. But then I looked at the rest—the endless stretch past wave 11, the regional difficulty curves. I rewrote waves as floor formulas: x = 0.25 per region + 0.01 per level minus 1. A fractional multiplier quietly gave some critters extra hits. Fixing that retuned everything. Twenty percent of dailies were unwinnable at 150 starting cash. Bumped it to 250. New Ember cost: 250, dmg 7/11/16.

By mid-afternoon the campaign UI needed fixing. The three muddy cards with blurry lenses read as broken—60% of the screen empty, weak lenses, pointless palette dots. The user hated it enough that the whole thing got rebuilt.

At 16:25, pushed into a complete restyle. Hand-picked pastels per region: Softwood golden #FFDD94, Apricot Bay sky blue #92d2ea, Moss Valley moss green, Rust Mesa sand. Each card got a bigger lens rimmed in region colour with a soft white border on a light backdrop. Added a yellow Play tag bottom-right—flat design, white bold text—and dropped the pointless dots. Campaign page read cleaner immediately. Matches the Mini Motorways language.

The fonts were wrong too. Nunito wouldn't bundle on Android. Helvetica the same. Picked TeX Gyre Heros (GUST licensed, already bundled in the tree) for everything UI. Heros reads cleaner at display sizes—clean and workmanlike, which is what campaign needs. Later added Chakra Petch Bold (Google Fonts, OFL) for the home wordmark and square buttons—wanted something with personality. White on the Play yellow is technically 1.7:1 contrast, below spec. Kept it anyway because Mini Motorways does the same.

By 16:40, the lenses came next. Started with blur and weak terrain. Added the coastline from Apricot Bay's bay shape, showed actual water texture so locked regions didn't read as broken. Each region's postcard had character built into the crop itself—Softwood L6 showing the gentle flow, Apricot Bay L26 showing the coast, Moss Valley showing both walls. One simple rule: show the region's signature feature.

Then I looked at the home page and forgot about balance for a while.

Home was a logo and three muddy cards. At 16:59, killed the ARCO logo, drew a TeX Gyre Heros wordmark and painted a city grid—two wide white streets, a diagonal crossing an avenue—with a ground colour and faint grid. Put a ticker underneath that walks invaders down patrol routes at game pace. The city grid itself was a new painter (ground #E5EBEA, faint grid, white streets). The "Mini TD." wordmark in bold 84pt, full stop in Play yellow. Invaders walk 3 routes via a ticker at in-game speed. First cut had a bug—a `late final` ticker that never got accessed, so nothing moved. Fixed by starting it in initState. Extracted invader drawing from the game's scene so home + game share bodies, code stays DRY.

By late afternoon the invader designs shifted six times.

First cuts: burned bells, spirilla, flocking specks, bead chains, krill, dividing cells. But iterations kept pulling. The creatures started to feel alive. Motes became tadpoles (kick & glide), swarms became water fleas (hop & drift), tanks became mantas, colonies became rosettes of 6 darts instead of 5 motes. The colony burst mechanic changed too—now spawns six real darts (dart speed + health) instead of motes, so waveWeight comes in sixths. Tests updated. Help text updated.

By 18:45, pushed the stroke-driven swimming as the keeper. Single-eyed swimmers, stroke-driven outlines, one centred eye each. No eyespots, no internal detail. Just motion and intention. Slowed movement = weaker wave energy, which felt right. Shipped TestFlight 1.1.0 (build 14) then build 15 with new designs; measured +65% CPU / +16% GPU overhead on a busy wave. Noted it. Shipped anyway because the designs read better.

Built a Field Guide while momentum carried. Two tabs: invaders with Latin names and lovecraftian notes, and towers. Each tower card runs on a real board zoomed so you see it work. New tower selection: drag-to-build with a floating ghost, reach preview in tower colour, counters ticking up one-by-one. Renamed Lance to Lens—the charging effect drains light around the focal point while 9 lilac motes accelerate in from 1.5 cells. That visual landed immediately.

But the greedy bot only built Needles. All 30+ of them. Every level. Every region. The unlocking system was meaningless.

Back to balance. But this time I didn't spiral. I pushed deeper on the tower earning system itself. Built tower unlocking: stars from campaign levels unlock new towers. Needle and Bloom free, Frost at 25 stars, Ember at 100. After a win's stars land, a dark toast drops from the top. "FROST UNLOCKED" or the next tower dimmed with progress. That juice matters.

By early evening the campaign structure came together region by region. At 19:03, moved the campaign onto the home map itself. Campaign + Field Guide share the district right of home; region levels below. Regions as block cards (lens rimmed in region colour, clears, stars, Play tag); levels as 5x5 lots on half-block lattice, next unbeaten level in Play yellow. Leaving a game returns to region grid. Built an arrival animation: camera pans over empty city, page header + cards drop in staggered (45ms) from 28px above with easeOutBack. Lift away in reverse on leave.

Softwood opened the campaign, canyon walls carved with sine meanders. Apricot Bay with its tidal bay-shaped coast, headlands blocking routes, and small rocks strewn to block tower placement. Moss Valley as a winding canyon, walls narrowing and breathing, difficult by geometry alone. Rust Mesa last: a full-width rock band placed over the first river so it tunnels underneath.

Each region got its own palette and character. Softwood golden ground, Apricot Bay cream, Moss Valley moss green, Rust Mesa sand. Built a journey layout—regions as stations down a white road, alternating left and right, each ringed by 25 level stops. Road dashed in Play yellow, filling as clears opened the next region. Unlock at 10 clears to keep progression paced. Winding paths inside each region too—no straight grids. Per-region character baked in at the generator level via seeded sine curves (Softwood gentle, Apricot Bay long sweeps, Moss Valley switchbacks). Catmull-Rom smoothing on the stops so they felt alive. Stops went through sizing iterations—too small felt pointless, 80-100pt landed right—and each region showed its own map miniature shrunk down, one per frame queued by priority (next level first), so Moss Valley's ~40ms per level generation didn't hitch.

Added a milestone card at level 10 for each region. Once the clears landed, the card showed "Clear level 10 to unlock [Region Name]", with the region name in its cliff colour. That framing worked—promise, not done deal.

Shipped builds 17 through 19 in a blur. Campaign postcards, region lenses, invader redesigns, tower showcase in the Field Guide. Each region with its own character, each build a little more real than the last.

Then came the tower Field Guide redesign. Each tower card now shows a real game board, the tower running on real waves, with matched prey circling. Locked cards show "UNLOCKS AT N ★" + the stars you have. Watching the progression feel real.

By evening, the balance work kicked back in. Waves rewritten as floor formulas. x = 0.25 per region + 0.01 per level minus 1. Gating by wave: motes and swarms only on 1, shells on even waves, darts on odd waves past 3, colonies rare. The lever was mote HP growth. Started fresh cash at 250. Ember 7/11/16. Bloom buffed on range (user's call—range identity over DPS parity). Bloom went from 3% of builds to 12%. Also discovered: 20% of dailies are unwinnable at 150 cash. Every bot dies by wave 4. That's not balance. That's broken.

The reorder decision came late: Moss Valley first (easiest by definition), then Apricot Bay, Softwood, Rust Mesa. Re-picked all seeds. Ran the bot ladder via a new tool—parallel isolates, 20 maps per level, CSV output, matplotlib plots. Measured win rates, lives lost, wave reached. No more guessing. Just data and tweaks.

Shipping got tricky at 19:35. Signing broke halfway through (Apple ID token stale in Xcode so flutter build ipa failed missing-Xcode-Token). Pivoted to xcodebuild + exportArchive with -allowProvisioningUpdates + ASC API key path and issuer ID from config. Worked immediately. Uploaded via asc CLI, added to TestFlight tester groups, submitted for beta app review.

By 22:32, Softwood cliffs rose mid-level like rivers grow. Omen = dashed footprint a wave ahead, build-blocked; rise = 1.2s easeOutCubic slide+fade. Mouth clearance: rising cliffs keep a 1-cell ring clear around both ends of every route. Seeds re-picked. 108 tests green.

Shipped build 32 late, with the new balance (cash 250, Ember 7/11/16, Bloom range buff, four regions authored, journey layout, Field Guide, tower unlocking). The journey layout appearing like the ground shifting under the game. Field Guide appearing. Towers unlocking as the design solidified. All of it shipped by evening.

There's a thing you learn: you can't solve balance before humans touch it. You ship. They find breaks you didn't simulate.

But today—today I built the whole thing in front of people while it was still moving. Campaign UI while tests were running. Invader designs through six iterations. Campaign regions authored region by region. Journey layout appearing. Field Guide appearing. Towers unlocking as the design solidified. All of it shipped by evening.

The mad scientist was on fire. And for once, the fire didn't burn anything down.
