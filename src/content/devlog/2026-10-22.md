Started convinced the balance was close. It wasn't.

The perf patch landed (clamp helper) and round-2 tweaks landed. Ember at 5/8/13. Wave-mix genes for the evolved bot. The hybrid-seeded run was pulling 17+ waves where the old one barely hit 14. That felt like vindication.

Then I ran the full ladder and the edifice cracked.

The evolved bot was good at exactly one thing: Bloom + Lance in a specific ratio. The neutral bot went straight Ember and hit 20 waves. Even the hybrid start plateaued—CMA-ES stuck in a local optimum. Two days of compute and the answer was: I'd trained a bot that was excellent at one strategy and couldn't break out of it.

So I built a search tool. Beam search from scratch—what's the cheapest defense that survives wave 11? No evolution, no machine learning. Just: what does the search pick?

Needle + Ember.

Not Bloom. Not Frost. Not even the evolved hybrid.

I'd spent 18 hours tuning Bloom's reload and range. The search said it didn't matter.

There's a voice that says: you're not good at this. Someone smarter tries search first.

There's another that says: you didn't know that until you tried the other thing.

Both are true.

The search cracked the first 11 waves. But then I looked at the rest—the endless stretch past wave 11, the regional difficulty curves. The old formula x = 0.25 per region + 0.01 per level was making Apricot Bay and beyond unwinnable. Rewrote waves as x = 0.01 per level only, gating by wave: motes and swarms only on 1, shells on even, darts on odd past 3, colonies rare. Mote HP growth became the lever. Fixed a fractional multiplier that gave darts extra hits. Twenty percent of dailies were unwinnable at 150 starting cash. Bumped it to 250. New Ember cost: 250, dmg 7/11/16. Bloom range buffed per feedback—range identity over DPS parity.

By mid-afternoon the campaign UI needed fixing. The three muddy cards with blurry lenses read as broken—60% of the screen empty, weak lenses, pointless palette dots. The feedback was harsh enough that the whole thing got rebuilt.

At 16:25, pushed into a complete restyle. Hand-picked pastels per region: Moss Valley moss green, Apricot Bay sky blue #92d2ea, Softwood golden #FFDD94, Rust Mesa sand. Each card got a bigger lens rimmed in region colour with a soft white border on a light backdrop. Added a yellow Play tag bottom-right—flat design, white bold text—and dropped the pointless dots. The campaign page read cleaner immediately.

The fonts were wrong too. Nunito wouldn't bundle on Android. Helvetica the same. Picked TeX Gyre Heros (GUST licensed, already bundled) for all UI. Later added Chakra Petch Bold (Google Fonts) for the home wordmark and square buttons—something with personality. White on the Play yellow is technically 1.7:1 contrast, below spec. Kept it anyway because Mini Motorways does the same.

By 16:40, the lenses came next. Added the coastline from Apricot Bay's bay shape, showed actual water texture so locked regions didn't read as broken. Each region's postcard had character built into the crop itself—Softwood L6 showing the gentle flow, Apricot Bay showing the coast, Moss Valley showing both canyon walls. One simple rule: show the region's signature feature.

Then I looked at the home page and forgot about balance for a while.

Home was a logo and three muddy cards. At 16:59, killed the ARCO logo, drew a TeX Gyre Heros wordmark and painted a city grid—two wide white streets, a diagonal crossing an avenue—with a ground colour and faint grid. Put a ticker underneath that walks invaders down patrol routes at game pace. The city grid itself was a new painter (ground #E5EBEA, faint grid, white streets). The "Mini TD." wordmark in bold 84pt, full stop in Play yellow. Invaders walk 3 routes via a ticker at in-game speed.

By late afternoon the invader designs shifted six times.

First cuts: burned bells, spirilla, flocking specks, bead chains, krill, dividing cells. But iterations kept pulling. The creatures started to feel alive. Motes became tadpoles—kick and glide through invisible water. Swarms became water fleas, hopping and drifting. Tanks became mantas, squat and wide. Colonies split into rosettes of six darts instead of five motes, so the wave weight would come in sixths, feeding into the balance math. The colony burst mechanic changed too—now spawns six real darts (dart speed + health) instead of motes.

By 18:45, pushed the stroke-driven swimming as the keeper. Single-eyed swimmers, outline drawn with a travelling wave, one centred eye each. No eyespots, no internal detail. Just motion and intention. Slowed movement = weaker wave energy, which felt right. Shipped TestFlight 1.1.0 (build 14) then build 15 with new designs; measured +65% CPU / +16% GPU overhead on a busy wave. Noted it. Shipped anyway because the designs read better.

Built a Field Guide while momentum carried. Two tabs: invaders with Latin names and lovecraftian notes, and towers. Each tower card runs on a real board zoomed so you see it work. New tower selection: drag-to-build with a floating ghost, reach preview in tower colour, counters ticking up one-by-one. Renamed Lance to Lens—the charging effect drains light around the focal point while lilac motes accelerate in from the edges. That visual landed immediately.

But the greedy bot only built Needles. All 30+ of them. Every level. Every region. The unlocking system was meaningless.

Back to balance. But this time I didn't spiral. I wired tower unlocking: stars from campaign levels unlock new towers. Needle and Bloom free, Frost at 25 stars, Ember at 100. After a win's stars land, a dark toast drops from the top. "FROST UNLOCKED" or the next tower dimmed with progress. That juice matters.

By early evening the campaign structure came together region by region. Tried two approaches first—map cards showing a postcard of each region, or a journey line with regions as stations. Built both. After trying them live, the journey version won. Regions as stations alternating left/right down a white road, each a lens onto its postcard ringed by 25 ticks (region shade = cleared, gold = 3 stars). Road dashed in Play yellow up to the current region; station spacing fits the screen with a 170pt floor. Unlock at 10 clears to keep progression paced. Winding paths inside each region too—no straight grids. Per-region character baked in at the generator level via seeded sine curves (Moss Valley tight switchbacks, Apricot Bay long sweeps, Softwood gentle meanders).

Moss Valley carved first as a canyon. Two cliff walls with breathing width, 4–6 cells across, narrowing and opening like lungs. Apricot Bay got its tidal shape—a bay mouth opening to a coast, small rocks strewn for tower placement, headlands jutting from the sides. Softwood opened the campaign with golden ground and gentle sine meanders. Rust Mesa last: a full-width rock band placed over the first river so it tunnels underneath, 3–8 cells hidden—the region's whole identity is that constraint.

Each region got its own palette and character. Softwood golden ground, Apricot Bay cream, Moss Valley moss green, Rust Mesa sand. Built a milestone card at level 10 for each region. Once the clears landed, the card showed "Clear level 10 to unlock [Region Name]", with the region name in its cliff colour. That framing worked—promise, not done deal.

The balance work kicked in deep. Waves rewritten as floor formulas. x = 0.01 per level (per region made Apricot Bay unwinnable). Gating by wave: motes and swarms only on 1, shells on even waves, darts on odd waves past 3, colonies rare. The lever was mote HP growth. Started fresh cash at 250. Ember 7/11/16. Bloom range buffed per feedback—range identity over DPS parity. Bloom went from 3% of builds to 12%.

The reorder decision came late: Moss Valley first (easiest by definition), then Apricot Bay, Softwood, Rust Mesa. Re-picked all seeds. Ran the bot ladder via a new tool—parallel isolates, 20 maps per level, CSV output, matplotlib plots. Measured win rates, lives lost, wave reached. No more guessing. Just data and tweaks.

Shipping got tricky at 19:35. Signing broke halfway through (Apple ID token stale in Xcode so the build failed). Pivoted to xcodebuild + exportArchive with provisioning updates and ASC API key path. Worked immediately. Uploaded via asc CLI, added to TestFlight tester groups, submitted for beta app review.

Shipped build 32 late, with the new balance (cash 250, Ember 7/11/16, Bloom range buff, four regions authored, journey layout, Field Guide, tower unlocking).

There's a thing you learn: you can't solve balance before humans touch it. You ship. They find breaks you didn't simulate. But search found something evolution missed—you need to try the dumb way sometimes to know the smart way was wrong.

The mad scientist was on fire. And for once, the fire didn't burn anything down.
