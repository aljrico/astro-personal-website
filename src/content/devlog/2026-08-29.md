Committed a state machine today. Not a feature. A state machine. For deciding when to ask someone to rate the app.

This is the kind of thing that sounds like nothing and eats an afternoon. When do you ask? After the first lesson they actually finish, or the first eval — whichever lands first. Not before. Nobody rates an app they've used for ninety seconds, or if they do, it's a one-star "no me deja entrar." You ask when they've felt something.

Then the sentiment sheet. Do you like it or not. If they say yes, native review prompt. If they say no, we don't drag them to the store to complain — we just listen. And then the ads. This is the piece I kept going back and forth on. Ads only unlock after the sheet, and then only for three practice questions before the first interstitial. The exam simulator never enters the machine at all. Thirty questions, thirty minutes, and an ad in the middle? That's how you get the one-star review the whole machine exists to avoid.

Veterans skip all of it. If you've already sat through the ad intro, or we already asked you to rate it once, the sheet doesn't come back. You've been asked. That's enough.

I wrote it pure. No I/O, no Riverpod, just events in and states out. Fifteen tests. It felt clean. Then I did the thing I've started doing lately: threw it at two reviewers, one from each camp, high effort on both, and let them tear it up.

They did. Hydrate was silently swallowing unknown versions — meaning a future me could rename a state, ship it, and the old persisted data would just… vanish into a default with no complaint. A veteran who said they loved the app got their answer thrown away, because I'd wired the skip before the sentiment. The "sheet presented" event didn't check whether the sheet was actually due, so anything could fire it from anywhere. Sentiment could be replayed. An untyped Map lurking in there. Some docs pointing at nothing.

Six real issues in a thing I'd have shipped an hour earlier. Same thing every time: I read the code, it looks right, it *is* right for the path I was picturing. Then somebody walks a different path and the floor is missing.

Applied them all. Fifteen out of fifteen, analyze clean. Two commits' worth of work for maybe four hundred lines that most users will never consciously notice. That's the job, apparently.

The quiet thought, on the way out: this whole machine is a mechanism for asking strangers to say the app is good. I've built an elaborate, well-tested, dual-reviewed apparatus for fishing for approval. I'm aware of how that sounds.

Next is the AdManager. Every interstitial zeroed until the machine says go. Right now it's the one place where the app could still ambush someone mid-study, and I want that door closed before I let anyone else near it.
