Morning started with fonts. Swapped everything to TeX Gyre Heros (GUST license) — body, titles, menu buttons, locked it in everywhere except the logo (08f0083). Nunito was fine. Heros reads cleaner, lands harder. The decision came from comparing against Mini Motorways, which uses Helvetica, but can't bundle on Android. Bundled Chakra Petch Bold (Google Fonts OFL) for the wordmark and square buttons (7847864) — that sci-fi edge almost clashed with the soft organic swimmers, but kept it anyway. The techy feel ended up working.

Campaign cards came next. They were muddy — desaturated ground, grey backdrop fighting the lenses, pointless palette dots, weak everything. Tried Mini Motorways-style portrait orientation first; user preferred the original side-by-side layout. The direction unlocked it: bright hand-picked pastel colors per region. Moss Valley a soft moss green, Apricot Bay warm peachy sand. Bigger lens with soft white rim, lighter backdrop, dots gone (b37243e). Play tag added in flat gold (#F8C66A) with white text, bottom-right — white on that background sits at 1.7:1 contrast, below WCAG, but matched the MM reference and the user kept it (c915cb2). Found and fixed a bug where Apricot Bay's inlet was drawing as a straight bar across the river — the preview wasn't computing shore paths the way the game does. Fixed by computing Landscape shore/river in board coords.

Home page got rebuilt as a Mini Motorways-style city grid (ad73418). Grey ground, faint grid, two wide white static streets. New wordmark in Heros. Then the real thing: invaders walk three routes at in-game pace via a Ticker ValueNotifier. First cut had a ticker never accessed so nothing moved; caught that immediately. Extracted the invader drawing into shared code so home and game always match.

Invaders went through a bunch of iterations, and the vocabulary mattered more than I expected. Started with spirilla and amoebas (pseudopod reach, lumpy rim), then tried tapered rods and water fleas (eb6bfa3, 6bb5a4d). The language locked in around one family: mote (tadpole kick-glide), dart (water flea hop-drift), tank (manta ray), colony (rosette of six darts that burst on impact, not five motes anymore). Cleaner. Each had a character — the mote was a tiny drop, darts were elongated protozoa with a flagellum (cut per user), tanks were squat superellipses with 12 lobes. Rejected: bells, runs, amoebas, spirillum, krill, dividing cells, anything that felt too specific. The final roster worked because it read as one family across behaviors, not a taxonomy of random creatures.

Shipped TestFlight 1.1.0 early (builds 14, 15) to test the new roster before the big push. Built from clean git worktree with config/release.json to keep work-in-progress out. Uploaded via asc and added to testing groups, then submitted for beta app review.

Field Guide got a second district: Turrets tab (0aa6925). Each tower runs on a real board zoomed to a 2.6-cell window, with prey circling. Lance renamed to Lens. Dark rounded tile with a white-hot glass circle — nine lilac motes accelerate in from 1.5 cells while focusing. The charging effect was tricky. Tried a black-hole warp first; rejected as too aggressive. Switched to a light drain via fragment shader (75% desaturate, 22% dim) plus subtle heat shimmer. Tuning lesson: user asked to push drain harder, then walked every knob back to the original values. First instinct was right. Then added tower toast (f757915): after a win's stars land, a dark card drops from the top with "FROST UNLOCKED" or the next tower's progress. Small moment, huge juice.

Campaign journey became something real instead of a grid of samey squares (152a3e1, 611fe72). Built it as a winding road — each region a station, 25 stops, next region at the bottom. Per-region meander via seeded sine mix: Softwood gentle, Apricot Bay long sweeps, Moss Valley switchbacks, Rust Mesa's canyon walls creating the tightest curves. Maps built one per frame, next level prioritized so the visible region renders first. Rare hitch if campaign opens within 500ms of launch, invisible on device. Later simplified map building by moving it to an Isolate (663d0bd), which cut overhead from the main thread. The level-10 unlocks-next-region card was first phrased as "OPEN" — rewrote it as a quiet promise: "Clear level 10 to unlock Apricot Bay" with the level number and region name in contrasting shades. Road dashed in Play yellow up to the current region, filling as clears opened the next.

Parallel thread: canvas sandbox on a separate worktree for the in-game map redesign. Started with four browser concepts (Canal Town, Specimen, Survey Sheet, Lights Out), landed on "way more minimal". Canvas on home grey grid, black sea at the top, cream canal feeding into a white lake. Critters invisible in the sea, eyes fade in near shore. Tower picker as boxes. Reagent counter ticks up as critters die; homes counter jolts when lives are lost. Leak animation: critter collapses into its eye, eye arcs to the HUD. Shot critters: body bursts with spinning chunks and a white flash ring; shards fly in tower color. Small tweaks cascaded: "shake vs jump are different complaints" — ask which motion when a counter feels wrong. Reagent no longer jolts on gains, just counts up and flashes. Homes counter keeps the jolt: lost life is a physical hit, gain is calm.

Board repainted in promo icon colors — grass #CBE2B5, canal #F8F7EF cream, grid #D6E9C4. Lake same cream as canal. Wakes flipped to full ink via saveLayer. Reach light-up changed from white overlay to tower color tint. Shot critters now mirror the leak anim: collapse into eye, eye arcs to reagent counter. Bounty impact: burst and jolt and flash in Play yellow, softer than the red life-lost hit. River growth became real — three kinds (merge into side, fork from corner, independent river), each with its own telegraph (dashed course, eyes gathering at the mouth). Towers in the way sell at 100% refund (user's idea). Tried animated ground cracks and screen shake; user cut both, kept it calm.

Regional work branched in parallel. Moss Valley carved out of the canyon with organically-grown walls — sine meander, width breathing, erosion via Chaikin smoothing. Apricot Bay became a bay with hand-picked headlands — blocky cliffs jutting from sides — plus small rocks strewn over the land. Rust Mesa: one big boxy mesa placed over the first river so it tunnels 3–8 cells hidden. The hidden river is the region's difficulty. Campaign order locked: Moss Valley (easiest by definition), Apricot Bay, Softwood, Rust Mesa. All 100 levels seeded with unique palette, terrain rules, and difficulty curve per region. Stars filed by region and place, not campaign number, future-proofing for reordering and new level types.

Softwood got a mid-level twist. Cliffs now rise during the game, alternating with river growth — a dashed footprint appears a wave ahead, telegraphing the rise, then a 1.2s slide+fade brings the cliff in. Mouth clearance keeps a 1-cell ring clear around river ends so spawns never pop inside rock. The blurb: "Cliffs rise from the groves. Read their shadows."

Tower earning system came next. Towers unlocked by campaign stars: Needle and Bloom free, Frost 25★, Ember 50★, Lance 100★. Dailies use the player's own unlocks — leaderboard should push unlocking. Team rejected per-critter tier rings (too noisy), kept tank bar flattened to 1px corners with a faint track. Each turret in the Field Guide shows unlock progress when locked.

Balance work hit a wall and then a breakthrough. The greedy bot was stuck building only needles — thirty to thirty-six everywhere, winning everything. The insight hit hard: needle DPS per cost dominates. Ditched the learned weights and ran a cheap beam search instead (tool/search.dart) — find the minimal viable defence across waves 1 to 11, no genes, no evolution. Result: Needle plus Ember, nothing fancy. Bloom 0%, Frost 0%, Lance 0%. Just two towers.

That unblocked everything. Starting cash 250. Needle reload/dmg locked. Ember 250 cost, dmg 7/11/16. Bloom 250, reload 2/1.5/1, dmg 3/6/12, range 3/4/4.5. User's call: buff Bloom's range identity, not DPS parity. Range buff alone moved Bloom from 3% to 12% of bot spending. Damage buff alone did nothing. The insight: specialist towers matter when they solve what others can't, not when they out-DPS the generalist.

Waves rewritten as floor formulas. Difficulty starts at 0.01·(level−1), climbs per region. Bounty = floor(5+2√hp). Hits-to-kill matters more than raw HP. A fractional multiplier had quietly given darts extra hits; floors fixed it. Terrain alone ramps difficulty across regions — that was the breakthrough insight that cut the knot. Multiple balance rounds followed. Each one tightened the knob. Final wave spec locked: Frost .2/.3/.5 range 1.5/1.75/2 with stacking chill; Bloom 3/6/12; Ember 7/11/16; Needle 1/1.15/1.3. Then Frost got a rework — every foam hit stacks a 0.8 chill (same tower stacks too), chilled bodies become "brittle" and take extra damage equal to the chill (20%..80%), range now 2/2.5/3. And Bloom levels started paying x1.5 bounty since they were starved — kills weren't generating enough income against the tanky waves.

Parallel perf work crept in. Earlier measurements had blamed the sim; actual profile showed map generation at 81–85% of headless time. Grid-lookup walk in RoadMap._constrainedWalk was the bottleneck. Moved expensive Session operations to an Isolate, pooled worker isolates, memoized shared boards. Moss Valley ladder dropped from 35 seconds per generation to 9 seconds. No correctness regressions — 660 board fingerprints stayed identical. A single-line clamp helper yielded 1.07–1.08x wall-clock gain on JIT, measured across 536 game states.

Dailies added a twist: three level types to drive tower variety. Bloom dailies get fast super-tanks from wave 6, double tanky shells, long rivers (pushing spend toward Bloom more than the single-tower meta). Random-region dailies mean every day pulls from Moss Valley, Apricot Bay, Softwood, or Rust Mesa equally — 7% of Rust Mesa dailies had no mesa on short/winding rivers, so added fallback shapes, trying 24 smaller mesas after full-size fails.

Signed build 32 with a signing gotcha: Xcode's Apple ID token was dead. Just fails with "No Accounts". Tried the workaround — used an authentication key instead via asc, worked but friction we shouldn't carry. Root fix: remove and re-add the Apple ID in Xcode settings, token refreshes. Someone will hit this again.

Late night, one more thing surfaced: tower picker menu was broken when only two towers were earned. Built-tower menu reused shop boxes assuming ≥3 towers; with only Needle+Bloom, upgrade+sell shared a box, so tapping upgrade SOLD the tower. Broke when the new player tower earning system launched. Fixed it properly (1329637): PickerBoxes(towers) + MenuBox roles, 26 widget tests across phone/tablet widths. Built with a subagent at xhigh effort since the test matrix was thorny.

Rounds 3–4 of balance followed. Each round a small tweak and measurement. Upgrade formula: first upgrade costs the tower's build price, second costs 2x. Frost went to 250, Ember to 300. Every region played identically except one: Bloom level type (one third of dailies) with its long rivers and double-tanky shells pushed the spending curve. Beam search kept finding Needle + Ember as the minimal defence across the first 11 waves, so other towers only matter when a specific level type forces them. The language of the discovery: specialists matter when they solve what others can't.

Runs with beam search at every cash step + every wave change found Bloom's range buff alone drove adoption from 3% to 12%. Damage buff? Nothing. This became the rule: change one knob at a time, measure, and trust the first instinct more than the iteration.

Build progression stacked up: 14, 15, 17, 19, 20, 21, 23, 25, 26, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34. Each one a reason — palette, unlock gate, winding road, analytics wired, regional rework, balance round, UI simplification pass, towers earned, Field Guide turrets, journey layout, rounds 1-2, simplify pass, then 31 with Bay headlands and random-region dailies, then 32 with the final balance, 33 with Bloom level type and the tower picker fix, and 34 with Frost rework and Bloom bounty buff.

Good momentum. The insight — that terrain ramps difficulty, wave formula drives everything else, specialists matter by solving what generalists can't — unlocked the whole balance direction. Softwood cliffs rise mid-level. Apricot Bay gets headlands and tunneling rivers. Rust Mesa hides its rivers. Moss Valley's walls do the work. Then currency system, then Ember unlock. The pattern is clear now: each region has its own identity via terrain, towers unlock via earned currency, dailies randomly sample regions and level types. Rest can wait.

But there's a weight to this: I've been shipping builds all day and the engine works. No crashes, no regressions, the bots converge. It *feels* right. And that's when you know you should stop and let it breathe — let the user play it, let the randomness of real sessions reveal what the numbers didn't. The spreadsheets said Needle+Ember beats everything; now I want to see what a real player builds. The analysis is done. Time to move.
