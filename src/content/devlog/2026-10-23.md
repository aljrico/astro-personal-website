CMA-ES bot was stuck in a local optimum. So we ditched learned weights entirely and ran a cheap beam search instead—find the minimal viable defense across waves 1 to 11, no genes, no evolution. Result: Needle + Ember, nothing fancy. Boring answer, but clean. It means everything else actually matters instead of being noise around the dominant strategy.

That unblocked the balance work we'd been holding. Campaign redesign landed—regions as stations along a journey line, each one a postcard lens into the board. Softwood first, then Apricot Bay with its shoreline, Moss Valley with its canyon walls, Rust Mesa with tunnels. Twenty-five levels per region, hand-authored. Invaders got redrawn as soft swimmers—tadpoles, water fleas, mantas, colonies that split into darts. The Field Guide got a Turrets tab showing each tower on a real board, magnified in a 2.6-cell window.

Multiple TestFlight builds shipped as each piece landed. 14, 15, 17, 19-26, 29, 30, 31, 32. Each one was a reason—palette, unlock gate, winding road, analytics wired. The notes are full of small iterations. Half-cell erosion on canyon walls. Coral headlands for the bay. Shader warps for the Lens tower, then reverts when they felt wrong. Reagent counters that tick instead of jump. A warning line on the canal when rivers start to grow.

The visual polish started mid-afternoon. Swapped the fonts—TeX Gyre Heros for everything body-level (was Nunito), Chakra Petch Bold for titles and buttons. Campaign cards got bright hand-picked pastels per region, bigger lenses with soft white rims, light backdrop. Old Mini Motorways reference had been muddy and desaturated. The new versions read instantly. User loved the colors live.

Then came the campaign itself. Tried two directions: map cards with blurry lenses, or a journey line with regions as stations. User picked the journey after testing both. Regions alternate left-right down a white road, each lens rimmed by a ring of 25 ticks (cleared levels in region shade, 3-stars in gold). Road dashes in Play yellow, filling toward the next region as clears unlock it. Postcard crops locked in: Softwood at L6, Apricot Bay at L26 showing the coast, Moss Valley at L52 showing both walls. Level 10 in each region unlocks the next with a quiet card promise: "Clear level 10 to unlock [Region]"—no false sense of progress.

Inside each region, levels lay out as a winding road. Not a grid. Per-region meander via seeded sine waves—Softwood gentle, Apricot Bay long sweeps, Moss Valley switchbacks. Catmull-Rom curves, 25 stops. Stops snap to a grid so maps render fast, but the eye reads flow. Locked levels greyed and blurred. Maps build on a background isolate; nearest-next-level prioritized so the visible region always ready first. First paint shows bare ground until the isolate finishes, then AnimatedSwitcher fades the map in. Rare hitch on launch if a player opens Campaign within 500ms of app start (map gen is ~40ms per level in debug), but invisible on device.

The bot work ran parallel. Beam search told us Needle + Ember. But evolved bots didn't match hand-tuned bots, and evolved bots stuck in local optima. Rounds of genes. Pools and isolates. Clamping helpers. Bit-identical fingerprints across 536 validation games. Perf debates. The user rejected certain balance changes—nerfing Bloom, for example—so we iterated instead. The real move came down to one insight: Bloom's range buff instead of DPS. Range alone moved it from 3% to 12% of bot spending, whereas damage tweaks alone did nothing. Small lever, right place. User's instinct was right.

Mid-evening, the sandbox opened. Canvas map redesign for an in-game mode (canal survival)—a separate game board where rivers flow into a lake, towers build along the canal, and critters leak if they touch the water. Mockup in a worktree; whole new world building. Black sea at the top, cream canal feeding into a white lake. Critters invisible in the sea, eyes fade in near shore. Tower picker as a row of boxes (name, icon, price, reach preview). Reagent counter counts up as critters die; homes counter jolts when lives are lost. Splash effects tinted by the water they land on. Warning line in red dashed across the canal—critters past it blink red but still targetable. Drag-to-build ghost floats above the thumb, snaps to grid, watermelon red if invalid. Multiple iterations on the visual juice: leak collapse (body swells once then implodes with a twist, inside a red radial glow that gathers onto the eye), kill bounty (body bursts with 7 spinning chunks, white flash ring, shards fly in tower color).

Deployed multiple TestFlight builds tracking each visual and gameplay piece. Build 17 playtested "Survival (new)" too hard—critters were moving 1.3-1.9x faster per cell because routes were twice as long as the old 7×12 survival. Clamped the speed multiplier, drew critters 1.25x bigger. Generator improved: river placement via DFS guided by BFS distance field, weighted pick. Forks group routes per house; critters pick a way at spawn. Sunk towers refunded 100%. Board fills after 2-4 growths under no-adjacency; balance curves smoothed.

Waves rewritten as floor formulas. Difficulty starts at 0 and ramps with region (Softwood hardest, Moss easiest by definition so it opens first). Gating: motes and swarms on wave 1; darts on odd waves ≥3; colonies on waves 9/10; tanks on even waves, none on first level. The finding that stuck: hits-to-kill matters more than raw HP. Bounty is the real tuning lever. Floor formula: bounty = floor(5 + 2√hp). That gives the defence enough currency to spend meaningfully.

Rounds of balance. Starting cash 250, Ember 250 cost (dmg 7/11/16), Bloom 250 with a range buff (3/4/4.5 cells, identity preserved). Needle dmg 1/1.15/1.3; Frost slow and chill stacks. Lens (renamed from Lance) dark rounded tile with a white-hot glass circle, 9 lilac motes accelerate in during charge. Build 21, build 23, builds climbing toward finalization.

Pushed a /simplify pass on the UI work. Extracted shared components, pooled the isolate workers, cached postcard scenes across tab switches. Verified byte-identical fingerprints over 100 campaign maps + dailies. Another pass on region placement: cliff forms moved into an enum (block, wall, butte, mesa) so growth rules could read them. Apricot Bay headlands rose from user's call (cliffs jut 3-4 columns from a side, placed before river grows, pairs stack same side with a gap or face each other). Rust Mesa tunnels (river hidden under a mesa 3-8 cells) became the region's whole difficulty—no other twist needed.

Regions reordered: Moss Valley first (easiest by design), then Apricot Bay, Softwood, Rust Mesa. Daily challenges now pick a random region each day. Stars earned the towers (Needle + Bloom free, Frost 25★, Ember 50★, Lens 100★). Tests passed 100 levels + 6 dailies with bit-identical determinism.

Pushed Build 32 to TestFlight, thought we were done. Then tried to upload and Xcode's stored Apple ID token died. Just fails with "No Accounts". There's a command-line workaround (ASC API key), but it's friction we shouldn't carry. It's a known problem—token expires, needs re-adding in Xcode settings. Someone will hit this again.

Good momentum on the UI and tuning work itself. Build shipped clean. Infrastructure headaches at the finish line, usual story.
