Morning started with fonts. Swapped everything to TeX Gyre Heros (GUST license) — body, titles, menu buttons, locked it in everywhere except the logo. Nunito was fine. Heros reads cleaner, lands harder. Bundled Chakra Petch Bold (Google Fonts OFL) for the wordmark and square buttons. That sci-fi edge almost clashed with the soft organic swimmers, but kept it anyway. The techy feel ended up working.

Campaign cards came next. They were muddy — desaturated ground, grey backdrop fighting the lenses, pointless palette dots, weak everything. The direction unlocked it: journey instead of a static map preview. Hand-picked pastel colors per region: Moss Valley a soft moss green, Apricot Bay warm peachy sand. Bigger lens with soft white rim, new lighter backdrop, dots gone. Play tag added in flat gold with white text, bottom-right. Also found and fixed a bug where Apricot Bay's inlet was drawing as a straight bar across the river — the preview wasn't computing shore paths the way the game does.

Home page got rebuilt as a Mini Motorways-style city grid. Grey ground, faint grid, two wide white static streets. New wordmark in Heros. Then the real thing: invaders walk three routes at in-game pace via a Ticker. First cut had a ticker never accessed so nothing moved; caught that immediately. Extracted the invader drawing into shared code so home and game always match.

Invaders went through a bunch of iterations before locking in. Tried spirillum, bells, runs, amoebas with pseudopods, swarms with corkscrew breathing, clumps of dividing cells. They all missed something. The vocabulary mattered. Final roster: mote (tadpole kick-glide), dart (water flea hop-drift), tank (manta ray), colony (rosette of six darts that burst on impact, not five motes anymore). One family. Cleaner. Shipped TestFlight 1.1.0 early (builds 14, 15) to test the new roster before the big push.

Field Guide got a second district: Turrets tab. Each tower runs on a real board zoomed to a 2.6-cell window, with prey circling. Lance renamed to Lens. Dark rounded tile with a white-hot glass circle — nine lilac motes accelerate in from 1.5 cells while focusing. Charging effect was tricky. Tried a black-hole warp first; rejected as too aggressive. Switched to a light drain via fragment shader (75% desaturate, 22% dim) plus subtle heat shimmer. Pushed for harder; walked every knob back to the original. First instinct was right. Then added tower toast: after a win's stars land, a dark card drops from the top with "FROST UNLOCKED" or the next tower's progress. Small moment, huge juice.

Afternoon: the region selector became something real instead of a grid of samey squares. Built it as a winding road — each region a station, 25 stops, next region at the bottom. Per-region meander via seeded sine mix: Softwood gentle, Apricot Bay long sweeps, Moss Valley switchbacks, Rust Mesa's canyon walls creating the tightest curves. Maps built one per frame, next level prioritized so the visible region renders first. Rare hitch if campaign opens within 500ms of launch, invisible on device. Later simplified map building by moving it to an Isolate, which cut overhead from the main thread.

The campaign became a journey. User asked for a real side-by-side, so I mocked two directions. The winding-road version won. Regions as stations alternating left/right down a white road, each a lens onto its postcard ringed by 25 ticks — region shade means cleared, gold means 3 stars. Road dashed in Play yellow up to the current region, filled to min(clears, 10)/10 as levels unlock. Journey card wording became a quiet promise: "Clear level 10 to unlock [Region]" — level number bold, region name in that region's dark cliff color. User said "astonishingly good" when it landed.

Parallel thread: canvas sandbox on a separate worktree for the in-game map redesign. Started with four browser concepts, landed on "way more minimal." Canvas on home grey grid, black sea at the top, cream canal feeding into a white lake. Critters invisible in the sea, eyes fade in near shore. Tower picker as boxes. Reagent counter ticks up as critters die; homes counter jolts when lives are lost. Leak animation: critter collapses into its eye, eye arcs to the HUD. Shot critters: body bursts with spinning chunks and a white flash ring; shards fly in tower color. Small tweaks cascaded: "shake vs jump are different complaints" — ask which motion when a counter feels wrong. Reagent no longer jolts on gains, just counts up and flashes. Homes counter keeps the jolt: lost life is a physical hit, gain is calm.

Board repainted in promo icon colors — grass #CBE2B5, canal #F8F7EF cream, grid #D6E9C4. Lake same cream as canal, no fade. Wakes flipped to full ink via saveLayer. Reach light-up changed from white overlay to tower color tint. Shot critters now mirror the leak anim: collapse into eye, eye arcs to reagent counter. Bounty impact: burst and jolt and flash in Play yellow, softer than the red life-lost hit.

Regional work branched in parallel. Moss Valley carved out of the canyon with organically-grown walls (sine meander, width breathing, erosion via Chaikin smoothing). Apricot Bay became a bay with hand-picked headlands (blocky cliffs jutting from sides) plus small rocks strewn over the land. Rust Mesa: one big boxy mesa placed over the first river so it tunnels 3–8 cells hidden — the hidden river is the region's difficulty. By 18:09, the campaign order locked: Moss Valley (easiest by definition), Apricot Bay, Softwood, Rust Mesa. All 100 levels seeded with unique palette, terrain rules, and difficulty curve per region. Each region got its own balance tuning: Moss steeper growth, Apricot Bay smaller cliffs with tunneling, Rust Mesa's hidden passages.

Tower earning system came next. Towers unlocked by campaign stars: Needle and Bloom free, Frost 25★, Ember 50★, Lance 100★. Dailies use the player's own unlocks — leaderboard should push unlocking. Team rejected per-critter tier rings (too noisy), kept tank bar flattened to 1px corners with a faint track.

Balance work hit a wall and then a breakthrough. The greedy bot was stuck building only needles — thirty to thirty-six everywhere, winning everything. The insight hit hard: needle DPS per cost dominates. Ditched the learned weights and ran a cheap beam search instead — find the minimal viable defense across waves 1 to 11, no genes, no evolution. Result: Needle plus Ember, nothing fancy. Bloom 0%, Frost 0%, Lance 0%. Just two towers.

That unblocked everything. Starting cash 250. Needle reload/dmg locked. Ember 250 cost, dmg 7/11/16. Bloom 250, reload 2/1.5/1, dmg 3/6/12, range 3/4/4.5. User's call: buff Bloom's range identity, not DPS parity. Range buff alone moved Bloom from 3% to 12% of bot spending. Damage buff alone did nothing. The insight: specialist towers matter when they solve what others can't, not when they out-DPS the generalist.

Waves rewritten as floor formulas. Difficulty starts at 0.01·(level−1), climbs per region. Bounty = floor(5+2√hp). Hits-to-kill matters more than raw HP. A fractional multiplier had quietly given darts extra hits; floors fixed it. Multiple balance rounds followed. Each one tightened the knob. Final wave spec locked: Frost .2/.3/.5 range 1.5/1.75/2 with stacking chill; Bloom 3/6/12; Ember 7/11/16; Needle 1/1.15/1.3.

Parallel perf work crept in. Codex measured map generation at 81–85% of headless time (not the sim — the earlier guess was wrong). Grid-lookup walk in RoadMap._constrainedWalk was the bottleneck. Moved expensive Session operations to an Isolate, pooled worker isolates, memoized shared boards. Moss Valley ladder dropped from 35 seconds per generation to 9 seconds. No correctness regressions — 660 board fingerprints stayed identical.

Then came bot evolution. Hand-rolled CMA-ES trainer on survival dailies. First run got stuck — evolved bot hit a local optima where it builds 100% Ember (overtuned). Tried fresh training, hybrid seeding, random-tower "casual" bot. Nothing unlocked things faster than the beam-search insight: two towers beat four towers every time. The language matters. Rust maps are 2x slower than Softwood to generate, but a language worth keeping.

Six dailies passed bit-identical fingerprints across balance iterations. TestFlight builds stacked up: 14, 15, 17, 19, 20, 21, 23, 25, 26, 29, 30, 31, 32. Each one a reason — palette, unlock gate, winding road, analytics wired, regional rework, balance round, UI simplification pass, towers earned, Field Guide turrets, journey layout, build 29, simplify pass, then 31 with Bay headlands and random-region dailies, then 32 with the final balance (cash 250, Ember/Bloom 250, Bloom range buff).

Signed build 32 around 19:35. Xcode's Apple ID token was dead. Just fails with "No Accounts". Tried the workaround — used an authentication key instead, worked but friction we shouldn't carry. Root fix: remove and re-add the Apple ID in Xcode settings, token refreshes. Someone will hit this again.

Late night, one more thing surfaced: tower picker menu was broken when only two towers were earned. Built-tower menu reused shop boxes assuming ≥3 towers; with only Needle+Bloom, upgrade+sell shared a box, so tapping upgrade SOLD the tower. Broke when the new player tower earning system launched. Fixed it properly: PickerBoxes(towers) + MenuBox roles, 26 widget tests across phone/tablet widths. Committed early morning (08:12) before wrapping.

Good momentum on the UI and tuning. Build shipped clean. Infrastructure pain at the finish line, usual story. The beam search insight unlocked the whole balance question — minimal towers, maximum elegance. Softwood rework pending (cliffs that rise mid-level like rivers grow). Then currency system, then Ember unlock. The pattern is clear now: terrain alone ramps difficulty across regions; wave formula drives everything else. Rest can wait.
