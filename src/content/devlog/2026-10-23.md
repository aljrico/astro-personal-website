Morning started with fonts. Swapped everything to TeX Gyre Heros (GUST license) — body, titles, menu buttons, locked it in everywhere except the logo and a few legacy spots. Nunito was fine. Heros reads cleaner, lands harder. Later bundled Chakra Petch Bold (Google Fonts OFL) for the wordmark and square buttons (Campaign/Survival/Resume, Continue on results). That sci-fi edge almost clashed with the soft organic swimmers, but kept it anyway. The techy feel ended up working.

Campaign cards came next. They were muddy — desaturated ground, grey backdrop fighting the lenses, pointless palette dots, weak everything. User's direction unlocked it: journey instead of a static map preview. That ripple downstreamed into everything. Hand-picked pastel colors per region: Moss Valley a soft moss green, Apricot Bay warm peachy sand. Bigger lens with soft white rim, new lighter backdrop, dots gone. Play tag added in flat gold with white text, bottom-right. Also fixed a bug where Apricot Bay's inlet was drawing as a straight bar across the river — the preview wasn't computing shore paths the way the game does them.

Home page got rebuilt too. Dropped the wordmark mockup, dropped the art demo, built a Mini Motorways-style city grid instead — grey ground, faint grid, two wide white static streets. Wordmark in the new font. Then the real touch: invaders walk three routes at in-game pace via a Ticker. First cut had a ticker never accessed so nothing moved; caught that immediately. Extracted the invader drawing into shared code so home and game always match the same look.

Invaders went through a bunch of iterations before locking in. Started with swimming outlines (travelling wave plus surge, one centered eye). Tried alternatives — spirillum, bells, runs, amoebas with pseudopods, swarms with corkscrew breathing — but they all missed something. The vocabulary mattered. Final roster: mote (tadpole), dart (water flea), tank (manta), colony (six darts). One family. Cleaner. Shipped TestFlight 1.1.0 early (builds 14, 15) to test the new roster before the big push.

Field Guide got a second district: Turrets tab. Each tower runs on a real board zoomed to a 2.6-cell window, with prey circling. Lance renamed to Lens. Dark rounded tile with a white-hot glass circle — nine lilac motes accelerate in from 1.5 cells while focusing. Charging effect was tricky. Tried a black-hole warp first; rejected as too aggressive. Switched to a light drain via fragment shader (75% desaturate, 22% dim) plus subtle heat shimmer. User wanted harder; walked every knob back to the original. First instinct was right. Then added tower toast: after a win's stars land, a dark card drops from the top with "FROST UNLOCKED" or the next tower's progress. Small moment, huge juice.

Afternoon: the region selector got rebuilt as a winding road. Each region a station, 25 stops, next region at the bottom. Per-region meander via seeded sine mix — Softwood gentle, Apricot Bay long sweeps, Moss Valley switchbacks. Locked levels greyed and blurred. Maps built one per frame, next level prioritized so the visible region renders first. Rare hitch if campaign opens within 500ms of launch, invisible on device. Shipped as build 29. Simplified the map building later by moving it to an Isolate, which cut overhead from the main thread.

The campaign became a journey. User asked for a real side-by-side. Regions became stations alternating left/right down a white road, each a lens onto its postcard ringed by 25 ticks (region shade = cleared, gold = 3 stars). Road dashed in Play yellow up to the current region. Player fills the road between regions to min(clears, 10)/10 as levels unlock. Journey card wording shifted from "OPEN" to a quiet promise: "Clear level 10 to unlock [Region]" — level number bold, region name in that region's dark cliff color.

Parallel thread: canvas sandbox on a separate worktree (sandbox/canal) for the in-game map redesign. Started with four browser concepts, landed on "way more minimal." Canvas on home grey grid. Black sea at the top, cream canal feeding into a white lake. Critters invisible in the sea, eyes fade in near shore. Tower picker as boxes. Reagent counter ticks up as critters die; homes counter jolts when lives are lost. Leak animation: critter collapses into its eye, eye arcs to the HUD. Shot critters: body bursts with spinning chunks and a white flash ring; shards fly in tower color. User said "shake vs jump are different" — asked which motion when a counter felt off. Jolt on reagent gains got cut. Homes counter keeps the spring.

Board repainted mid-afternoon in promo icon colors — grass #CBE2B5, canal #F8F7EF cream, grid #D6E9C4. User: "astonishingly good." Lake same cream as canal, no fade. Wakes flipped to full ink via saveLayer. Reach light-up changed from white overlay to tower color tint. Shot critters now mirror the leak anim: collapse into eye, eye arcs to reagent counter. Bounty impact: burst and jolt and flash in Play yellow, softer than the red life-lost hit.

Regional work branched in parallel. Moss Valley and others carved onto the canvas board with hand-picked palettes per region. Apricot Bay: user proposed ground #EED3B8, grid #F1DCC6 (sampled from Mini Motorways reference). Moss Valley: a soft moss ground, canyon carved via seeded sine meander. Rust Mesa: organically grown mesa placed over the first river so it tunnels 3–8 cells hidden. By 18:09, the campaign order locked: Moss Valley (easiest by definition), Apricot Bay, Softwood, Rust Mesa. All 100 levels seeded with unique palette, terrain rules, and difficulty curve per region.

Tower earning system came next. Towers unlocked by campaign stars: Needle and Bloom free, Frost 25★, Ember 50★, Lance 100★. Dailies use the player's own unlocks. Team rejected per-critter tier rings (too noisy), kept tank bar flattened to 1px corners with a faint track.

Balance work hit a wall and then a breakthrough. The greedy bot was stuck building only needles — thirty to thirty-six everywhere, winning everything. The insight hit hard: needle DPS per cost dominates. So I ditched the learned weights and ran a cheap beam search instead — find the minimal viable defense across waves 1 to 11, no genes, no evolution. Result: Needle plus Ember, nothing fancy. Bloom 0%, Frost 0%, Lance 0%. Just two towers.

That unblocked everything. Starting cash 250. Needle reload/dmg locked. Ember 250 cost, dmg 7/11/16. Bloom 250, reload 2/1.5/1, dmg 3/6/12, range 3/4/4.5. User's call: buff Bloom's range identity, not DPS parity. Range buff alone moved Bloom from 3% to 12% of bot spending. Damage buff alone did nothing. The insight: specialist towers matter when they solve what others can't, not when they out-DPS the generalist.

Waves rewritten as floor formulas. Difficulty starts at 0.01·(level−1), climbs per region. Bounty = floor(5+2√hp). Hits-to-kill matters more than raw HP. A fractional multiplier had quietly given darts extra hits; floors fixed it. Multiple balance rounds followed. Each one tightened the knob. Final wave spec locked: Frost .2/.3/.5 range 1.5/1.75/2 with stacking chill; Bloom 3/6/12; Ember 7/11/16; Needle 1/1.15/1.3.

Six dailies passed bit-identical fingerprints across balance iterations. TestFlight builds stacked up: 14, 15, 17, 19, 20, 21, 23, 25, 26, 29, 30, 31, 32. Each one a reason — palette, unlock gate, winding road, analytics wired, regional rework, balance round, UI simplification pass, towers earned, Field Guide turrets.

Signed build 32. Xcode's Apple ID token was dead. Just fails with "No Accounts". Tried the workaround — used an authentication key instead, worked but it's friction we shouldn't carry. Root fix: remove and re-add the Apple ID in Xcode settings, token refreshes. Someone will hit this again.

Good momentum on the UI and tuning. Build shipped clean. Infrastructure pain at the finish line, usual story. The beam search insight unlocked the whole balance question — minimal towers, maximum elegance. Tomorrow: verify the balance holds on device, then think about what's next. Maybe the currency system for Ember unlock, maybe the regional tweaks. Or maybe just rest.
