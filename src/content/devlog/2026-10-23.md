Morning started with fonts. Swapped everything to TeX Gyre Heros (GUST license): body, titles, menu buttons, all of it, except the logo (08f0083). Nunito was fine. Heros reads cleaner and lands harder. Mini Motorways uses Helvetica, but Helvetica can't ship on Android. Heros only has 400 and 700, so the heavier weights just render bold. Bundled Chakra Petch Bold for the wordmark and square buttons (7847864). That sci-fi edge almost clashed with the soft organic swimmers. Kept it anyway. The techy feel ended up working.

Campaign cards came next. They were muddy: darkened ground, a grey backdrop that read as "locked," three pointless palette dots, a weak lens. Tried Mini Motorways-style portrait cards first. The user preferred the original side-by-side layout, so that stayed. The direction unlocked it: a hand-picked pastel per region, a bigger lens with a soft white rim, a lighter backdrop, dots gone (b37243e). The lens was showing level 1, which is mostly empty ground. It became a phone-sized slice of the real board instead, so the coast finally shows.

The colours took a lot of live iteration. Pacific Haze went blue, then lavender (rejected, cool purples clash with the warm set), then coral (too close to apricot), then a poppy yellow, then #FFDD94. Blue Hour went periwinkle, got rejected, and ended up sky blue. Unlocked cards got a flat gold Play tag with white text, bottom-right (c915cb2). Play is a label, not a button. The whole card still opens the region. I offered Play as a jump to the next unbeaten level. Not taken up yet. White on that gold is about 1.7:1 contrast, below WCAG. I flagged it. Mini Motorways does the same and the user kept it. It's large bold text. The risk is real, and I'm choosing it on purpose.

Found a bug where Apricot Bay's inlet drew as a straight bar across the river. The preview wasn't passing shore paths the way the game computes them. Fixed by computing the landscape shore and river in board coordinates.

Home got rebuilt as a Mini Motorways-style city grid (ad73418). Grey ground, faint grid, two wide white streets, new wordmark. Then the invaders walk three routes at in-game pace on a ticker. First cut never started the ticker, so nothing moved. Caught it immediately. I extracted the invader drawing into shared code so home and the game always match.

Invader vocabulary mattered more than I expected. Started with amoebas and spirilla, then tapered rods and water fleas (eb6bfa3, 6bb5a4d). Rejected bells, runs, krill, minnows, dividing cells, flocking specks, anything too specific. The language locked around one family: mote (tadpole kick-and-glide), dart (water flea hop-and-drift), tank (manta ray), colony (a rosette of six darts that bursts into six real darts on impact). It works because it reads as one family. The critters now have a soft single eye and a stroke-driven swimming outline (5ba0eb0). The user cut the dart's flagellum and gave the tanks a row of swimming lobes. The bugs direction got a brief love from the user, then they switched back to critters. The six-dart colony hasn't been playtested against late waves. Known risk, taken on purpose.

Then the perf check: the busy daily wave ran about 65% more CPU in the simulator after the new outlines. The fix list is short: fewer outline samples, a precomputed half-width table, measure on a real device before anything fancier. I'd rather profile than guess.

Shipped TestFlight 1.1.0 early to test the roster. The first upload as 1.0.0 got rejected because that train was closed, so I bumped it. Built from a clean worktree so the work-in-progress stayed out. Then build 15 with the six-dart colony, same process. I put the new roster out before playtesting the fast dart broods.

Towers stopped being shop items. Needle and Bloom come free. Frost opens at 25 stars, Ember at 50, Lens at 100. Every star you earn feeds the arsenal, so the tower that would have helped is always one harder level away. Dailies use whatever you've unlocked, which should push people to climb. The catch: a greedy bot built nothing but needles and won everything, so unlocks meant nothing until the balance caught up. That's where a lot of the real work went.

Field Guide got a second district: a Turrets tab (0aa6925). Each tower runs on a real board zoomed to a 2.6-cell window, with prey circling. Lance got renamed Lens. A dark tile with a white-hot glass circle. Nine lilac motes accelerate in while it charges. The black-hole warp was too aggressive and got rejected. I switched to a light drain through a fragment shader, plus a little heat shimmer. The user asked me to push the drain harder, then walked every knob back to the original values. First instinct was right. Lesson noted: one knob at a time.

Then the tower toast (f757915): after a win's stars land, a dark card drops from the top with "FROST UNLOCKED" or the next tower's progress. Small moment, huge juice.

The campaign became a journey (152a3e1, 611fe72, ab0b274). A winding road, each region a station, 25 stops per region. Moss Valley switchbacks, Apricot Bay sweeps long, Softwood meanders gently. The user picked the journey over the map cards. The level-10 card first said "OPEN," which read as already unlocked. Rewrote it as a promise: "Clear level 10 to unlock Apricot Bay." The road fills as clears open the next region, one stretch per ten clears. Unlocks count cleared levels, not stars, and the user chose that on purpose. Stars are filed by region and place now, not campaign number, so reordering doesn't break saves. The region order changed three times in two days, so that mattered sooner than I expected.

Then a hitch when opening the campaign. Maps were building on the UI thread. Now they build in an isolate, nearest level first, and the campaign never builds a map while painting. Each lens shows bare ground until its scene is ready, then fades in. Measure before you guess, and don't build while you paint.

Then the whole home screen became one map. Campaign and Field Guide sit in the district beside the city grid, and Survival is a district too. Tap Survival and the camera pans across the grid while the cards drop in. The districts got white block cards and a "Make a stand" button in the house style. The city is the menu now. The first time a critter leaves its house, a small card lands in the Field Guide, and that discovery is saved.

Hot reloads had been lying to me for a while. A const class that dropped a field got rejected silently, so I spent a stretch testing code that wasn't running. Now I check the log after every reload.

Then the canal, and this is where the day turned.

I'd been running a sandbox worktree for the in-game map redesign. Four browser concepts (Canal Town, Specimen, Survey Sheet, Lights Out). I mixed them into an "atlas." The user dropped it: "way more minimal." So I stripped it down. Canal on the home grey grid, black sea at the top, cream canal feeding a lake. Critters are invisible in the sea and their eyes fade in near shore. The objective went through four versions: houses filling with goo, a row of houses on water, comb branches, and finally a liquid front that darkens the last stretch with each leak. The user rejected the house and branch versions. The lake ended up pure white, mirroring the black sea, and never darkens. The HUD is one line under the wave bar: gear, homes, reagent, time. The currency is a placeholder until the real icon gets traced.

Lots of cutting. Sea swell lines, crashing waves, pulsing mouth rings: cut. Screen shake, ground cracks, clods, outlet rings: cut. The user keeps asking for calmer. Usually they're right.

The counter bug taught me something. "Shake" and "jump" are different complaints. The reagent counter had two motions: a spring jolt and a digit slide-in that restarted on every +1 tick. Fixed by swapping digits with zero duration. Only the homes counter keeps the slide, because a lost life is a physical hit and a gain should be calm. When a counter feels wrong, ask which motion. The reagent counter now counts up one by one, catching up in about half a second, and the user called counting-up "a form of juice for counters."

Critters die with juice too. The body bursts into charcoal chunks with a white flash ring and shards in the shooter's colour, while the eye flies to the reagent counter. Leaks collapse into their eye first, then the eye arcs to the homes counter in red. Red end to end, because the user said red "clearly signals it reached destination." A dashed warning line runs across the canal, one row short of the lake. Critters past it blink red but stay targetable. That's the wiggle room the user asked for.

Rivers grow now: a merge into a canal side, a fork from a corner, or an independent river. Each one telegraphs with a dashed course and eyes gathering at the mouth. Towers in the way sell at 100% refund (user's idea). The growth animation took a lot of iterations. A ball-headed black front got rejected. A subagent measured how Mini Motorways roads swell (nothing published, so it's footage-based): thin rounded thread to full width, exponential ease, no moving object. The final version is closer to that: pure charcoal, cream fade only where rivers meet the lake, and the fork opens in cream from the bifurcation. The per-river warning line kept snapping off a wavy shore, so the fork's line ended up one row high. One board-wide line anchored to the last full row fixed it.

Drag-to-build works. The ghost floats above the thumb, snaps to grid, turns watermelon when invalid, cancels if you drag it back over the picker. Selecting a tower morphs the picker boxes into upgrade and sell. The reach preview lights the canal water inside the tower's range. Board repainted in the promo icon palette: grass, cream canal, faint grid. User said "astonishingly good." It was the first time the sandbox looked like the thing I'd pictured. That's the flow moment.

Regional work ran alongside, and this is where the regions got their identities.

Pacific Haze, Green Cliffs and Blue Hour got scrapped when the campaign was rebuilt. Some of this morning's colour fights were for regions that no longer exist. Four regions survived: Softwood, Apricot Bay, Moss Valley and Rust Mesa.

Moss Valley got carved into an organic canyon with sine-wave walls, width breathing, and erosion via Chaikin smoothing. The user's verdict: "love it. let's keep it." The catch: the valley walls leave almost no room for rivers, so its dailies barely grow them. I flagged it and didn't fully solve it. While it was a handicap, its canyon had steeper waves and four-cell-shorter rivers, so every tower hugged the river. Once it became the first region, both handicaps went. It's now the easiest region on the normal curve, and that's the point.

Apricot Bay got headlands, blocky cliffs jutting from the sides, and small rocks strewn over the land. The user liked the headland idea and rejected my rounded contours on the small cliffs. The lake got a bay shape too, and that one took six looks. Narrow cove, ziggurat, pointy peak, smooth arc, sine headlands, then broader bends. Too smooth, like a fake coastline. Too rough, too even. The final version is detail masked by a slow patch envelope. User: "this one is it." Its cliffs grow organically from one cell, and the names are coastal (GULL, OYSTER, DRIFTWOOD, with POINT and HEAD for the sea ones). The user loved the names. Apricot's palette is sand, not the terracotta the user first proposed, because cream rivers vanish on terracotta. I pushed back with contrast numbers and we shipped the sampled sand. Bay has its own 25 seeds now, and the bot still loses a few of its late levels. That needs its own balance pass.

Rust Mesa got one organic mesa placed over the first river, so the river tunnels 3 to 8 cells, hidden. Later rivers can burrow under a mesa once, up to 8 cells. The full-width rock band was rejected as another narrow passage, and one big boxy mesa was "way too squared." The hidden river is the region's difficulty. The user's call: no twist needed. Ochre ground with red cliffs, pulled from a Mars reference. About 7% of dailies had no room for a full mesa, so those fall back to smaller ones. The tunnels bite hard in the bot numbers: three lives lost on level 6 where the curve wanted about 1.6. Parked until a real playtest.

Softwood got cliffs that rise mid-level, alternating with river growth. A dashed footprint shows a wave ahead, then a slide brings the cliff in, never on a tower and never next to a river mouth. Blurb: "Cliffs rise from the groves. Read their shadows." Each region got its own palette too. Moss Valley is forest green. Dailies draw a random region each day, same for everyone that day, so the daily is no longer always Softwood. Campaign stars are keyed by region, so the reorder didn't scramble anyone's saves.

Balance is where the numbers got honest. Terrain alone ramps difficulty. My old +0.25 per region step made everything past Moss unwinnable, and the sweep proved it. Took it out, so every region starts at zero and ramps only with level. Waves became floor formulas. Hits-to-kill matter more than raw HP. A fractional multiplier had quietly given darts extra hits. Floors fixed it. Softwood and Rust Mesa still run a bit steeper than I'd like. Parked for playtests.

The bot search (tool/search.dart) replaced the learned weights. It looks for the cheapest defence across waves 1 to 11. Result: Needle plus Ember. Bloom, Frost, and Lance at zero. Bloom's range buff moved it from 3% to 12% of spending. The damage buff did nothing. Specialists matter when they solve what others can't, not when they out-DPS the generalist. Rule of the day: change one knob, measure, trust the first instinct. Starting cash went to 250, and Bloom got a level type of its own: fast super tank from wave 6, tankier shells, long rivers. Then a Lens level type, one tank from wave 6 at double speed with fast shells. It's the first type where Lens decides whether you hold or get overrun. The lesson from both: tanky bodies just slow down into Ember's range. Speed is the lever.

Frost got reworked. Every foam hit stacks chill, and chilled bodies turn brittle, taking extra damage equal to the chill. The mechanics were the user's design. My job was the numbers. Bloom levels were starved, not too tough. Halving small bodies halved income, so the search held only 8 of 16 Ember dailies. Bloom kills now pay 1.5x, which lifted that to 14 of 16. Frost's share of spending on Ember levels went from 2% to 13%. Side effect: Frost and Ember took over Bloom levels, and the Bloom tower's share of spending fell from 32% to 3%. Deferred until playtests say more.

The rest of the balance work was rounds. Each round is a few hours of machine time, and the numbers only moved when I touched one thing at a time. Round one nudged Bloom's reload and Ember's damage. Round two added wave-mix genes. Round three was too easy and got killed. Round four made Ember overtuned, so Needle and Bloom went to zero spend. Round five is next, with Ember and Bloom tuned tighter. The endless waves past 11 still haven't been validated. Ember also came out of every campaign level until an earned currency exists, so no level asks for a tower nobody can afford yet.

Perf had its own humbling. I'd assumed the simulation was the bottleneck. Profiling said map generation was 81 to 85% of headless time. Moved the walk to a grid lookup, pooled worker isolates, memoised shared boards. Moss Valley's balance ladder went from 35 seconds to 9. 660 board fingerprints stayed identical. Then the same problem showed up on device, and the campaign fix above came out of it. Measure before you guess. I guessed and the profiler laughed.

A playtest on Softwood level 12 went too easy: three stars, reagent piling up. The cause was my canal bounty, which climbed with health, plus a needle spam meta. I halved the bounty climb and added wigglers and skipper packs. The user rejected softening the finale and swapping skippers for wigglers. They want more units, so more units it is.

The canal runs on the real session now, a 9 by 17 board, and it went out as build 17. Then the playtest: "too hard, critters too fast and too small." The cause was route length. Canal routes are about twice as long, so the speed multiplier scaled up per cell. Clamped it, scaled the critters up 1.25x. Waves split across the mouths. Growth no longer adds bodies, which fixed a spike of about 6x by wave 4 on fast-growing seeds. The bot reaches the finale 61% of the time now, up from 28% on the old survival.

The worst find of the day was quiet. The canal runs had sent zero gameplay analytics since launch. The only place events were wired was the classic game page, and I'd just deleted the classic game page without noticing. Joder. Now the canal page reports run start, tower built, upgraded and sold, wave passed, run finished, and run abandoned. Nobody complained, because nobody could: the ghost audience can't react to events it never received.

Before the big deletion, a simplify pass found a real bug. The canal cash counter used the base bounty instead of the wave's bounty, so it ran ahead of the critters still in flight. Fixed, and the counter now waits for the eye to land.

Then the big one. Every region moved onto the canal board, and the classic engine got retired. 40 files, +337 and -6083. The old game page, replays, road and mountain generators, the old wave planner, the old terrain painter, a pile of tests. I verified bot runs were tick-identical to the previous build before deleting anything. It felt good. It also felt like grieving six thousand lines of work I'd been proud of. The old engine was a dead end I kept polishing. Finishing is a kind of loss. Each simplify pass after that deleted more code than it added, and that felt better than any feature. Every daily plays the canal now, and saved classic-daily runs get moved aside on load, so nobody crashes into a dead mode. Board taps got a selection haptic too. Small, but it makes the board feel like it's listening.

Signing bit me again. Xcode's stored Apple ID token went dead and the build died with "No Accounts." Got past it with a command-line archive and an API key. The real fix is removing and re-adding the account in Xcode, and that's still on my list. Someone will hit this again, and that someone is me. Also bumped the iOS minimum from 13 to 15, because the newer Xcode refuses anything lower.

Build 33 also fixed a nasty picker bug. When only Needle and Bloom were earned, the built-tower menu reused shop boxes and assumed at least three towers. Upgrade and sell shared a box, so tapping upgrade sold the tower. The fix lays the menu out from the towers on offer, with 26 widget tests covering 1 to 5 towers at several widths and text scales.

Builds 28 through 34 went out over the day: the journey road, the winding road, Rust Mesa, Apricot Bay's headlands, the balance rounds, the picker fix, and finally the Frost rework with the Bloom bounty. Build 34 went to the internal group and processed clean. External groups are waiting on my explicit OK, which is fine. I don't want to push this at strangers before I've watched a real player.

Two things I haven't resolved. The "Mini TD" name next to a Mini Motorways look is a trademark risk I flagged and didn't settle. And Moss Valley's dailies still barely grow rivers. Apricot Bay's tide idea and Ember's unlock for the bay are still undecided. The regions have identities now in terrain, cliffs, and hidden rivers, but Ember still has no earned currency to buy it with, so it's locked behind stars for now.

Was the optimism justified? Today's answer is "maybe." The canal runs. The regions have their own shapes. The numbers stopped lying to me, and they only did it once I deleted the things they were lying about. I'm proud of the calm, cut-down look. I'm not sure it's better yet. A real player will decide that, not a spreadsheet.
