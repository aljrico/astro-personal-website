Pushed the road refactor this morning. Went from the radius-0.70 filled concentric approach to simple rounded polylines at 0.45 cells — just quarter circles at each junction with a single stroked centreline per route. The arc math moved into `Invader.locate` now, exact and tight. Removed `cornerRadii`, `roadOutline`, `filletPolyline`, `truncatePolyline` and all the junction parameter passing that lived with them. The architecture is cleaner. The rendering is cleaner.

The moment it rendered right was good. That's the stuff. The pixel-perfect instant when the screen does exactly what you pictured. Roads sit clean now. Junctions flow. The centreline doesn't lie to you.

But now the bodies don't fit.

Enemy bodies overhang the narrower road. The width dropped from ~0.55 to 0.45 cells, and the super tank is 0.5 wide. It's a real problem, not a "close enough" one. Two paths: shrink all bodies by ~20% or revert to 0.5-width roads. Both have a cost. Narrower roads make the lanes feel more fragile. Smaller bodies make the tank less imposing. Haven't decided which loss is bigger yet. Open too: diagonal staircases are still a maybe for step 5, and the junction merge hasn't shown up on screen yet to validate it actually works.

Later, the consent flow and ad precedence hit main. Wired the purchase hook to the review engine. Added the readiness memoization — UMP successful state stays memoized while ads-impossible states keep retrying. Baked in the confirmed-no-fill schedule and the 60-second no-stack guard to prevent cascade failures. 119 tests passed, 2 skipped, full validation. That part was clean. Build the logic, wire it up, watch it work. No surprises.

Then the leaderboard thing surfaced. An idle daily run somehow escaped the sandbox and hit the real leaderboard instead of staying contained. The test setup was loose. Started testing against campaign levels instead, which has tighter boundaries. It's the kind of mistake that compounds quietly if you're not watching.

Left some work uncommitted on purpose — inverted the IconControl home buttons, formatter churn, some store logic. None of it's baked. Sometimes you need the mess visible so you don't forget it's there.

The roads feel like progress. The bodies problem feels like tomorrow's puzzle. The test pollution is a note for later. That's how it goes.
