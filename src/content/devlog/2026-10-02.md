Started the afternoon with campaign cards. The old design was muddy — darkened ground, weak lens, pointless palette dots fighting with the green backdrop. Needed the whole thing brighter. Pulled hand-picked pastels per region, but the colors didn't land right the first time. Lavender felt too cool against the warm palette. Coral clashed. Kept iterating until the feedback locked in on the right tone: poppy for one, sky blue for another. The Play tag followed — flat gold, white bold text, bottom-right, a label not a button. The whole card still opens the level grid. It's a label thing. Contrast was thin (1.7:1) but that's fine since the text is large and bold and you know immediately when the refinement is right.

Font hunt was its own thing. Mini Motorways uses Helvetica but can't bundle on Android, so TeX Gyre Heros became the answer. Dropped it everywhere. Close enough. ARCO stays for the logo. Nothing that makes you leap out of your chair, just the invisible work that has to be right.

The lenses needed work. They were showing level 1 mostly empty, spawn markers clipped at the rim. Real problem: the lens painter wasn't getting the shore and river paths that the game computes in the actual game. Found it — the game computes them from the backdrop, but the lens painter wasn't asking for them. Fixed it by computing shore and river in board coordinates, shifted by the right offset. Now when you look at Apricot Bay in the lens, you see the water. The inlet draws properly. The landscape sits right. The coast shows. One of those bugs that's invisible until you notice it, then it's obvious.

Then the real thing. Ditched the old wordmark and demo art. Built the home page as a Mini Motorways city grid — static streets, ground with faint gridlines, two white avenues cutting through. "Mini TD." wordmark, simple, bold. Then hooked in invaders walking patrol routes in real time, at game pace, across the screen.

First draft didn't move anything. I'd built a ticker and never started it — just sat there staring at a static screen for a second before I caught it. Fixed it. Suddenly there's life on the home screen. Invaders walking invisible paths. That's the moment. There's a naming thing sitting in the back though. "Mini" plus the Motorways grid look hits dangerously close. App Store trademark risk, possibly a polite letter from the dev behind Mini Motorways territory. Something to circle back to.

The invaders got redesigned too. Swimming things now. Soft outlines, traveling wave motion, one centered eye each. Motes like drops, darts as long protozoans, tanks as squat superellipses with tail lobes, colonies that pulse with five members. Quick iteration — tried a few directions, feedback switched to "Critters," and it stuck. That's the kind of polish that doesn't change how anything works but changes everything about how it feels.

Late afternoon: pushed everything to TestFlight. Version numbering had drifted — pubspec was ahead of what App Store Connect saw — but caught it before upload. The first cut was 1.0.0+13 but ASC already had 12 and wouldn't accept it (old train was closed). Bumped to 1.1.0+14. Built from a clean worktree with the release config flags set right, made sure the work-in-progress stayed out like we planned. Built, uploaded, added to the test groups, submitted for review.

By the time it was up, that late-afternoon light was doing its thing where finishing something feels like relief and restlessness at the same time. Shipped a build. Tightened the home page. Made the invaders look alive. Refined the colors through iteration. Did all the pieces. Now it's waiting — TestFlight review, user feedback, what lands next.

The WIP is still sitting uncommitted, untouched. That's fine. Some things aren't ready yet. Some days you ship what's solid and leave the rest for later.
