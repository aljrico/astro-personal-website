Morning started with the purchase sheet. Rebuilt it from the trimmed mockup—header, CURRENT/NEXT BOOST cards, gem button forked off the existing upgrade sheet. The code was clean enough to move fast, but the palette work was where the session actually lived. Purples threading from gem to wing family. Greens cascading caribbean into the button colors. Ring widths all locked to one constant. Stack pill through x32. These aren't decisions that move the funnel, but when they're wrong you feel it in your bones. When they're right—which took three passes to get there—you *see* it. Committed and pushed.

Mid-day pivot to notifications. Copy and localizations. Twenty files in l10n, boilerplate work that's necessary and about as glamorous as watching paint dry. New case notification keys, environment case body copy. The scheduler code is still sitting unstaged—the actual trigger logic—but the surface layer is out. That's the tradeoff today: ship the messages, leave the delivery mechanism for later.

Then the afternoon hit different. Restored the permanent-boost wing badge—I'd deleted it earlier and the tap flow needed rewiring through the sheet closure. Clean. But more importantly, that's when the Fast scheme landed. 

This isn't elegant architecture. It's not a refactoring. It's just an app-target-only build configuration that bypasses everything the normal scheme touches. Warm builds went from ninety seconds to four or five. The simulator doesn't have to reinstall the container, doesn't have to re-resolve paths. Iteration gets *frictionless*. This is the kind of improvement that compounds so quietly you don't notice it until six months later when you realize you've built twice as much because the feedback loop was shorter. Patched the global build-ios skill, committed both pieces.

Three pushes. One day. The momentum is real.

Unfinished weight in the background: the notification scheduler still needs to wake, the icon swap is pending, contracts and marketing assets are sitting untracked. This is the normal rhythm—ship, see the next twenty things, decide what matters. The work doesn't end. It just reshuffles.

Morning will say what's next.
