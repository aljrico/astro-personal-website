Started with a bug that wasn't a bug.

Desert Hard 6. Someone reported it like the board was lying. I went in ready to fix logic. Then I sat with the screenshot and the board was fine. The player had read a barrel cactus as not-a-cactus, and "1 row south" as the same column. That's not a solver error. That's a language problem. The board was speaking cactus and the player was hearing something else.

So I did the Lanterntown thing. One cactus kind per Desert board. Layout revision 2. Campaign v2 gets the new recipe, v1 stays grandfathered, Daily cuts over on 2026-12-01. Added a shade guarantee while I was in there: non-saguaro boards get a spire, and every one-kind board keeps a saguaro or a spire shading an open cell. Goldens regenerated. Only desert moved. Legacy hashes byte-identical, which is the kind of quiet that makes me trust the rest of the day.

settings_sheet_test still has two failures. They were already on main. Not my circus today. They were still not my circus at 4pm, which I guess is a kind of consistency.

Then Cookie Empire pulled.

Redesigned the Mine expedition cards. I wanted them to feel like Verdoku's ticking stopwatch: odds on every reward, a separate red death chance, a small flat send button. Named every depth. The Shallows down to The Last Light. Each one gets a single line. That's it. One line has to carry the whole mood of going deeper.

Ran a writing bake-off. Five models. Grok, Codex, Fable, Sonnet, Opus. All of them came out too Hemingway. Sparse in the way that thinks it's profound. I rewrote four lines myself and used those as the style reference. Look at this. The titles and descriptions blur into focus at random times, only when they scroll into view. Locked tiers stay blurred. It looks like a game now. Not a spreadsheet wearing a costume.

Gave the cards more air. Twice. By eye. Small visual iterations, the kind I can live in. Then the Crystaller cards wanted the same treatment. Same padding, a much bigger ore cost, more room for the description. The Craft button became the expedition flat pill. Pulled it into one shared widget instead of copying it. I usually don't bother. Today the cards were asking.

Then the tabs. Every Expeditions tab got its own backdrop. Slate grey forge light for the Smith. Tower violet for the Crystaller. A bonfire glow rising from the bottom of the Hollows. The Fall kept its Hell gradient. Moved the light off-centre so they stop feeling like a matching set. Tab switches glide between backdrops.

At first the glide felt late. I thought I'd added a delay. It was the ease-in-out curve dragging its feet. Switched to ease-out and it snaps. Hostia. This one felt great. This is the stuff. The screen doing exactly what I pictured. I kept switching tabs just to watch the light change.

Deleted the screenshot tests. The golden ones. For good. They changed one decision in weeks and cost minutes on every iteration. New rule: change, hot reload, analyze, I look at it myself. Code quality is overrated. If I can see it, I don't need a robot to photograph it for me.

Late afternoon I retired the per-repo engineering diary. Commits and memories already cover it. I was writing the same day twice and calling it process. Committed the retirement. That commit is what finally woke the reminder. First time it has ever reached the model.

Because the scratch reminder for this devlog had never worked. Wrong event check. User-only message. The thing that was supposed to catch the day had been silently failing the whole time. I retired the diary and the diary started. Of course it did.

Fixed the hook. It now drafts with Grok 4.6 and falls back to Haiku. Grok's CLI kept acting like a coding agent, going looking for files that aren't there, until its Claude-compat discovery and tools got switched off. The machine wanted to be an engineer. I needed a writer. Sometimes that's the whole job: telling the tool which room it's in.

There's a cost to this. Agentic speed is heroin. Direct, don't type, keep moving. But the idle thoughts used to arrive mid-keystroke. Today I noticed them arriving after. Or not arriving. Hard to tell which.

Then the evening became a factory.

The cactus change in the morning wanted plumbing. Boards that remember which recipe made them. Hero art that doesn't flash the wrong image on cold start. Islands misreading that actually reaches Endless and Daily. I pointed. Agents shipped. /simplify over everything since v1.21.0 first, four reviewers, then two agents and me applying. Sixteen files, no behavior change. The deeper stuff I skipped on purpose and spawned into.

Hero art A/B: the treatment arms were flashing the huge control image on cold start. Now they remember the last settled arm and read it before the app even runs. First-ever launch the home hero stays blank for a second and a half instead of lying. Nobody will notice. That's the point.

Boards record recipe and generation now. Old data infers. Desert v2 has its own namespace so it lands on revision 2. Stale Endless and Daily caches regenerate unless you already started or solved. The cactus work from this morning is survivable tomorrow because of this. I did not type it. I directed it. The shower thought never came because there was no shower in the keystrokes.

Found a Riverpod 2 trap in the access policy. An unlistened FutureProvider that gets invalidated mid-await never rebuilds, so .future just hangs. Joder. A helper that holds a subscription. Skipped the optional drip-timer listener. Timing risk. Not tonight.

Islands misreading tuning finally reaches Endless and Daily. Generation revision 3. Then the physicist brain wanted numbers. Tuning alone barely moves the strong-misreading gate. Easy 7 to 17 percent. Hard 2 to 10. beside_stops_at_shore still dominates. The Hard cap costs yield, 12 percent below floor if you don't budget it. I could have guessed that this morning. I measured it anyway. It felt productive. Was it?

A ticker deprecation. A migration that lists the attempts directory once instead of over and over. I almost took a shortcut on the seed and didn't, because another agent was rewriting how recipes get identified and duplicating the rule would silently diverge. Rare moment of future-me doing present-me a favor.

The timer badge was shifting glyphs by a third of a pixel. Pixel-diffed old vs new in a throwaway test like I still believed in goldens. Bounded saveLayer at a fractional rect was the culprit. Unbounded, and it disappears. The frustration of the trivial. Then the solver grew callbacks, the evaluator reused them, 10 to 15 percent faster on 78 boards, same solutions in the same order. Quiet. I wanted to show someone. There was no one around who would care about solution order.

Then the analytics SDK got a pause flag and a this-run install bit. Not pushed. Host persists the pause so Verdoku keeps its own prefs key. Enrollment events wait. Plumbing for a problem I don't want to think about at this hour.

Three projects. Then a swarm. A cactus that needed to be simpler. Cards that needed to look like a game. Tabs that glow. A diary that was already writing itself. A pile of Verdoku internals I directed instead of typed.

The unfinished stuff is still unfinished. That's fine. Nothing is abandoned.

Was the optimism justified. Today, a little. The ease-out snap was enough by itself.
