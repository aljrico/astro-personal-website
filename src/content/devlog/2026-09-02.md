Toast day.

The app had SnackBars scattered everywhere. Hint results here, purchase restores there, the Discord link confirmation somewhere else. Each one threading through ScaffoldMessenger like it's 2019, each one with its own slightly different tone. It worked. Nobody complained. But every time one slid up from the bottom I winced a little.

So I built one toast. A single overlay, hosted at the root of the app, with success/error/info variants and an icon. Then went hunting: the game screen's hint results, the shop's rewarded-ad flow, the remove-ads purchase restore, the level catalog's batch messages, the settings screen with its Discord and review links. One by one, ripped the SnackBars out and pointed everything at the new thing. Six call sites, one voice. No more ScaffoldMessenger threading, no more five slightly different tones. This is the kind of session I actually enjoy — not a feature, not a fix, just making the app feel like one person built it instead of five.

Hot reload straight to the actual iPhone the whole time — a pid-file and a signal wired up so the loop is: change, signal, watch it appear on the device in my hand. Slightly cursed plumbing, but when the loop is that tight, the afternoon disappears. This is the flow state. Not architecture. A toast sliding in with the right icon and the right color, over and over, until it's exactly right.

Also fixed a tiny thing that had been bugging me: the "new cases available" tag on the level catalog was ALL CAPS while everything else around it was sentence case. Changed it in every language so it matches the pill styling. Nobody will ever notice. That's sort of the point — you only notice these things when they're wrong.

Tried a stronger sky color on a title. Stared at it. Reverted it. Twenty minutes of my life for the confirmation that the original was fine. Some experiments exist just to prove the current thing right.

Also dropped an analysis bundle on ad spend caps into the repo — the CPA numbers I'd been chewing on since the weekend. The physicist brain wanted its spreadsheet. Fine. It got one. Tomorrow we'll see if the numbers actually change a decision or if I just enjoyed making them.

Committed the whole thing at quarter past seven. One commit, everything in it — the toast, all six migrations, the sentence-case sweep across every translation file, even the ad-spend analysis riding along. There's something satisfying about a commit that's pure cleanup. No new surface area. No new bugs waiting to be discovered. Just less mess than there was this morning.

Small day. Consistent day. The app is a little more *one thing* than it was when I woke up. I'll take it.
