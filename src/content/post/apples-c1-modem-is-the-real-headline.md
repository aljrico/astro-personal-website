Apple just revealed C1, its first in-house 5G modem. This is the real headline, not the iPhone 16e. And my God, what a headline.

A multi-billion-dollar effort over seven years has culminated in a monumental technical achievement. More vertical integration, prying another supplier's grip off the iPhone supply chain. This could be the biggest news in the hardware business since the first M chip.

## Deepening the moat

At the stage Apple is at with the iPhone, the most impactful thing it can do is not to grow the market. **It's to deepen the moat around the business.** That mostly comes from increasing efficiencies and synergies within its own products. I first got interested in the stock when I noticed how methodically Apple was doing exactly that, and I became convinced when I learned about the M series. Improvements like these don't just raise margins. They make the products better. The first M1 was a ground-breaking change for the laptop market, giving Apple a lead in both product quality and cost-effective production.

C1 is another one of those moments. To the shock of nobody, one of the most crucial parts of a phone is the modem: the little machine that keeps you connected. Consumers overlook it for one reason. Almost everyone in the market, Apple included, has been using the exact same modems, the ones Qualcomm makes. With nothing to compare against, everyone took current modem performance for granted.

That's only natural when you know Qualcomm has held an iron grip on phone modems for decades, charging astronomical prices and imposing ruthless deals that only worked because phone makers had no alternative. It enjoyed a position similar to the one Intel had in PCs a couple of decades ago. What happened to Intel? AMD happened, on TSMC's factories. And eventually the M chips happened. C1 is what's now happening to Qualcomm.

Many engineers said this might be near impossible to build. Radio-frequency engineering is a nightmare of a field: tons of weird edge cases, interference issues and nearly unpredictable physics. Most of the technology we take for granted is built on decades of tribal knowledge that Qualcomm was gatekeeping.

## Why launch it on the cheap phone?

Do I think C1 will outperform Qualcomm's current line-up? No. I think it will be worse. Simply getting C1 out the door is a monumental achievement, and I don't want you to read this as me downplaying it. But Apple needs to test it in as many devices as possible, to get real data from people using it in strange places and under weird conditions. With that data it can iterate, refine and make the next modem better.

Apple itself is downplaying this. Two sentences in the press release and 15 seconds in the announcement video of an iPhone whose main selling point is being cheaper. Apple is usually proud of its in-house achievements and loves to brag about vertical integration. It's downplaying this one because it knows the product isn't great yet. There's a very good reason it's launching on the entry-level phone and not on the Pros. These devices are the guinea pigs. A high-volume phone with more tolerant users is the perfect petri dish to keep working on it until it's right.

And getting it right is crucial. It can improve the iPhone's connection speed and robustness, but most of all its battery life. Unsuccessful network searching burns through a phone's battery and is probably the main reason you rush for a charger at the end of the day. If anyone can take a modem and fully integrate it with the main chip, the OS and the whole phone into a far more efficient system, it's Apple. That means an unequivocally better product, sold at a wider margin.

This is huge news. For consumers, because more competition in 5G modems can only improve things. And for Apple investors, because it's yet another example of why this company is the giant it is. People place far too much importance on flashy, consumer-facing features that are shallow and meaningless, while the true engineering feats that unlock new products go unnoticed. This is the sort of thing Apple's competitors wish they could do.

## Looking back from 2026

The guinea-pig plan worked faster than I expected. Seven months later, Apple shipped the [C1X in the iPhone Air](https://9to5mac.com/2025/09/09/apple-debuts-new-c1x-in-house-modem-the-successor-to-c1/), up to twice as fast as C1 and using less power, and in March 2026 it put C1X in the iPhone 17e and the iPad Air too. Ookla's real-world data found the C1X [roughly matching Qualcomm's X80](https://www.idropnews.com/news/apple-c1x-modem-vs-qualcomm-x80-performance/261427/) on download speeds and beating it on latency in most markets, though Qualcomm still has the edge on peak performance. Apple's Pro phones were still on Qualcomm at that point.
