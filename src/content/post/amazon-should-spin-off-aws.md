Amazon is really the tale of two companies.

On one side is the part everyone knows: the retail business, the Amazon store, e-commerce. Its competitors are the likes of Walmart and Costco.

On the other side is the cloud business, Amazon's real cash cow: Amazon Web Services. Its competitors are the likes of Microsoft and Google.

Amazon has led that cloud market, and it shows in who runs on it. Big streaming services like Netflix and Disney+ run on AWS, and Apple uses it for parts of iCloud.

Amazon has clearly won the trust of these big companies. Their businesses depend heavily on Amazon's services. That's huge leverage.

## Who doesn't trust Amazon?

But you know who doesn't trust Amazon? Other big retailers. Most of the top US retailers won't touch AWS with a stick. They use Google Cloud or Microsoft Azure instead. Walmart, Kroger, Target, Walgreens and CVS have all been reported to avoid it.

Why? It doesn't matter whether AWS is better or cheaper. They won't accept it. Ever. Anyone who has sold software to them knows this. Some of these companies won't even accept third-party software if it's hosted on AWS.

AWS is the pearl in the oyster. The cash cow. The truly profitable segment that keeps the whole company fed. And the harsh truth is that Amazon's retail business may be dragging it down. It erodes other retailers' trust and pushes customers away. That's bad.

Think about it from Walmart's side. Amazon might benefit if Walmart's website went down on Black Friday. Can you imagine the drama if AWS happened to malfunction for one of those clients?

I'll say it again: retailers don't trust Amazon. And that hurts AWS's bottom line.

## Why spin it off

AWS's cash flow is no longer needed to fund Amazon's e-commerce business. Advertising already generates enough for that. What shareholders actually need right now is for AWS to be spun off.

AWS is the main reason I own Amazon. If Amazon's retail business really needs AWS to prop it up, that battle is already lost. I don't think that's the case, by the way. I believe advertising and Prime can keep fuelling Amazon's retail business.

So it seems to be in shareholders' best interest for Amazon to spin off AWS. But hey, maybe it's just me. Maybe I just don't know better. Maybe Amazon has bigger plans and needs AWS's sweet cash flow to make them happen.

I guess time will tell.

## Looking back

Time told. Amazon never spun off AWS, and the bigger plans turned out to be AI. AWS's cash went into data centres and chips for the AI boom, including Amazon's multi-billion-dollar bet on Anthropic.
