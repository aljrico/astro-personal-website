Be honest: when you think "fast fashion", you don't think "luxury-tier gross margins". And yet here we are. Amancio Ortega's creation just reported Q3 2025 numbers that have analysts comparing Zara's parent company to luxury houses rather than to H&M or the Shein crowd. The stock jumped 8-9% and pushed the IBEX 35 close to record highs.

## The headline numbers

For the first nine months of the 2025-26 financial year (February to October 2025):

- Sales: €28.2B, +2.7% reported, +6.2% in constant currency
- Net income: €4.62B, +3.9% year over year (a record)
- Gross margin: 59.7%, up 27 basis points
- Net cash: ~€11.3B

Q3 on its own was even juicier:

- Sales: €9.8B, +4.9%
- Gross margin: 62.2%, up 79 basis points
- Net profit: €1.83B, +9%

And then the little bomb at the end: sales from 1 November to 1 December were up 10.6%. That's your Black Friday and early Christmas trading update.

## The margin story

Reported growth of 2-5% looks "meh" at first glance. But 6-8% in constant currency is solid for a company this size, and a fourth quarter starting at +10.6% is genuinely impressive.

The real story, though, is that 62.2% gross margin. For context, H&M typically sits in the low 50s. This is a mass-market clothing retailer posting margins that would make some luxury names jealous. Inditex is proving it can push prices, nail the product mix and keep markdowns under control, even in a choppy economy.

Operating expenses grew only 3% while sales grew 4.9%. That's operating leverage actually working. Staff and rent costs move with sales, and the company has built store and logistics systems that scale without blowing up the cost base. If it sustains mid to high single-digit sales growth, a good chunk of that should fall straight to the bottom line.

## The logistics moat

Inditex is in the middle of a two-year "extraordinary" investment programme: around €900M a year, focused on logistics and technology. The Zaragoza 2 logistics centre is part of a broader €1.8B expansion. In some flagship stores, up to 90% of transactions go through assisted checkouts. They're automated to hell and back.

That sounds scary until you look at the P&L. They're spending all that while still growing net income and expanding margins. What they're really doing is trading a bit of near-term free cash flow for a stronger moat in speed, inventory turnover and omnichannel execution. That's exactly what made Inditex special in the first place. They're doubling down on their core advantage instead of getting distracted.

## The US opportunity

Management's tone on the US was clear: "This is a huge market where we're still small." New flagships in Los Angeles and Las Vegas. Upcoming stores in Charlotte and Miami. They're even pushing Bershka into the US.

If they execute decently, the US could be a growth driver for years. It's obviously competitive as hell, with fast fashion, online players and Shein breathing down everyone's neck. But Inditex's model is built for exactly that kind of competition: fast reaction to demand, local operations, integrated online and offline. This is their game.

## What could go wrong?

The call was mostly upbeat, but the subtext shows the pressure points.

Currency is a real headwind. Management mentioned around 4% currency impact on 2025 sales at current rates. That's material. Regulatory changes to EU duty exemptions on low-value parcels could add friction, although management says its exposure is low, since its model is built on local operations, not shipping tiny parcels across borders.

And as always with clothing: fashion risk. One or two bad collections and all of this suddenly looks less bulletproof. The whole story depends on staying culturally relevant. Which, to be fair, is the unspoken assumption behind any investment in this sector.

## The big picture

The market reaction tells you everything. Several analyst houses raised their price targets to around €59-60 a share. Some explicitly compare Inditex's metrics to "luxury-adjacent" businesses rather than plain clothing retailers. The rally is the market saying: growth is good enough, margins are outstanding, and Q4 is starting hot. Inditex is being treated as a quality compounder, not a cyclical fashion name. That's a very different kind of investment.

Here's my take. If you're thinking about Inditex in a portfolio, this call reinforces the idea that it's more of a quality, defensive growth company than a high-beta fashion rollercoaster. A steadily growing top line, expanding margins, record profits and a €11.3B net cash war chest. Management sounds calm and confident, not hyped. They believe their model is structurally advantaged and can keep compounding.

The real question is whether you're happy paying a premium multiple for that. Not hyper-growth, but reliable expansion with high returns on capital.
