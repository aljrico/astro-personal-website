Honestly, kind of both. Let me explain.

## I've seen this before

When I started doing data science in the early 2010s, the name had only just been coined, and everything was about Big Data. Cloud computing, huge databases, predictive machine learning, Kaggle competitions, you name it. We had tree-based models and neural networks, and you could train an algorithm to spot a cat in a picture. Amazing stuff.

The expectations completely blew up. "We'll cure cancer by training algorithms to spot tumours." "Cars will be 100% autonomous in a couple of years." "Facebook will know what you want to buy before you do." And of course: "Algorithms will replace humans. Run for the hills!"

Needless to say, almost none of that materialised. Or not enough to live up to the hype. It was 100% a bubble.

But was it all smoke and mirrors? Not really. Recommendation algorithms do run social media. Face detection is definitely a thing in your phone's photo gallery. Teslas start screaming at you if you're about to drive into a wall. We got real value out of all that frenzy.

## Then came the Transformer

By 2017 the Big Data hype was cooling off. Deep learning was doing impressive things, but mostly narrow ones: classifying images, playing Go, translating sentences. Nobody outside the field expected machines to hold a conversation any time soon.

Then researchers at Google published their famous paper "Attention Is All You Need", introducing the Transformer. I won't go into the technical details, but this paper is what made ChatGPT, Midjourney, Gemini and every other hot "AI" possible. In short, Transformers are a deep learning architecture that lets a model look at all the words in a long text at once and work out which ones matter for each other, instead of reading word by word and forgetting what came before.

The GPT in ChatGPT stands for "Generative Pre-trained Transformer".

Transformers are a substantial technological leap, the way calculus was in the 17th century, or the first simple machine learning algorithms were in the 1950s.

All the AI frenzy we're living through is the process of putting this shiny new tool to use, and frantically refining it. But eventually we'll hit another wall. We'll optimise everything that can be optimised and progress will plateau, until the next breakthrough. When will that happen? Nobody knows. Nobody *can* know. Anyone who claims to is fooling themselves, or fooling you. Maybe this year, maybe in 10 years, maybe in 1,000. Discoveries have to be discovered before we can know anything about them.

## Hype and winters

We've had AI winters before. In the 1960s, plenty of people assumed human-level AI was imminent. Money flooded into software and hardware ventures promising to lead the AI revolution. Everyone daydreamed about what robots and AI could do, and what new knowledge they'd uncover. People started wondering what the point of employing humans would be once machines were better at everything. Were they wrong? Well, yeah! But that excitement also brought a lot of progress with it.

Hype and despair cycles are natural. We just need to be aware of them. It's fine to get excited about new technology, but we need to be careful with overly optimistic expectations.

I'm not going to pull out my crystal ball and say Nvidia is done. Honestly, it's a great company. But I see a lot of people going all in on very specific expectations about a future that, frankly, nobody knows.

We're at the "Peak of Inflated Expectations". Just be ready for what comes next.
