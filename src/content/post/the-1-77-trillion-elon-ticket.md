*Originally posted on [eToro](https://www.etoro.com/posts/91f31f80-6681-11f1-8080-800012744355) on 12 June 2026, the day SpaceX went public. All figures are from the S-1 and market data as of that day.*

The largest IPO in history goes live today. SpaceX raised $75 billion, nearly triple the $25.6 billion Saudi Aramco pulled in back in 2019, the previous record. That values the company at roughly $1.77 trillion, instantly one of the ten largest listed companies on the planet. I spent days going through the S-1, and after the corrections I had to make to my first pass, the picture is uglier than I thought.

## Starlink: growing subscribers, shrinking ARPU

Let's start with the good. SpaceX dominates the launch market, and Starlink hit 10.3 million subscribers across 164 countries by the end of Q1 2026, roughly double a year earlier. Connectivity revenue was $3.26 billion in Q1 alone.

Sounds great until you look at the unit economics. Starlink's average revenue per user went from $99 a month in 2023, to $91 in 2024, to $81 in 2025, to $66 in Q1 2026. A third of per-user revenue gone in 30 months, and the decline is accelerating. New subscribers come from cheaper countries on cheaper plans, and SpaceX's own filing says to expect more of the same. Revenue still grows, but far slower than the subscriber count, because every new cohort is worth less than the last.

It sounds like a nitpick. But the only profitable segment in this company has structurally deteriorating unit economics that nobody talks about.

## The xAI hole

As a whole, SpaceX swung from $791 million of net profit in 2024 to a $4.94 billion net loss in 2025, on $18.67 billion of revenue. A $5.7 billion reversal in twelve months, driven almost entirely by the merger with xAI (which includes X). And it's accelerating: the combined company lost $4.28 billion in Q1 2026 alone.

Two numbers are easy to mix up here (I mixed them up myself). xAI's 2025 operating loss was $6.4 billion. Capex is a different line: $12.7 billion in 2025, and AI capex alone hit $7.7 billion in Q1 2026, which annualises north of $30 billion. Losses and capex aren't the same thing, but both are cash leaving the building. Elon intends to fund that buildout with this IPO.

Run the maths: $30B+ a year of AI capex plus xAI's losses, against Starlink's ~$4.4B of operating income and ~18,700 bitcoins on the balance sheet. Roughly two to two and a half years of runway at the current pace. And the pace is accelerating. A risky place to be.

## The valuation

Despite all that, at $1.77 trillion the company is priced at ~95x its 2025 revenue. The most expensive big tech companies trade at 20x or 30x sales, and they're massively profitable. SpaceX isn't. For an independent sanity check, Morningstar's fair value estimate sits around $780 billion, less than half the IPO price.

Think of it as a sum of the parts.

- **Starlink:** $11.4 billion of revenue growing ~50%, with $4.42 billion of operating income, around a 39% margin. Not bad at all. Worthy of a premium.
- **Launch:** $4.1 billion of revenue with a $657 million operating loss (Starship R&D ate $3 billion in 2025). Even losing money, it's deeply strategic and makes everything else possible.
- **xAI:** the elephant in the room. $12.7 billion of capex generated $3.2 billion of revenue. Mature big tech runs a revenue-to-capex ratio of 2-3x. xAI sits at 0.25x. There's no way to sugarcoat it: that's catastrophic.

Purely on the business, buying SpaceX means buying Starlink at a price where all the growth you hope for is already paid for, plus a trillion dollars on top for Grok and the bet that orbital data centres are the future.

## The Elon factor

And that's before the Elon factor. He owns Class B shares with 82%+ of the voting power. The only person who could remove him as CEO is himself. This is his company, his project, his dream. Let's be real: this isn't investing in a company in the traditional sense. Starlink's cash funds xAI's bet, and public shareholders have zero say in that capital allocation. It's a bet on Elon, his vision, and his ability to shape the future.

## Two things in its favour

For balance, two things work in SpaceX's favour.

First, it's an all-primary offering. Every dollar goes to the company, not to insiders cashing out. Elon didn't sell a single share. That's a war chest, not a liquidity event.

Second, the float. Only ~4% of the company trades (roughly $70 billion of stock), insiders are locked up for 180 days, and MSCI inclusion starts on 13 June, with index demand that could reach $35-50 billion against that float. That's a structural setup for a squeeze, so the first weeks of trading will tell you almost nothing about fundamentals. Don't let a green candle on day one convince you the valuation makes sense.

## So what are you buying?

Strip away the noise and SpaceX is: Starlink, the fastest-growing internet service on the planet, with structurally shrinking ARPU. Grok, burning tens of billions a year against $3.2 billion of revenue. The X platform, plus bitcoin on the balance sheet. All controlled by one man with 82%+ of the votes, and priced at more than double independent fair value estimates.

Buying SpaceX is, essentially, buying a ticket to ride along with whatever Elon wants to do next. Make of that what you will.
