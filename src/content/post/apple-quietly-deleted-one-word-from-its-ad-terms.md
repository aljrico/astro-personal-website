On July 28, Apple updated its Advertising Services Terms of Service for the first time since February 2024. Almost nobody reads those things, and that's a shame, because buried in Section 6(a) is one of the clearest signals yet about where Apple's next growth engine is headed.

The old contract said Apple could serve your ads on "Apple software applications or Apple devices." The new one says "devices, operating systems, software or web applications, or other platforms or properties." Spot the difference? The word **Apple** is gone. Contractually, Apple Ads is no longer confined to surfaces Apple owns.

Now, a lawyer will tell you a terms update proves nothing. Fair. But Apple has a track record here. In April 2025 it renamed Apple Search Ads to just "Apple Ads", and roughly a year later, Maps ads were announced. Finally, Apple is trying to seriously compete with Google on the Maps business. That's kind of how the company operates. They do the legal paperwork first, quietly, and the product changes come later. If the pattern holds, we should take this seriously as groundwork for a third-party ad network: Apple demand flowing into inventory it doesn't own.

I've been tracking this for a while. Back in October 2022 I wrote a piece asking [whether Apple would start showing ads on iPhones](https://www.etoro.com/posts/333be580-4b00-11ed-8080-800064873804). Four years later, the answer is turning out to be much bigger than the iPhone.

## Why it matters

Financially, this is super relevant because Apple has been pushing Services as the main growth engine behind the company. And advertising is one of the few levers inside Services with genuine hypergrowth potential. External estimates put Apple's ad business somewhere around $10B a year. A rounding error next to the iPhone, but growing fast and at software-like margins. Search ads in the App Store are arguably the highest-margin business Apple operates: an auction on shelf space it already controls, with near-zero marginal cost.

Fate, it seems, is not without a sense of irony. Back in 2021, Apple kneecapped the whole mobile ad industry with App Tracking Transparency. Meta alone said it would cost it [about $10B in revenue in 2022](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/02/02/facebook-says-apple-ios-privacy-change-will-cost-10-billion-this-year.html), and its stock fell 26% the next day, wiping more than $200B off its market cap. Every third-party network got hobbled along the way. Apple now sits on 2.5B+ active devices, 850M weekly App Store users, and the only first-party data pipe that ATT didn't touch: its own. If Apple extends that demand off-platform, it enters a market Google and Meta have proven is worth hundreds of billions a year, while holding a privileged position on the very devices where everyone else's measurement got degraded.

## The risks

Such a move would obviously attract regulatory heat Apple doesn't need, and the EU is already circling. From a PR point of view, I doubt "Apple, ad company" will sit comfortably next to all the marketing Apple has done around "Apple, privacy champion". An important brand tension is brewing.

## What to watch

If you care about Services growth, watch the ads line. This is a ~$10B business with a credible path toward Meta-scale economics, hiding in plain sight inside a $4T company. It's exactly the kind of optionality the market tends to underprice until it shows up in a keynote.

Apple just told us, in the fine print, that it's thinking bigger than the App Store. I would take them at their word.
