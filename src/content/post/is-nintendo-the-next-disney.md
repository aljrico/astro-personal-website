Nintendo has always depended heavily on console and game sales. It's lucrative, but it's also highly cyclical, which makes performance hard to predict. One day the company is swimming in money, and the next the word "bankruptcy" starts floating around.

Microsoft had a similar problem with Windows releases. Windows XP was great and the future looked bright, then Windows Vista came knocking and Microsoft started sinking. Microsoft eventually escaped that cycle by moving to subscriptions.

Now look at Nintendo's recent console cycles. The Wii was a massive success, so the company looked unbeatable. Then the Wii U flopped, and everyone started jumping off what looked like a sinking Titanic. But hey! The Switch gets announced, it's a huge success again, and investors pile back in full of optimism.

So the stock jumps up and down, riding the waves of investors' emotional journey through each cycle. You could say those investors are too short-sighted, and I'd agree. But who can blame them? Nobody knows how the next console will do. What if it's another flop? Too many failed launches and the company might never recover. That's a genuine risk, not just investors lacking perspective.

## A company that actually saves

Every big-company CEO prides themselves on being a good money manager. They know they should save during the good years to future-proof the next cycle. But they rarely do. When the cycle is up, they don't save enough, so when the tide goes out, debt creeps up, dividends get cut and a new management team gets brought in.

Nintendo is different. It really is. When the Wii and the Switch were at peak profit, management paid down debt and saved cash. Look at Nintendo's balance sheet: about twice as much cash as total liabilities, and barely any debt.

They're so conservative that the main criticism they get is that they should spend more and put that money to use. Other companies burn through cash, too proud to admit harder times will come. Nintendo is the total opposite. It sits on a huge pile of money, and management has been so afraid of downturns that it has done almost nothing with it.

Well, not anymore. Management is finally starting to put that cash to work. It has announced new buybacks and dividend increases. They're mild and shy, but it's a start.

## Following Disney's path

What's really interesting is how Nintendo is opening up to new opportunities. The plan is to build revenue streams that don't depend on the console cycle and keep cash coming in between generations.

And there's an obvious example for that: Disney.

Disney was in a similar position a few decades ago. It had a massive brand and hugely popular characters, but its revenue depended far too much on new releases, in its case movies and TV shows. Some were huge hits. Others were flops that pushed it close to the edge. We all know how much it has grown since.

Nintendo is clearly following the same steps. How?

- **Theme parks.** Like Disney World, Nintendo has partnered with Universal to build "Super Nintendo World" areas in Universal's parks. Nintendo provides the characters and takes a cut, without having to build and run the parks itself.
- **Movies.** Nintendo has massively under-used its characters in film and TV. Disney hasn't: think of the Marvel films, the Star Wars reboot and all the shows spun out of them, to the point that Disney is now challenging Netflix itself. Nintendo has now teamed up with Illumination, the studio behind *Despicable Me*, on an animated Super Mario Bros. movie.
- **Memberships.** Nintendo's paid online membership for the Switch looks like just another add-on today. But it could become much more than perks in a few games: a home for all this extra content, movies and shows included.

Just imagine. Nintendo is putting the pieces in place for a platform that blends games, shows, movies and commerce.

Nintendo isn't only a console maker anymore. If the plan works, it has the potential to become the next Disney. And if anyone can pull it off, it's Nintendo. It has the money to do it, and characters fans love.

In my humble opinion, the bears are too short-sighted to see the potential here.

## Looking back from 2026

This one aged well. Super Nintendo World opened in Japan in 2021, in Hollywood in 2023 and in Orlando in 2025. *The Super Mario Bros. Movie* came out in April 2023 and made more than $1.3 billion at the box office, and a sequel followed. The console cycle didn't go away, but Nintendo now has a lot more than consoles between one generation and the next.
