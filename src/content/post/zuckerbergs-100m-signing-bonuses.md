Meta is poaching talent and throwing real money at it. According to Sam Altman, Meta has been offering individual OpenAI researchers signing bonuses of $100M. Just for signing a contract. The race to capture the brightest minds in AI is on.

This is not a drill, and it's also not a bubble. Many people are pointing at this as proof that the AI race is a tulip-style mania. They're so, so wrong. The top researchers at OpenAI, DeepSeek, Anthropic, DeepMind and Meta's AI lab are chasing a very specific and very tangible objective: AGI. I'm not saying it's a good or desirable objective for humanity. I'm saying it's not snake oil.

## The Manhattan Project

Think of it as the Manhattan Project. The brightest minds of the 1940s were thrown into a desperate race to build the atomic bomb. At the very least, so the enemy wouldn't get it first. Not because the bomb was good for humanity, but because whichever nation got it first would have enormous leverage over everyone else.

Something similar is happening here. Don't get me wrong, there is a huge bubble in the AI space. But this news isn't about your typical "AI startup" that wraps an off-the-shelf model in a pretty chatbot to sucker customers and VCs. Those "disruptors" will fade the moment the hype deflates a little. No argument there.

The race at the top is different. It's a small circle where everyone knows everyone, and it really does feel like the world of physics during WWII. Back then, it was the theory of relativity. Now it's "Attention Is All You Need", the 2017 paper that completely changed machine learning. We went from recurrent and convolutional neural networks to Transformers. Hammer the word "Transformers" into your brain if you're even mildly interested in AI. That paper made possible all the progress we've seen since, from OpenAI's first GPT model a year later to ChatGPT in 2022.

And the gates of hell swung open. Now everyone knows the theory. Everyone involved understands what's at stake. Every big tech company's survival depends on NOT being left behind. If you believe AGI is a real possibility, whoever gets there first will steer the course of history. I'm not being hyperbolic. In the 1940s, whoever built the first bomb would decide how WWII ended and what the world looked like afterwards. The same thing is happening here, especially for big tech. What do you think happens to Google if Microsoft gets to AGI first? Or to Amazon, Meta or even Apple if they're not well positioned for that scenario?

## Do the maths

Now back to the $100M signing bonus. That's the equivalent of hiring Teller, Oppenheimer or Heisenberg. Say you run a tech company with a $10B budget to chase AGI. The pool of top talent isn't big. We're talking 50, maybe 100 people. Divide the numbers and you see where $100M comes from. It would be irresponsible NOT to do it in their position.

Do I get a bit jealous seeing some 28-year-old get a "job offer" bigger than the net worth I'll build in my whole life? A little, but not really. I'm not jealous of Messi's salary either, even though I remember being quite decent at football in high school. These are the top 0.1% of brains on the planet. People who were getting seven-figure offers during their PhDs, long before AI was a buzzword.

Is this good news for humanity? I honestly don't know. Probably not. But what Zuckerberg is doing makes perfect economic sense. He's doing what's best for Meta right now.
