What does Mark Zuckerberg want? What was Facebook built for? Some people say it's just about money. Everything is about money. Others say it's power: Facebook has been used to manipulate mass trends and opinions, which can be turned into real political power.

Plenty of debates have explored the ethics of what Facebook does. Some analysts go as far as calling it a "sin stock", a company you shouldn't own if you want an ethical portfolio. But I wonder: is Facebook truly an evil company?

I think the answer is complicated. Labelling things as simply good or bad is almost always too simplistic, however obvious it looks on the surface.

## Facebook connects people

What does Facebook actually do? Essentially, it connects people.

It's that simple. That's its purpose and its main goal. Every decision it takes, every feature it ships, every penny it puts into R&D aims at the same thing: connecting more people, more.

I can already hear the replies saying Facebook's goal is to make money, full stop. That's kind of true, but it misses the point. Facebook does want to make money, but it makes it by connecting people. It earns more when more people are connected, and it spends that money connecting even more people. It all revolves around the same idea.

## Is that a good thing?

It depends. People can be lovable, helpful, sympathetic and caring. They can also be harmful, hateful, deceitful and nihilistic. Connecting people magnifies all of it.

Connecting bullies with vulnerable people can literally cost lives. Making it easier to spread hateful propaganda can fuel hatred, racism and even terrorism.

But connecting people can also keep long, healthy relationships alive. People find love, friends and common causes. A connection might even save the life of someone on the brink of suicide.

How you see more connection depends on how you see human nature. If people are basically bad, we'd better stop them from coordinating with each other. If you believe people are good deep down, connecting them is the most powerful thing you can do for a better world.

And Facebook happens to believe the latter. It believes so deeply in the power of connecting people that anything that helps counts as good. Every questionable thing it does is worth it, because it serves the ultimate objective. And that objective is baked into everything: all the subtle design that keeps you findable by friends, the questionable contact-importing practices, the aggressive growth tactics.

## Why growth is everything

Let's not be naive. The natural state of the world is not unified. Left alone, we scatter like mushrooms in a forest. We're split by languages, cultures, religions, borders and, especially nowadays, by the products we use.

You can sometimes tell more about someone's personality from their phone brand or favourite social network than from their religion. Twitter users are wildly different from Instagram users, or Reddit users.

Nothing makes Facebook or Twitter as valuable as the people who use them. No feature is as impactful as a massive, ever-growing network of connected users.

That's why growth is so imperative, even when it's aggressive and ruthless. Merciless growth is what made Uber and Shopify valuable. Relying on product quality alone while being shy about growth is what killed Google+.

Nobody uses a social network unless lots of other people use it. Facebook knows this very well. Google took too long to realise it. It's all that matters, and all of Facebook's efforts aim at it, even the ones that look clueless or unprofitable. Messenger, photo tagging, news feed algorithms. Every one of them is measured by its own metrics. But all those metrics sit under the umbrella of one bigger purpose: connecting more people, even more.
